630.01 POLICY AND TITLE.

It is the intent and purpose of this Ordinance to prevent the build-up and spread of
injurious plant and tree pests from tree to tree and orchard to orchard; particularly from
areas not properly treated, or from areas untreated or abandoned, to commercial or semi-
commercial trees; to offer alternative control measures; to encourage property owners to
implement control measures; and to recoup expenses incurred by Jackson County in
treatment of pests due to noncooperation or nonaction of property owners, or any tenant,
occupant, lessee or person in possession of subject property. This Ordinance shall be
known as the "Jackson County Pest Control Ordinance."

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02.; P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.02 DEFINITIONS.

As used in this Ordinance:

(a) Board of Commissioners or Board - means the Jackson County Board of
Commissioners.

(b) Disease Organisms - means those disease organisms as identified and defined by the
Oregon Department of Agriculture or OSU Extension and includes, but is not limited to,
the following:  Pseudomonas blight (Pseudomonas sp.), Coryneum blight (Wilsonomyces
carpophilus), Fire blight (Erwinia amylovora), and Pear scab (Venturia Pirina Aderh).

(c) Host Plant or Host Tree - means Pear (Pyrus sp.), Apple (Malus sp.), Quince
(Cydonia sp.), Peach (Prunus sp.), and may include other rosaceous hosts of Apple
Maggot as deemed necessary by the Oregon Department of Agriculture or OSU
Extension.

(d) Insect Pest - means those insect pests as identified and defined by the Oregon
Department of Agriculture or OSU Extension and includes, but is not limited to, Codling
Moth (Cydia Pomonella), Oriental Fruit Moth (Grapholita Molesta), Pear Psylla
(Cacopsylla Pyricola), San Jose Scale (Quadraspidiotus Perniciosus), Shot Hole Borer
(Scolytus Rugulosus), Apple Maggot (Rhagoletis Pomonella) and Peach Twig Borer
(Anarsia Lineatella).

(e) Integrated Pest Management (IPM) - is a sustainable approach to managing pests by
combining biological, cultural, mechanical, and chemical tools in a way that minimizes
economic, health, and environmental risks.

(f) Organic - means the techniques and process of raising and producing agriculture
without synthetic fertilizers, pesticides or drugs.

(g) Owner - means any actual title holder of land on which an affected orchard is
located.  It also refers to those tenants, occupiers, agents of the owner and any other
persons who are responsible for management of the orchard.

(h) Orchard - means any property containing 50 host plants or host trees as defined by
this ordinance, or any property containing a lesser number that poses a significant threat
to a commercial orchard as determined by the Code Enforcement Officer.

(i) Trap - refers to a Pheron Apple Maggot trap or other brand as recommended by
Oregon Department of Agriculture as set by this department, an Oregon State University
(OSU) Extension agent or a representative of Jackson County.  “Trapped” means an
insect pest caught in a trap.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.03 PROHIBITIONS.

It is a county violation for any owner or possessor of property within the control area to
fail to control disease organisms or insect pests on host plants or host trees on such
property as set forth in this Ordinance.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.04 CONTROL AREA.

A control area is hereby created for the control of insect pests and diseases related to, but
not limited to, Pseudomonas Blight, Coryneum Blight, Peach Leaf Curl, Fire Blight,
Apple Scab, Pear Scab, Codling Moth, Oriental Fruit Moth, Pear Psylla, San Jose Scale,
Shot Hole Borer, Apple Maggot, Peach Twig Borer, and mites, within the boundaries of
Jackson County, Oregon.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.05 CONTROL METHODS.

(a) Integrated Pest Management (IPM). Shall guide the evaluation and implementation
of pest control methods.  Orchards containing insect pests and disease organisms on host
plants and host trees within Jackson County shall be controlled by one, or a combination,
of the following methods:

(1) Biological Control:

A. Release and/or rely on natural enemies and beneficial organisms.
B. Create a situation favorable to beneficial organisms.

(2) Cultural Control:

A. Cover crops
B. Cultivation, tillage, moving, and water management
C. Trapping
D. Destruction of pest habitat
E. Proper sanitation (i.e. burning, shredding, and fruit removal and
destruction)

(3) Chemical Control:

A. Insect pheromones
B. Biologically produced toxins
C. Synthetic and natural pesticides

(4) Mechanical Control:

A. Sticky bands
B. Burlap bands
C. Removal of host plants or trees

(5) Genetic Control:

A. Pest resistant plant varieties

(b) Other Control Methods:  The Enforcement Officer may authorize other methods of
control as deemed appropriate by qualified experts such as the Oregon Department of
Agriculture or Oregon State University agents.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.06 ENFORCEMENT OFFICER RIGHT OF ACCESS.

(a) The buildup and spread of injurious plant and tree pests as defined in 630.02 is
hereby deemed an emergency and upon reasonable cause to believe such buildup and
spread of pests exists as advised by OSU Extension and/or the Department of
Agriculture, the Code Enforcement Officer, or designee, is authorized to inspect any
property within the control area at reasonable times and in a lawful manner.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.07 ENFORCEMENT PROCESS.

(a) OSU Extension and/or the Department of Agriculture will advise the Jackson
County Code Enforcement Officer on problem identification, the basis upon which the
problem poses a threat to other orchards, and control recommendations.  Upon the
Enforcement Officer’s referral to the Jackson County Development Services Director, the
Director will determine when to commence proceedings to cause the abatement of
nuisance.

630.08 CONCURRENT JURISDICTION.

The Jackson County Hearings Officer, the justice court of this county, and the circuit
court of this State shall have concurrent jurisdiction for all violations of this ordinance.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.09 EXEMPTION.

Accepted scientific experiments conducted by Oregon State University or its designee on
test plots for the benefit of the fruit industry are exempt from this ordinance.

(E. Ord. 2002-17. Passed 8-7-02. P. Ord. 2002-21. Passed 10-30-02.)

630.99 ENFORCEMENT AND REMEDIES.

(a) Code Enforcement.  Enforcement of the Jackson County Pest Control Ordinance
shall comply with Chapter 203 of the Jackson County Code.

(b) Penalty and Equitable Remedies:

(1) Violation of any provision under this Ordinance, unless otherwise provided, is
subject to penalties set forth under JCC 202.99.

(2) In addition to the penalty provided in Chapter 202.99, violation of the Jackson
County Pest Control Ordinance is considered a public nuisance which is
subject to abatement procedures set forth under Sections 1864.10 through
1864.13 of the Jackson County Code, said sections to be interpreted to apply in
all respects to the Jackson County Pest Control Ordinance.

A. If abatement is ordered by the Jackson County Hearings Officer or Circuit
Court, as the case may be, the County may enter onto the subject premises
to abate the nuisance by treating or removing the host plants or host trees
or their produce.

B. The method of control will be according to methods described in Section
630.05 above or whatever method deemed to be the safest, most effective,
and most economical.
