Yamhill County Community Corrections

SAFETY RULES FOR CHILDREN

Because sex offenders get their power through secrecy, the single most effective means
of protecting your child is to communicate with them. Open communication between
parents and children are vital components of family safety. Talk to your children. They
have to feel comfortable discussing sensitive matters with you, and then they are more
likely to tell you when a sex offender has put them in an uncomfortable situation. Be
aware of the common lures and don’t accept a ride from a stranger.

If someone follows you on foot or in a car, stay away from him or her. You DO NOT
need to go near a car to talk to people inside.

Grown-ups who need help should not be asking children for help; they should be
asking other grown-ups.

No one should be asking you for directions or to look for a lost puppy or telling you
that your mother or father is in trouble and that he will take you to them.

If someone tries to take you somewhere, quickly get away from him or her and yell
or scream. “This man is trying to take me away!” or, “This person is not my mother
(or father).”

It is safer to be with other people when going places or playing outside, so always
use the BUDDY SYSTEM.

If someone tries to touch you in ways that make you feel uncomfortable or
frightened, tell them “NO” and go tell a grown-up you trust what happened.

Know it is NOT your fault if someone touches you in a way that is NOT okay. You
do not have to keep it secret.

The information gained through this notification should assist children and their families
in avoiding situations that allow sex offenders easy access to victims. Do not harass your
neighbor; an offender put in a stressful state is more likely to reoffend.
