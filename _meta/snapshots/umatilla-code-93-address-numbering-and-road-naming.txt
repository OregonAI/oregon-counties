GENERAL PROVISIONS
§ 93.01  TITLE.
This chapter shall be known as the “Umatilla
County Rural Addressing System.”
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92)
§ 93.02  AUTHORITY.
ORS Chapter 215 and Chapter 92 empowers the
County Board of Commissioners, hereinafter, the
Board, to define through an ordinance the procedures
and requirements relating to the addressing of dwellings
in the unincorporated areas of the county; and to
provide a uniform road naming system for the county.
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92)
§ 93.03  PURPOSE.
(A) To establish and enforce a permanent,
systematic and county-wide rural address and road
naming system.  Such a system is necessary for the
protection of health, safety and welfare of the residents
of the county.  A systematic addressing plan will
facilitate the location by emergency services and others,
of dwellings, businesses, and other buildings of interest
and importance by a site address and identifiable road
name.  The provisions of this chapter will serve to
eliminate the problems and confusion associated with
the continually changing rural route and box system.
(B) Naming or renaming private lanes or public
roads not currently in the County Road Department's
maintenance system, shall not mean the county accepts
or will maintain these roads or lanes.
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92)
§ 93.04  AREAS EXEMPT FROM PROVISIONS.
This chapter does not pertain to the following areas
within the county:  inside incorporated city limits and
their associated urban growth boundaries, or other areas
deemed necessary and appropriate by the Board.
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92)

§ 93.15  PURPOSE AND INTENT.
The procedures set forth herein are for the purpose
of establishing a uniform policy in the county for the
naming and renaming of existing and future roads.  It is
intended that this policy will clarify and set the
requirements and responsibilities of individuals, public
bodies, and departments involved in the naming and
renaming of roads.  In addition, it provides the public
with an outline of the required process for road naming
and renaming.
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92)

§ 93.16  ROAD NAMING.

Planning Department, subject to the approval of the

(B) The following situations will require approval

ADDRESS SYSTEM
§ 93.40  METHOD OF ADDRESSING.
(A) The county address system is established by
this chapter as the offici al address system for the
unincorporated portions of the county.  All qualifying
areas of the county shall be renumbered in such a
manner that the address of a residence, business, or
other building requiring an address, will be a five digit
number taken from the Oregon State Coordinate System
with appropriate alterations to meet local needs for a
uniform numbering system.
(B) Existing homes, buildings, and businesses
shall be numbered on a postal delivery area basis.  New
addresses issued under the provision of this chapter
shall not be assigned on an individual or piecemeal
basis outside the postal delivery area being
comprehensively readdressed.
(C) Buildings accessed fro m roads predominantly
extending north-south will have a five digit number
derived from the north-south axis, or y-axis, of the State
Coordinate System.  The last digit from the SCS y-axis
numbers shall be disregarded.  Numbers for buildings
essentially west of a road shall end in an even number
and numbers for buildings east of a road shall end in an
odd number.
(D) Buildings accessed from roads predominantly
extending east-west will have a five digit number
derived from the east-west axis, or x-axis of the State
9-45
Coordinate System.  The first and last digit from the
SCS x-axis numbers shall be disregarded.  Numbers for
buildings essentially south of a road shall end in an
even number and numbers for buildings north of a road
shall end in an odd number.
(E) Address numbers are estimated from an
overlay of the State Coordinate System onto field
sheets based on the intersection location of the building
access road and named road, lane, drive, loop, or
highway.  The State Plane Coordinate System is used
to determine a point of reference on a road.  All
buildings along the road are then assigned addresses
based on the distance (following the road) from this
reference point to a driveway or entrance.
(F) Apartment houses, mobile home parks, and
other multi-unit dwellings shall be addressed with one
main address.  Individual units within these complexes
shall be assigned an address identifier in a uniform and
understandable manner.  Interior addresses shall be
jointly developed by both the management/owner(s)
and Planning Department.  Final approval shall be
acceptable to the Planning Department.
(G) If deemed appropriate, business complexes
where there are several businesses within the same
building shall have individual addresses for each
business.  Businesses associated with dwellings shall
have individual addresses if conducted from a separate
building.
(Ord. 92-03, passed 5-20-92; Ord. 92-03A, passed
4-19-95; Ord. 2017-07, passed 5-22-2017)

SCENIC-HISTORIC ROADS
§ 93.60  PURPOSE.
The purpose of this subchapter is to create a
“Scenic-Historic Road” system in the county in order to
direct and expose residents and tourists to the scenery
9-47
and history of the county in fulfillment of needs for
educations, recreation, and tourism.
(Ord. 87-5, passed 5-28-87)
§ 93.61  CRITERIA.
The following general criteria are hereby
established to assist in determining the eligibility of a
particular road or road segment as a “Scenic-Historic
Road:”
(A) The road shall pass through or by, or lead to,
scenic area and/or historical sites of local, state, or
national interest.
(B) The roadway shall be improved gravel or
pavement, to insure relative ease of travel. The road
need not be high speed, and indeed a winding “country
road” is preferable.
(C) The road shall be either a through road or
shall have a convenient turn-around at the end. The
latter road type is acceptable in recognition of the many
scenic and historic routes leading up canyons in the
foothills of the Blue Mountains.
(D) The road shall be a dedicated county road, or
also a city street, state or federal road or highway, if a
city, the state, or the Umatilla National Forest agrees to
participate in the Scenic-Historic Road designation and
marking program.
(Ord. 87-5, passed 5-28-87)
§ 93.62  ESTABLISHING A SCENIC-HISTORIC
ROAD.
The following procedures shall be used in
establishing a “Scenic-Historic Road:”
(A) Nomination.  A road or road segment may be
nominated as a “Scenic-Historic Road” by any of the
following methods:  petition by citizens, written request
by a city or citizen's group, or at the initiative of the
county itself. Nominations are to be directed to the
Board of Commissioners and shall contain the
following information:  a map of the proposed route, a
brief description of the historic and scenic attributes of
the route, and the reason for the nomination.
(B) Review of a nomination.  Once a nomination
has been received at the County Courthouse, the
following steps shall be taken to review a nomination:
(1) The Board of Commissioners will
forward copies of a nomination request to the County
Road Department and County Planning Department
within one week.
(2) The County Road Department and
County Planning Department will then prepare a report
on the road or road segment proposed for designation
and report back to the Board of Commissioners within
one month. Their report shall include the following: an
accurate map of the proposed route, a description of the
scenery to be observed along the route, an identification
of the historic sites to be found along the route, an
inventory of the roadway conditions, a set of color
slides illustrating the scenic and historic sites along the
route, a discussion of the value of the particular route as
a “Scenic-Historic Road,” a discussion of any problems
that might arise with regard to official designation, and
a recommendation in favor or against designation.
(3) The Board of Commissioners will
schedule and advertise a public hearing and informa-
tional meeting regarding the proposed “Scenic-Historic
Road,” utilizing the standards and procedures for a
public hearing as set forth in the County Development
Code (Chapter 152). In addition, a pres release
describing the proposed “Scenic-Historic Road” and the
county departments' recommendations will be prepared
and sent out two weeks in advance of the hearing to the
press, and to all affected cities, state and local agencies,
citizen's groups, and key property owners. Notices will
also be posted at intervals not to exceed two miles along
the proposed route, at least two weeks in advance of the
hearing.
(4) At or following the public hearing, the
Board of Commissioners will decide whether or not to
designate the proposed route as a “Umatilla County
Scenic-Historic Road.” Written notice of their decision
will be provided to all interested parties. The official
designation shall be in the form of a resolution.
(Ord. 87-5, passed 5-28-87)

SCENIC-HISTORIC ROADS.
9-48
(A) Marking and maintenance of scenic-historic
roads shall be a public/private partnership between the
County Road Department, cooperating citizen's groups,
and a city, the state, or the Umatilla National Forest.
(B) The County Road Department will continue
maintenance responsibility on dedicated county roads
only, and the degree of maintenance shall only be that
which is normally given the road or that which is
necessary to maintain easy travel during the spring,
summer, and fall months, if so authorized by the Board
of Commissioners.
(C) A city, the State Highway Division, or the
Umatilla National Forest shall be solely responsible for
maintaining those portions of a scenic-historic road that
are city streets or state or federal roads and highways,
if these jurisdictions agree to inclusion of their facilities
as a scenic-historic road.
(D) Marking of scenic-historic roads on dedicated
county roads shall be the financial responsibility of
citizen's groups, with the cooperation and assistance of
the County Road and Planning Departments, as
necessary. Marking shall include both signage and
advertising in the form of brochures, maps, etc. All
signage shall be of a standardized agreed to by the
County Road Department, and as necessary, the State
Highway Division.
(E) Signage shall include the following:
(1) Identification signs along each scenic-
historic road.
(2) Directional signs leading to each scenic-
historic road, from nearby cities, interstate freeways,
state highways, and major county roads.
(3) Identification signs for historic sites
along each scenic-historic road.
(4) Interpretive as needed and available.
(Ord. 87-5, passed 5-28-87)
