ORDINANCE NO. 1332

Amending Multnomah County’s Zoning Code to Incorporate Amendments to the City of
Portland’s Zoning Code (Title 33) Allowing Adjustments as Part of the Housing
Adjustment Compliance Project and Making Odor Code Updates; Amendments to the
Portland Sign Code (Title 32) Increasing Flexibility in the Rose Quarter Sign Code; and
Amendments to the Portland Tree Code (Title 11) Permanently Adopting the Large Tre e
Regulations; and Declaring an Emergency.

The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:

A. Pursuant to an intergovernmental agreement executed in 2002 (the “IGA”), the City
of Portland, Oregon (“City”), provides, with certain exceptions, land use planning
services for those areas of unincorporated Multnomah County located within the
City’s Urban Services Boundary (the “Unincorporated Urban Areas”).

B. Because the County retains legislative authority over the Unincorporated Urban
Areas, the County assumed an obligation in the IGA to amend County land use
policies and regulations as they relate to the Unincorporated Urban Areas to
incorporate applicable City land use policies and regulations, and all subsequent
amendments thereto.

C. Through Ordinance No. 191942 (adopted by the Portland City Council on October
30, 2024, effective January 1, 2025), the Housing Adjustment Compliance Project
amends the City of Portland Zoning Code, Title 33, to allow adjustments to
development and design standards that were previously prohibited for residential
projects, in compliance with the provisions of State Senate Bill 1537 (2024 Regular
Session).

D. Through Ordinance No. 191959 (adopted by the Portland City Council on
November 13, 2024, effective March 1, 2025), amendments to the current Sign
Code, Title 32, provide greater sign allowances and flexibility for signs within a
mapped designated area called the Rose Quarter Entertainment subdistrict.

E. Through Ordinance No. 191961 (adopted by the Portland City Council on
November 13, 2024, effective March 1, 2025), amendments to the Zoning Code,
Title 33, remove odor regulations and delete requirements to document
compliance with City Chapter 33.262, Off-Site Impacts, at the time of building
permit review.  Ordinance No. 191961 also amends portions of Title 29 that are
not subject to the IGA and therefore those amendments are not proposed for
adoption in this ordinance.

G. The City has requested that the County amend the County’s Zoning Code to
incorporate the changes implemented in City Ordinance numbers 191942,
191959, 191961, and 191975.

H. The Board of County Commissioners adopts the legislative intent and findings set
forth in City of Portland Ordinances 191942, 191959, 191961, and 191975 as
findings in support of this ordinance.

I. Pursuant to State and City notice requirements, as well as the terms of the IGA,
the City provided public notice of City Ordinance numbers 191942, 191959,
191961, and 191975. The City provided an opportunity for the public to be heard
at public hearings, c ulminating with hearings before the City’s Planning
Commission and the City Council.

Section 2. In accordance with ORS 215.427(3), the changes resulting from
Section 1 of this ordinance shall not apply to any decision on an application that is
submitted before the applicable effective date of this ordinance and that is made complete
prior to the applicable effective date of this ordinance or within 180 days of the initial
submission of the application.
Section 3. In accordance with ORS 92.040(2), for any subdivisions for which
the initial application is submitted before the applicable effective date of this ordinance,
the subdivision application and any subsequent application for construction shall be
governed by t he County’s land use regulations in effect as of the date the subdivision
application is first submitted.
Section 4. Any future amendments to the legislative matters listed in Section 1
above are exempt from the requirements of MCC 39.1210. The Board acknowledges,
authorizes and agrees that the Portland Planning Commission will act instead of the
Multnomah County Plannin g Commission for the Unincorporated Urban Areas by
employing the City's own legislative procedures, including providing notice to, and
facilitating participation from, property owners within Unincorporated Urban Areas. The
Board will consider the recommendations of the Portland Planning Commission and City
Council when legislative land use matters for the Unincorporated Urban Areas come
before the Board for action.
