WHEREAS, the Gilliam County Court has authority under Oregon R evised Statute  (ORS)
Chapter 215 and the Gilliam  County Zoning and Land Development Ordinance ( GCZLDO) to
adopt, amend, or decline to amend the text of the GCZLDO, provided that any such decision is
supported by findings addressing the applicable approval criteria of GCZLDO Article 10 and the
applicable statewide planning goals;
WHEREAS, the Gilliam County Planning Department received an application from a landowner
for a text amendment to the Rural Unincorporated Community (R -C) Zone to allow Marijuana
Retail as a use allowed conditionally in the R-C Zone. The Gilliam County Planning Commission
held a duly noticed public hearing on April 28, 2026, and continued the hearing to May 26, 2026,
at which oral and written testimony was received both in favor of and in opposition to the request.
At the conclusion of the hearing, the Planning Commi ssion recommended that the County Court
approve the request with modifications, including that the text amendment is limited to only apply
to the Rural Unincorporated Community of Mayville;
WHEREAS, the Gilliam County Court held a public hearing on the matter on June 17, 2026, and
continued the matter to July 1, 2026, for deliberations only;
WHEREAS, the Gilliam County Court received oral and written testimony both in favor of and
in opposition to conditionally allowing marijuana retail in the unincorporated community of
Mayville at the June 17, 2026 public hearing, and, following deliberations on July 1, 2026, reached
a decision, by a vote of 3 in favor and 0 opposed, to deny the application and directed planning
staff to prepare written findings in support of that decision;
WHEREAS, the Gilliam County Court has reviewed and hereby adopts the Findings of Fact and
Conclusions of Law attached hereto as Exhibit A and incorporated herein by this reference, which
findings address the approval criteria set forth in GCZLDO Section 10.050, the  Gilliam County
Comprehensive Plan, and the Transportation Planning Rule (OAR 660- 012-0060), and which
findings support the Court's conclusion that the requested text amendment does not adequately
satisfy those criteria;
WHEREAS, the Court further finds, consistent with Exhibit A, that Marijuana Retail as a
conditionally allowed use is more appropriately located within the County's General Industrial and
Limited Industrial zones, and this finding is offered as guidance only and doe s not constitute
approval, pre-approval, or any binding commitment regarding any future application for a text or
map amendment affecting those zones;
NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that Land Use File No. AZ -001-26,
requesting a text amendment to the Gilliam County Zoning and Land Development Ordinance to
allow Marijuana Retail as a use allowed conditionally in the Rural Unincorporated Community
(R-C) Zone, is DENIED, by a vote of 3 i n favor and 0 opposed, for the reasons set forth in the
Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law attached as Exhibit A and incorporated herein.
NOTICE: this Order constitutes a final land use decision of Gilliam County. Any person with
standing to appeal under ORS 197.830 may seek review of this decision by filing a notice of intent
to appeal with the Land Use Board of Appeals (LUBA) within twenty- one (21) days of the date
this Order is mailed or otherwise issued, as required by ORS 197.830(9).

GENERAL INFORMATION
The current GCZLDO allows marijuana production and processing in the Rural Unincorporated
Community (R-C) Zone subject to standards found in Article 8. Section 8.270 defines Marijuana
Businesses and identifies standards for marijuana businesses, including the zones and procedures
for which such businesses are allowed. The Applicant requested that marijuana retailing be
allowed conditionally in the R-C Zone and that wholesaling of marijuana products grown off-site
be changed from a prohibited to a permitted use under Table 1 of Section 8.270. The desired
outcome of the Applicant's requested changes, if adopted, would have been to enable the Applicant
to subsequently apply for a Conditional Use Permit to authorize a retail marijuana facility.
The Gilliam County Planning Commission recommended that the Applicant's request be modified
to conditionally allow marijuana retailing only within the Mayville area of the R-C Zone, with no
change to the existing prohibition on wholesaling. The Gilliam County Court (“Court”) considered
the Planning Commission's recommendation and, for the reasons set forth below, denied the
application in its entirety, including both the retailing and wholesaling components of the request.
PROCEDURE
This amendment was processed in accordance with GCZLDO Section 10.020, which requires a
hearing before the Planning Commission and a recommendation to the County Court, followed by
a public hearing and final decision before the County Court.
APPROVAL CRITERIA
The request was filed under GCZLDO Chapter 10 — Amendments. The applicable criteria are
identified below in bold, with the Court's findings in regular type. Evaluation of the Gilliam County
Comprehensive Plan follows the Chapter 10 criteria.
GCZLDO ARTICLE 10. AMENDMENTS
SECTION 10.010 - AUTHORIZATION TO INITIATE AMENDMENTS
An amendment to the text of this ordinance or to a zoning map may be initiated by the County
Court, the County Planning Commission, or by application of a property owner. The request by a
property owner for an amendment shall be accomplished by filing an application with the Planning
Department, using forms prescribed pursuant to Section 11.130.
This request is an application from a property owner meeting this requirement.
SECTION 10.020 - PUBLIC HEARINGS ON AMENDMENTS
The Planning Commission shall conduct a public hearing on the proposed amendment at its
earliest practicable meeting after it is proposed and shall, within 40 days after the hearing,
recommend to the County Court approval, disapproval, or modification of the proposed
amendment. After receiving the recommendation of the Planning Commission, the County Court
shall hold a public hearing on the proposed amendment.
As required by this section, a public hearing was held by the Planning Commission on April 28,
2026, and continued to May 26, 2026. During the initial hearing, the Commission heard testimony
from the public. Testimony in favor included the Applicant and one other resident of Mayville,
citing economic benefits. One member of the public offered neutral oral testimony. Three members
of the community spoke in opposition, addressing anticipated impacts from increased traffic and
public safety concerns. At the conclusion of its deliberations, the Planning Commission
unanimously recommended retaining the existing GCZLDO definitions for marijuana and
cannabis, and, by a vote of 3 in favor, 1 opposed, and 1 absent, recommended that the County
Court adopt changes allowing retail marijuana as a conditional use limited to the Rural
Unincorporated Community of Mayville only, with no further changes to the application as
submitted.
The County Court held a public hearing on the proposed text amendment on June 17, 2026, and
continued the hearing to July 1, 2026, for deliberations only. No new evidence was accepted after
the close of the evidentiary record on June 17, 2026. Having considered the full record, including
the Planning Staff Report and Findings, the Planning Commission's recommendation, and all oral
and written testimony, the County Court, by a vote of 3 to 0, denied the request in its entirety, for
the reasons set forth below.
SECTION 10.030 - RECORD OF AMENDMENTS
The County Clerk shall maintain records of amendments to the text and zoning map of the
ordinance.
This Order and these Findings will be provided to the County Clerk to maintain the record as
required by this section.
SECTION 10.040 - TIME LIMIT ON REAPPLICATION
No application of a property owner for an amendment to the text of this ordinance or to the zoning
map shall be considered by the Planning Commission within the one -year period immediately
following a previous denial of such request, except the Planning Commission, new evidence or a
change of circumstances warrant it.
This request is denied. Accordingly, another request for the same or substantially similar
amendment may not be considered within the one-year period following denial of this application,
except as provided in this section.
SECTION 10.050 - APPROVAL CRITERIA FOR AMENDMENTS
A. The applicant for an amendment must show that the proposed change conforms with the
Comprehensive Plan.
The Applicant did not specifically identify in the application how the proposed change conforms
with the Comprehensive Plan. The letter accompanying the application states that the use would
“highly enhance the County of Gilliam with income” and that resulting revenue “can be used by
the County for Public Safety, and many other areas as needed.” These statements imply that the
use would encourage economic development, generally implicating Goal 9 of the Comprehensive
Plan, addressed below.
As discussed further under Goal 9, the Court finds that general, unquantified assertions of
economic benefit are not sufficient to demonstrate conformance with a Comprehensive Plan that
directs commercial development in unincorporated areas toward specific, defined purposes. No
market analysis, needs assessment, or other substantial evidence was submitted showing that the
requested use serves those purposes. The Applicant therefore did not carry its burden of proof, and
the Court finds that the criterion in GCZLDO Section 10.050(A) is not satisfied.
B.-C. Transportation Planning Rule / Impacts to Transportation Facilities.
GCZLDO Section 10.050(B) -(C) requires that a plan or land use regulation amendment that
significantly affects a transportation facility be accompanied by measures assuring consistency
with the function, capacity, and level of service of that facility, consistent with the Transportation
Planning Rule, OAR 660-012-0060.
The Planning Staff Report found that the uses currently allowed in the R -C Zone are varied and
unlike a retail marijuana facility, and that due to the remote location of areas within the R-C Zone,
a retail use “could have an impact that may warrant evaluat ion of transportation impacts.” Staff
recommended that a trip -generation letter be adopted as an approval criterion for marijuana
retailing under Article 8. The Planning Commission's recommendation did not incorporate this
recommendation, and the record reflects no additional standard addressing transportation impacts
beyond limiting the use to Mayville. Three members of the public testified in opposition
specifically regarding increased traffic and public safety concerns associated with the proposed
use.
The Court finds that the record does not include any standard, condition, or mitigation measure
addressing the transportation impacts identified by Staff and testimony, and that the Applicant did
not submit evidence, such as a trip- generation analysis, sufficient to assure consistency with the
function, capacity, or level of service of transportation facilities serving the R -C Zone.
Accordingly, the Court finds the criterion in GCZLDO Section 10.050(B)-(C) is not satisfied.
GILLIAM COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN APPLICABLE GOALS
This application satisfies the policies identified in Goals 1 and 2, as public engagement was
encouraged throughout the hearings process, with opportunities provided at the Planning
Commission hearing in Condon and the County Court hearing in Arlington. Landowners within
the Rural Unincorporated Community Zone, and lands immediately adjacent to the zone, were
notified of the request and provided an opportunity to comment and participate in the process.
Goal 12 (Transportation) is addressed above in connection with GCZLDO Section 10.050(B)-(C).
For the reasons stated there, the Court finds the record does not demonstrate consistency with Goal
12.
Goal 8 is not directly applicable to this request; however, it is a policy of Goal 8 to promote
commercial establishments catering to recreational needs of the County in areas adjacent to major
recreation developments. The areas identified as best fulfilling this objective have been identified
as Rural Unincorporated Community Zone areas. This request neither promotes nor hinders major
recreational development. Because the R-C Zone has been specifically identified for this purpose,
and this request directly concerns that zone, this policy is considered even though its impact is
neutral. Goal 8 neither supports nor precludes approval.
Goal 9 focuses primarily on industrial and agricultural sources for the economic base of Gilliam
County. Commercial development in unincorporated areas is encouraged when appropriate to
support recreation, tourist accommodation near recreation sites, expansion of existing businesses,
or to serve the identified needs of residents. This request does not specifically relate to the
recreational needs of the County and is not an expansion of an existing business. The Applicant
asserts the use would serve the needs of residents; however, that assertion is not supported by
evidence in the record sufficient to allow the Court to gauge the need. Because the Applicant bears
the burden of demonstrating conformance, and has not submitted evidence establishing that the
proposed use serves a purpose Goal 9 identifies for commercial development in unincorporated
areas, the Court finds the application does not conform with Goal 9.
Because the application does not conform with Goal 9 and the record does not demonstrate
consistency with Goal 12, the Court finds the application does not conform with the Gilliam
County Comprehensive Plan, and the criterion in GCZLDO Section 10.050(A) is not satisfied on
this independent basis as well.

COUNTY COURT DECISION
At the conclusion of the County Court hearing on June 17, 2026, and deliberations on July 1, 2026,
the County Court's decision options included: (1) approve the Planning Commission's
recommendation as presented; (2) approve with other modifications; or (3)  deny the request
entirely. By a vote of 3 to 0, the County Court denied the application in its entirety, including both
the retailing and wholesaling components of the request, for the reasons set forth in these Findings.
In its deliberations, the Court additionally noted that marijuana retail, as a type of use, is
more consistent with the County's General Industrial and Limited Industrial zones than with the
Rural Unincorporated Community Zone, given the existing commercial and industrial
character, site access, and infrastructure found in those zones. This observation is offered as
guidance to the Applicant and Planning Staff only. It is based on the record developed in this
proceeding, including the Planning Staff Report's analysis of R-C Zone characteristics, and
does not rely on any determination made outside this record. It does not constitute approval,
conditional approval, or any binding determination regarding a future application affecting
the General Industrial or Limited Industrial zones, which would require its own independent
application, notice, hearing, and findings under GCZLDO Article 10.
