BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
ORDINANCE NO. 1285
Adopting Universal Preschool Now! Initiative Petition.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
A. Public investment in universal preschool may be our single best investment to
reduce racial, ethnic and income-based education and opportunity gaps. Children attending a
quality preschool have higher high school graduation rates and earnings in adulthood and
experience reduced rates of unemployment, substance abuse and involvement in the criminal
justice system.
B. Public investment in preschool is a powerful economic development strategy,
creating jobs and raising the skill levels of the labor force, particularly for historically
disadvantaged groups.
C. Universal preschool , free to all residents, brings us together as a community,
avoids reinforcing segregation based on economic status, creates greater support for children,
and is more effective and cost efficient than income-based programs.
D. Quality preschool  and childcare is expensive and hard to find in Multnomah
County. In 2018, the average tuition for a preschooler enrolled in full-time, center-based,
childcare was $1,077.00 per month, and $995.00 per month for home-based care.
E. The lack of affordable childcare or preschool is a serious burden on low-income
and middle-income communities. The high cost of care prevents parents (disproportionately
mothers)  from being able to work as many hours as they need to, or from working at all. As a
result, poverty rates for young children are higher than any other age group in Oregon.
F. Public preschool is currently available in Multnomah County through the
Headstart/Oregon Prekindergarten programs to children in families at or below the federal
poverty level, and through Preschool Promise to children with family incomes below 200% of the
federal poverty line. However, these programs are inadequately funded and do not fill the needs
of most families in Multnomah County. In 2017-18, these programs reached only 3,300 of
Multnomah County's nearly 20,000 three and four-year old children.
G. Universal preschool programs that make publicly-funded preschool available to
all preschool-aged children are more popular and better supported than programs only serving
low-income families, which are chronically underfunded. Headstart/Oregon PreK reach only
30% of eligible children in Multnomah County. Universal programs are more efficient and
effective in closing the opportunity gap and fueling economic development than income-based
programs. All children benefit from developmentally appropriate preschool and children from
low Income families gain more from mixed-income settings than income-targeted programs.
H. Current childcare providers, including family childcare providers and small
centers can transition to providing quality preschool for eligible children with the assistance of
professional development, mentoring and training.
I. The cost of providing universal preschool can be paid for by taxing certain
high-income residents of Multnomah County - individuals and households earning more than
$200,000 and $250,000 respectively - on income exceeding thresholds.
Multnomah County Ordains as Follows:
Section 1. Policy and Purpose
A. The policy and purpose of this Ordinance is to reduce the racial, ethnic and
income-based educational and economic opportunity gaps in Multnomah County by making
available to all children residing in Multnomah County two years of publicly-funded quality
preschool prior to kindergarten eligibility, paid for by a Multnomah County personal income tax
on individuals and households with annual gross incomes over approximately $200,000 and
$225,000, respectively.
B. The Multnomah County Universal Preschool Program ("Universal Preschool" or
"Program") created by this Ordinance shall seek to build on existing expertise and programs,
including the Preschool Promise program, to make two years of year-round publicly-funded
quality preschool available to all age-eligible children in Multnomah County by the 2027-2028
school year.
C. The first twelve (12) to eighteen (18) months after enactment of this Ordinance
will be used for initial planning, surveying and further developing infrastructure to meet needed
capacity, the establishment of support services for providers (such as shared benefits plans, a
shared substitute pool and other technical assistance), development of quality standards, and
building capacity through recruitment, training and workforce development. In addition,
revenues will be put aside from initial and ongoing tax revenue to create a reserve fund.
D. Funding for the earliest Universal Preschool providers will begin at the start of the
second school year after the enactment of this Ordinance (the 2022-2023 school year), with the
aim of funding providers that meet the needs of all children whose parents or guardians wish to
participate as soon as practicable, but not later than the 2027-2028 school year. Universal
Preschool shall be rolled out in phases, beginning in geographical areas serving low-income
communities and communities of color. All students in that geographical area will be entitled to
attend a qualified preschool program, free of charge.
E. Under this Ordinance, publicly-funded quality Universal Preschool programs are
those that:
1. Provide culturally inclusive or culturally specific year-round programs
consistent with early education best practices which emphasize child-led,
trauma informed, physically active, and developmentally appropriate
learning experiences and de-emphasize academic skill building and
testing;
2. Ensure adequate staffing, generally one adult for eight children;
3. Include parent involvement opportunities and connect families to
additional community resources and services;
4. Are staffed with workers who are paid living wages and benefits and
offered professional development; and
5. Do not charge private tuition to attend, above the amount provided
through the Universal Preschool Program, except for the cost of before or
after preschool childcare.
F. Quality Universal Preschool may be provided in schools, community centers
(publicly or privately owned), multi-generational classrooms, licensed family childcare programs
and cooperative settings that provide full-day (six hours) preschool on a 12-month basis, so long
as they meet the other requirements of this Ordinance. The goal of this mixed delivery model is
to support the establishment and retention of programs in each of these settings in order to
provide parents with a variety of options from which to choose (such as location, hours of
operation, days of operation, vacation/summer break schedules, size, language, and level of
parental involvement).
G. The Multnomah County Universal Preschool Program created by this Ordinance
shall be implemented in a manner that prioritizes job creation, training and support for
historically disadvantaged groups in Multnomah County, including women, people of color,
people with disabilities, immigrant communities and people from low-income backgrounds.
H. To ensure public accountability and community oversight, the Multnomah County
Universal Preschool Program shall be subject to financial and performance audits, and
oversight by the Multnomah County Universal Preschool Advisory Committee made up of early
education practitioners and stakeholders.
I. Revenues raised through this new tax will be deposited into a separate fund,
which shall be used to finance the Universal Preschool Program.
Section 2. Definitions
Unless otherwise defined in this section, terms that are defined in the Multnomah County Code
or the Oregon Revised Statutes shall have the meanings provided therein.
A. "Eligible children." Children who will turn 3 or 4 years of age on or before
September 1st of the enrolling year and are not yet eligible to be enrolled in kindergarten.
B. "Preschool workers." Employees of Universal Preschool providers including,
but not limited to, teachers, assistant teachers, teachers' aides, family childcare providers,
family childcare assistants I and 11, substitute teachers, coaches, administrators, and
administrative staff.
C. "Universal Preschool." Quality preschool programs that are offered free of
charge to children in Multnomah County (not based on income eligibility) by providers that meet

Section 7.
Qualifying Universal Preschool Providers

To
receive
Universal
Preschool
funds,
a
provider
must
meet
criteria
established
by
the
Program,

in consultation with the Advisory Committee, to include the following:

A.
Licensure/Certification
​
:
Providers
must
be
approved
to
operate
as
a
childcare

facility, provider or school under one of the following:

1.
A
family
childcare
provider
licensed
pursuant
to
ORS
329A.280
or

registered pursuant to ORS 329A.330;

2.
A
certified
childcare
facility
as
defined
by
ORS
329A.250(5)
and
certified

pursuant to ORS 329A.280 with 501( c)(3) status;

3.
A
certified
parent
cooperative
program
as
defined
by
ORS
329A.250(8)

and licensed pursuant to ORS 329A.280 or equivalent standards; or

4. A facility operated by a public body, as authorized by ORS
329A.250(5)(d) (such as preschools in public schools or city or county run
community centers).
B. Exceptions​: The Program may grant temporary exceptions to providers who do
not currently qualify under Section A of this section when insufficient slots are available to meet
demand. Exceptions shall be approved by the Program, in consultation with the Committee, and
shall remain in place for 3 calendar years, once approved. When granting exceptions based on
enrollment need, preference shall be given to providers that further the mixed-delivery model
and increase parent choices, are locally owned and operated, and are not structured to
distribute profits to shareholders.
C. Quality Programs​: Consistent with the standards developed by the Program,
qualifying providers shall:
1. Provide culturally inclusive or culturally specific programs consistent with
early education best practices which emphasize child-led, trauma
informed, physically active, and developmentally appropriate learning
experiences and de-emphasize academic skill building and testing;
2. Provide parents with the option of year-round programing at no additional
cost; except that the Program may permit a provider to align its schedule
with public school calendar, so long as adequate year round options
remain available;
3. Ensure adequate staffing, generally one adult for eight children; and
4. Include parent involvement opportunities and connect families to
additional community resources and services.
D. Surcharge Tuition Prohibited​: Qualifying Universal Preschool providers may
not assess any surcharge tuition for an eligible child in Multnomah County to attend Universal
Preschool year-round. However, a program may charge for extended hours (those in excess of
six hours per day) typically before and after preschool care.
E. Wages and Benefits​: A qualifying provider shall pay wages and benefits for
preschool workers, consistent with the compensation matrix developed by the Program. That
matrix shall (1) require that all preschool workers receive a living wage of at least 145% of
Portland's minimum wage; (2) adjust wages based on worker education, certification, licensure,
ORO Steps, special skills (such as language), and experience; and (3) move towards parity of
pay and benefits between preschool and elementary school teachers and staff.
F. Professional Development ​: Qualifying providers will provide and facilitate
ongoing professional development of workers, with the financial support of the Universal
Preschool Program. Professional Development funded pursuant to this Ordinance will include
but is not limited to classes that meet the Oregon Department of Education, Early Learning
Division, Oregon Registry program for qualified preschool workers; and onsite and ongoing
mentorship, coaching, workshops and training in relevant methodologies and practices. In
addition, the Universal Preschool Program shall create and support a pathway to licensure by
the Teachers Standards and Practices Commission.
G. Union Organizing Code of Conduc​t: Any employer accepting funds through
this program shall adhere to a code of conduct as related to union organizing:
1. Upon request by an IRC 501(c)(5) labor organization, within 15 days of
the request, the employer shall furnish a complete list of employees,
including job titles and contact information for each individual;
2. The employer shall not engage in any negative campaign with regard to
unionization of the workforce;
3. National Labor Relation Board ("NLRB") elections will be at mutually
agreeable times (as agreed upon by the employer and union) but in no
event in excess of 30 days from the date of filing for an election with the
NLRB;
4. For a first contract, after entering negotiations, at the end of 1 year of
bargaining, either party may submit any outstanding issues to the Federal
Mediation and Conciliation Services, who shall assign a Mediator to the
case. After 3 months of mediation, the Mediator shall have authority to
dictate final contract terms;
5. If the employer fails to adhere to Section 7.G.a to 7.G.d of this Ordinance,
it shall become ineligible to receive funds from Multnomah County tax
assessments for a period of 1 year; and
6. Universal Preschool Program and Multnomah County administration shall
remain neutral with respect to union organizing and representation for any
family childcare provider participating or applying to participate in
Universal Preschool Program, as authorized by ORS 329A.430.
H. Services for Children with Disabilities​: Enrollment in a Universal Preschool
Program will not affect a student's right to special education services, as applicable, as set forth
by state and federal law, including but not limited to Part B of the Individuals with Disabilities
Education Improvement Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1400, et. seq., and Chapter 343 of the Oregon
Revised Statute. Nothing in this ordinance shall be read to preclude or limit the identification of
students with disabilities in a Universal Preschool Program. Nothing in this ordinance shall be
read to preclude or limit ability of a qualifying student attending a Universal Preschool Program
to receive special education services, when applicable.
I. Expulsions​: qualifying Universal Preschool providers will work to minimize
expulsions, supported by additional professional development and coaching aimed at
developing strategies for positive behavioral supports.
Section 8. Multnomah County Board of Commissioners to Take Necessary Action.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners shall take all action necessary to ensure that
taxes imposed by this Ordinance are collected and are used to fulfill the policies and purposes
of this Ordinance: to establish a Universal Preschool program that makes two years of
publicly-funded quality preschool available to all children in Multnomah County.
Section 9. Severability Clause.
If any part, section or provision of this Ordinance or any tax against any Taxpayer imposed by
this Ordinance is found unconstitutional, illegal or invalid, such unconstitutionality, illegality or
invalidity will affect only that part, section or provision of this Ordinance or tax or Taxpayer, and
will not affect or impair any other part, section or provision of this Ordinance.
