A.   Background Facts

1       Lot size and Access: The property is one acre in size and has an easement that connects with

        to be used as the access.

        On-Site Land Use: The parcel is primarily an open field. The property owner also owns the
        adjacent one acre site to the west of the subject property. This one acre site is developed witha
        single family dwelling.

        Surrounding Land Use: The surrounding lots are developed with single family residences on
        generally one to fifteen acre lots. The only exception to this is a parcel to the northwest that is over
        140 acres and is in farm use. This larger farm parcel is outside of McMinnville’s Urban Growth
        Boundary (UGB).

B.    Ordinance Provisions and Analysis

1.       A request for a quasi-judicial zone map change must comply with the standards and criteria in
         Section 1208.02 of the Yamhill County Zoning Ordinance (YCZO), as follows :

         (A)     That the proposed change is consistent with the goals, policies, and any other
                 applicable provisions of the Comprehensive Plan.

         (B)      That there is an existing demonstrable need for the particular uses allowed by the
                 requested zone, considering the importance of such uses to the citizenry or the
                 economy of the area, the existing market demand which such uses will satisfy, and
                 the availability and location of other lands so zoned and their suitability for the uses
                 allowed by the zone.

         (C)      That the proposed change is appropriate considering the surrounding land uses, the
                  density and pattern of development in the area, any changes which may have
                  occurred in the vicinity to support the proposed amendment and the availability of
                  utilities and services likely to be needed by the anticipated uses in the proposed
                  district.

         (D)     That other lands in the county already designated for the proposed use are either
                 unavailable or not as well-suited for the anticipated uses due to location, size, or
                 other factors.

         (E)     That the amendment is consistent with the current Oregon Administrative Rules for
                 exceptions, if applicable.

         Regarding criterion (A), Comprehensive Plan policies that may be applicable include:

                 Policy (c) of Goal 1.A.1: Yamhill County will recognize the lands within established
                 urban growth boundaries as the appropriate and desired location for urban
                 development.

         A one acre lot size is often considered to be an urban scale of development. Since Tax Lot
         4409AD-800 is inside the urban growth boundaryof McMinnville, the scale of the proposed zone
         change complies with this policy.

                 Policyb. of Goal LA.1: Yamhill County will seek to establish a general commitment
                 by all providers of urban services that no subdivision of lands in designated urban
                 areas on the Plan Map will be permitted without the provision of urban services
                 commensurate with the location, nature and scale of the proposed development,
                 recognizing the overall capital program for community services and the ultimate net
                 cost to the community of the services to be provided.

         According to the McMinnville Urban Growth Management Agreement, lands within the UGB but
         outside the city limits shall be designated as FUL (Future Urbanizable Lands) on the county Comp
         Plan Map. Policy I.A.1.b. requires urban services at a scale appropriate to the nature of the
         development. In this case, there is the potential for one additional residence on an existing one acre
         lot. Water to serve the lot could be provided by a well. Individual septic systems and drainfields
         are proposed, but city approval is required prior to installation ofa septic system inside the UGB.
         Consequently, if the city feels that it would be more appropriate for the lots to connect to city
         sewer, the county will not issue septic permits but require connection to city sewer..

         Criterion (B), requires a finding that there is an existing, demonstrable need for the uses that would
         be allowed by the zone change, which in this case is one additional rural residential lot. The Board
         so finds. The subject parcel is almost entirely surrounded by one to fifteen acre lots that are
         developed with single family dwellings. Additionally, to the south are the city limits of McMinnville.
         The property within the city is zoned for residential use. However, the residential uses within the
         city are located on lots that are substantially smaller than one-acre. Because the applicant is
         applying for the AF-10 zone, this will be the primary zone to compare availability. However, since
         the applicant’s parcel is one-acre, areview of available VLDR-1 properties will also be evaluated.

         Areview of the map surrounding McMinnville shows that there are no undeveloped VLDR-1 lots
         located adjacent to the city limits. Further out in the county are exception areas with AF-10
         zones, An area north and south of Brentano Lane has 16 AF-10 lots and 14 of them are
         developed. Areas around St. Joe’s and Riverside Drive contain a total of 21 lots, 12 of which
         have been developed with single family dwellings. One ofthese 21 lots appears to be able to be
         divided. Based on this analysis, there areno undeveloped VLDR-1 lots within close proximityto
         the city and the potential for 12 AF-10 parcels (considering existing undeveloped parcels and those
         that could be divided.)

         Regarding criterion (C), the surrounding area is a mixture of residential and small farm uses. Since
         the subject parcel will be changed to arural residential zone, that change allows small farm uses
         in character with the surrounding area. In addition, the existing one acre lot size is consistent with
         the surrounding parcel sizes, which vary from 1 acre to around 15 acres in size.

         Changes in the form of higher density residential development are gradually occurring in the area,
         as evidenced by the construction of Grandhaven School in the area and 7,000 to 8,000 square foot
         lot size residential development a short distance to the south.

         Utilities and services are all available to serve the new residences that would be allowed by the
         zone change.

         As to criterion (D), the Board finds other residentially zoned lands are either unavailable or not as
         well suited for the development. As indicated in finding B.3., the ability of the request to
         demonstrate compliance with this criterion depends upon whether all residentially zoned land is
         considered, or whether the analysis is limited to rural residential lands. Neither the county or any
         ofits cities have run out of vacant residentially zoned lands. However, there is no - zero - vacant
         VLDR-1 zoned land in the McMinnville area.

         Regarding criterion (E), the parcel is an exception area, so Goals 3 and 4 do not apply. The
         proposal is fora 10 acre minimum lot size. This is not defined as an urban scale of development
         but even ifit was the parcel is already within the urban growth boundary. Therefore, Goal 14 does
         not apply. No other Goals apply to this request, and no exception is required.
