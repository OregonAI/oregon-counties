Program Name: Administration of the Department of Community Justice
Program Description: Program planning.  Operational coordination with criminal justice system components.
Budget preparation and monitoring.  Development and implementation of contract s for
offender resources.  Personnel administration.  Linkage to other county functions related to
services provided to fulfill the mission of the department.
Program Category: Administration
Program Objectives: To provide effective management of the public funds and programs allocated to the effort of
community corrections operations in the county.
Build and maintain working relationships with other departments in the county, to include law
enforcement, prosecution, judicial, treatment programs and the administrative divisions of
the county.
Provide coordination for the Local Public Safety Coordinating Council to oversee the mission
and direction of the program.
Best Practices: Yamhill County Community Justice Administration meets on a weekly basis
with the courts, prosecutor’s office, and representatives from treatment providers in the
county to discuss the movement of clients within the overall criminal justice system. Because
management staff of the various organizations participate, there is the opportunity to take
immediate action on an identified problem or to gather more information for future
discussion. Issues such as jail overcrowding, treatment availability, supervision resources
can be explored and, if resolution is not immediately available, at least understanding and
collaboration is reached between the parties.
Includes some administrative overhead, building reserve (i.e., rent, utilities, etc.), property
and liability insurances)
Method(s) of Evaluation: Statewide outcome measures and County Compliance Reviews (CCR) completed by the
Department of Corrections each biennium.
Evaluation and oversight is exercised by the Yamhill County Board of Commissioners and
the Yamhill County Local Public Safety Coordinating Council.

Program Name: Mental Health Services
Program Description: This category includes intra-county agreements to fund services to Justice Involved
Individuals (JII's), such as mental health programs and mental health evaluations. Services
are provided through Yamhill County Mental Health, Health and Human Services.
Some services are contracted for psychiatric or psychological evaluation with private
providers in the community as needed.
Mental health services are provided in partial support to a limited number of JII's ordered to
be involved in domestic violence treatment who also have special needs.
Best Practices: Yamhill County Mental Health as well as private physicians and
psychologists have advanced professional degrees and provide professional treatment
services.  Yamhill County is a part of the Behavioral Health Care Network.  The program has
rigid standards for treatment planning and care.
Program Category: Behavioral Health Tx Services - Mental Health
Program Objectives: Yamhill County Health and Human Services Mental Health Department provides contracted
services to Community Justice in support of individuals under supervision who have
identified mental health disorders.  The objective is to provide appropriate medical and
mental health services to individuals needing this service.
Private physicians and psychologists provide evaluation when this is ordered by the Court as
a part of a presentence investigation or ordered as a condition of supervision.
Program methodology and treatment approaches are considered to be consistent with
Evidence Based Practices, defined to suggest content and curriculum are designed to match
criminogenic factors identified in referral and evaluation of the JII.
Method(s) of Evaluation: The measurement of overall program effectiveness begins with the entry of the program in
the treatment module of CIS, permitting the department to determine treatment completion
rates and program recidivism rates.   In determining if the treatment program is evidence-
based, the RNR Program Tool evaluation will be utilized.

Program Name: Substance Abuse / Chemical Dependency
Program Description: Intake, evaluation, assessment and treatment of AOS court or parole board ordered or
referred by the supervising officer to enroll in alcohol & drug rehabilitation.  This is an intra-
county agreement for service between Health & Human Services via Yamhill County
Chemical Dependency services and Community Justice.
AOS enrolled in and who complete treatment programming for addicted individuals appear
to experience better outcomes (i.e. a reduction of re-arrest or re-offense, long term sobriety,
etc.).  Even resistive individuals generally gain some benefit and increased motivation for
pro-social change by participating in treatment and/or treatment readiness programming.
This is a collaborative model which stresses the balanced approach of accountability and
evidence-based treatment to produce the greatest possible reduction in chemical
dependency, alcohol and drug use and recidivism.
Program Category: Behavioral Health Tx Services - Substance Abuse
Program Objectives: Engage the individual 'where they are'  in level of motivation and readiness for chemical
dependency services and overall life change.
Utilize evidence-based practices in delivering treatment services.
Evaluate mental health status and chemical dependency risk points.
Develop an individualized case and treatment plan for each AOS in partnership with Health
and Human Services Chemical Dependency.
Provide ongoing status reports to the supervising officer regarding AOS performance in
treatment which, in turn, is provided to the Court and/or other supervising authority.
Method(s) of Evaluation: The measurement of overall program effectiveness is tracked with the data entry of the
program referral, entry date and completion date into the treatment module of CIS/AS400,
permitting the department to determine treatment completion rates and program recidivism
rates. The Corrections Program Checklist (CPC) evaluation is also used by the Department
of Correction to determine treatment program adherance to SB 267 requirements. The last
CPC was conducted in June 2017 and Health and Human Services collaborated with DOC
staff toward continued program improvements. Another CPC was tentatively scheduled for
2020 but was presumably cancelled due to the COVID pandemic. Another CPC has not yet
been rescheduled.

Program Name: Electronic Monitoring
Program Description: The Electronic Monitoring Program provides electronic supervision of Justice Involved
Individuals (JII's) through the use of global positioning system (GPS) device that the JII
wears on their ankle.  This system allows for continuous monitoring of JII's location and
adherence to geographic and curfew restrictions.  Optional 24 hour alcohol monitoring is
used as well with the use of the Secure Continous Alcohol Monitoring (SCRAM) unit. This
program serves all Medium and High risk JII's as a sanctioning or intervention option.
Community Justice contracts with Vigilnet America LLC to provide GPS, curfew and alcohol
monitoring services, end user software and all necessary hardware.  An in-house Probation
Officer Aide has been trained to install/remove the hardware on behalf of Vigilnet in order to
streamline enrollment locally, as Vigilnet does not have a local office.
This program provides an alternative option to incarceration when officers are addressing
violation behavior while maintaining a high level of monitoring.
Program Category: Community-Based Custodial Alternatives
Program Objectives: Allow for intensive surveillance for identified high risk JII's.
Allow for 24 hour alcohol monitoring, curfew verification and location services.
Provide JII's with an alternative to serving a period of incarceration, which affords them the
opportunity to maintain employment, housing and community stability.
Method(s) of Evaluation:  Local data collection for participant success rate, currently at 55% successful completion.

Program Name: Custodial and Sanction Beds
Program Description: Local Control - Incarceration of individuals convicted of a felony at the Yamhill County
Correctional Facility for sentences or terms of 12 months or less; includes Yamhill County
court sentences, probation revocations, parole/post-prison supervision revocations and
administrative sanctions. An Adult In Custody may also be moved to the Work Center if
determined to be eligible for that program.
Custodial sanction beds – incarceration for sanctions of up to 30 days in the Correctional
Facility or Work Center for JII who have violated terms and conditions of their probation,
parole or post-prison supervision.
Yamhill County Correctional Facility operates a 48-bed Work Release/Restitution Center for
which Yamhill County Department of Community Justice manages the program participants.
Sentenced and sanctioned individuals with verified employment are screened for enrollment
into the work release program.  Work Release enrollees are released each day to their jobs,
returning to the facility after each work shift.  The work center also serves as a part of the
continuum of available sanctions for PO utilization.
Program Category: Custodial/Sanction Beds
Program Objectives: Provide greater local authority over relatively short-term Adults In Custody.
Offer a range of sanctions to appropriately respond to violations of the conditions of
supervision.
Incorporate structured and supervised work activity.
Provide an opportunity for individuals with a history or prospect for employment to maintain a
job while serving a jail sentence or sanction.
Create opportunities for an employed individual to provide family support, pay restitution and
other court-ordered costs while serving a jail sanction/sentence.
Offer an intermediate sanction that would allow the person being sanctioned to maintain
existing employment.
Program effectiveness is measured by successful transition from custody status, to
transition, and then successful reintegration back into the community.  This JII cohort is
evaluated through standardized statistical tracking by the research unit of the Oregon
Department of Corrections.
Method(s) of Evaluation: The practical evaluation of success for this group is to serve the jail term without incident
and participate in available in-custody resources, as well as participate in work crew and
work release programs.  Thereafter, resulting in a successful return to the community by
securing an independent residence and full employment.  Data collection will occur through
CIS/OPS with analysis by Yamhill County Community Justice, Board of Commissioners and
the Department of Corrections.  Also, the Office of Economic Analysis completes two annual

Program Name: Supervision Services
Program Description: Field felony, funded misdemeanor and non-state funded misdemeanors supervision of
Adults on Supervision (AOS) sentenced in State Circuit Courts, released from Depar tment of
Corrections or county correctional facilities (i.e. local control SB1145 AOS), and cases
received through the Interstate Compact process.  Additionally, Adults In Custody (AIC) who
are granted Short Term Transitional Leave (STTL) are supervised in the community for a
period of up to 120 days. Officers provide intake orientation, initial risk assessment,
classification and identification of essential needs.
Cases are assigned to the appropriate department program, providing for supervision and
engagement in treatment if appropriate. Sanctions and incentives will be utilized for AOS
following department guidelines and available resources.  JII's scoring in the high and
medium risk category will be given supervision and services priority.  All High and medium
risk AOS will have an individualized targeted case plan developed in partnership with the
assigned PO.  Supervision strategies will adhere to the basic principles of Evidence- Based
Practices. Caseloads are organized by crimes type such as domestic violence, sex offense,
behavioral health, low risk casebank, geographically, gender female specific and Adult
Recovery Court (ARC) and Court Coodinated Services (CCS) behavioral health court.
Program Category: Supervision
Program Objectives: Achieve satisfaction of court ordered or Parole Board imposed conditions to promote a
positive transition from incarceration back into the community.  Promote law-abiding
adjustment from incarceration to self-support.  The department will assist in transition by
verifying an appropriate residence, developing employment and supporting the AOS in pro-
social activities and programs. PO will use motivational interviewing and refer to treatment
and coginitive programming to assist the AOS in making better life choices addressing such
issues as alcohol and drug use/addiction, criminal associations and poor choices leading to
involvement in criminal activities. A spectrum of supervision methods, sanctioning and
incentives serve to reinforce supervision compliance and law-abiding behavior.
Performance indicators measuring sustained lawful conduct, remaining in contact with the
supervising officer and fulfilling the terms of the order measure the effectiveness of
supervision programming.
To reduce public health hazard and community destabilizing impact of misdemeanor
offenses such as DUII that are not funded by Department of Corrections Community
Corrections Grant In Aid. Resources and policy practice among enforcement agencies,
prosecution, judiciary, community corrections supervision and public health agencies are
coordinated and county general funds are utilized to provide case supervision of certain
misdemeanor offense not otherwise funded for supervision.
Method(s) of Evaluation: Department evaluation is determined by completion of general and special conditions,
completion of mandated treatment programs, a sustained period of sobriety and no new
crime arrests while under supervision.
Annual caseload audits to confirm completion of service referrals, assessments, and contact
standards.
Performance indicators measuring sustained lawful conduct, maintaining regular contact
with the supervising officer and fulfilling the terms of the court or parole board order measure
the effectiveness of this program.
Evaluation for statistical purposes will be determined by successful case closure, compared
to the indicators of the intergovernmental agreement (IGA) between the Oregon Department
of Corrections and Yamhill County.
STTL data and outcome measures are tracked by both Department of Corrections and the
Criminal Justice Commission as it relates to the Justice Reinvestment Initiative.
Department of Corrections Community Corrections Reviews.

Program Name: Transition- Ready to Work & Subsidy
Program Description: The Day Management Center (DMC) acts as a hub of support services to clients under the
supervision of Yamhill County Department of Community Justice.  DMC focuses services on
medium and high-risk probation and post-prison supervision JII for the purpose of obtaining
employment, furthering education, and/or adding an extra layer of accountability to
supervision provided by Department of Community Justice.  In collaboration with the Health
and Human Services Specialist, the Probation Officer making the referral can request
assistance with utilizing job search tools, interview preparation and resume building.  These
services have been scaled back due to the departure of our Program Specialist and lack of
sufficient funding.
Services are provided to assist JII without a residence. Lack of stable, substance-abuse free
housing has been shown to be a major contributing factor in recidivism. Housing assistance
is viewed as an evidence-based practice.  Other subsidies as needed include DMV ID,
clothing, electronic equipment, food, and hygiene items, etc.
Program Category: Transition Services
Program Objectives: Provides skills development in resume creation, job application completion, information
systems, and employment interviewing/cognitive restructuring around employment
maintenance.
Assist JII transitioning from prison and/or local control jail sentences.
The core objectives of this program includes active participation by JII in life skills endeavors
and enhancement of the employment process. It is also an expectation that there will be a
reduction in probation/PPS violations and criminal activity among those successfully
participating in the program.
Concentrated employment assistance will be afforded to JII through job search strategies
training, active employment development, and one-on-one job search guidance/ coaching.
Make available a matrix of services, including: employment skills, job referral assistance and
support, incentives/rewards, mentorship and community support.
Provides assistance with obtaining identification, driver's license, transportation via bus
passes as needed.
Provides short-term placement in a gender specific, alcohol and drug free residence such as
Oxford House, Helping Hands and Serenity House.
In limited instances, permit the use of financial assistance as “seed money” for rent deposit
or a short stay in a moderately priced commercial lodging establishment.
Provides housing to homeless or indigent Short Term Transitional Leave Department of
Corrections Adults In Custody and other supervised JII to assist with the alleviation of
statewide prison bed usage.  Other subsidies as needed include DMV ID, clothing, electronic
equipment, food and hygenie items, etc.

with the adopted IGA between Yamhill County and Oregon State in order to maintain best
practices standards.  Evaluation will also be undertaken internally by tracking the DMC
cohort with programs and resources synchronized to attend to criminogenic risk factors.
Regarding housing or other subsidy need, the objective is placement in a temporary or semi-
permanent residence after the subsidy assistance is provided.  Evaluation will be
accomplished by the officer of record tracking the establishment of a suitable residence by
the offender.

Program Name: Evidence-based Sentencing Project (Smart Sentencing Initiative)
Program Description: Yamhill County implemented the Smart Sentencing Initiative program in November 2013.
This program was designed to decrease state prison growth by assessing specific
presumptive prison bound defendants to determine appropriateness for prison deferral or a
reduction in prison months.   The Defendant Asessment Report (DAR) targets presumptive
prison, non-person, felony, offenses on a pre-sentence level.  The DAR is 3-6 page report
prepared by Community Justice which outlines a defendant’s risk for recidivism based on the
LSCMI (Level of Services Case Management Inventory), Women's Risk Need Assessment
(WRNA) and the ASUS (Adult Substance Use Survey).  In addition to providing risk,
motivation, substance abuse and mental health information, DCJ makes a recommendation
whether or not the defendant could be safely managed in the community.  It also outlines
suggested Special Conditions of community supervision which are based on the risk areas
identified in the DAR.  Currently, there is one JRI funded Community Justice
Parole/Probation Officer responsible for completion of all DAR cases as well as provide
community supervision of those who receive downward dispositional departures as a result
of the DAR.  Supervision is enhanced, evidence and dosage based supervision with
increased contact standards and content of contact includes cognitive behavioral
interventions.  There is one JRI funded .5 FTE PO Aide who supports associated data
collection and cognitive behavioral intervention work with program participants.  One grant -
in-aid Corrections Manager supervises the program and is responsible for overall data
collection.
Program Category: Other Programs and Services
Program Objectives: Provide evidence based risk, motivation, substance abuse and mental health information on
a pre-sentence basis with the goal of reducing state prison growth.  Recommend Special
Conditions of community supervision that are directly connected to an individual’s identified
criminogenic risk areas to reduce the likelihood of recidivism.  Provide enhanced, dosage
based, community supervision for those who receive downard dispositional departures as a
result of the DAR.
Method(s) of Evaluation: The SMART Sentencing Program tracks the number of people that enter DOC custody; the
number of participants involved in the downward departure program; success rate of those
participants; treatment and other services offered to each JII.  It also continues to track
probation revocations (technical, treatment court failure, abscond and new crime).
The program also tracks a host of demographic data to aid in ongoing decision making, such
as race/ethnicity, gender, employment status, age, and education levels.  Judicial
concurrence rates, estimated prison months, and evaluator recommendations are tracked.
Our data indicates that an estimated 4335 prison months have been diverted from
November 2013 through June 2023.  As a result of our efforts, 220 JII have been deferred
from prison and 106 of those have successfully completed supervision.  Just 68 JII have
been revoked, and only 16 were connected to a new criminal conviction.
Project data is routinely shared through regular CJC progress reports, LPSCC and Evidence
Based Decision Making Policy Team meetings.

Program Name: Pretrial Justice Services
Program Description: The Yamhill County Pretrial Services Officers (PSO) provides all pretrial defendants with
interviews and risk assessments within 24-hours of initial arrest and booking at the local jail
for the purposes of providing a release recommendation to the Court for initial arraignment.
Current elements of Yamhill County’s Pretrial Justice Program include: assessment of risk,
community stability and other factors as determined by Oregon statute; communication of
information and written report of release recommendations to the Court; monitoring of any
pretrial release conditions imposed by the Court; additional review of those held in jail
pretrial on a regular basis for additional release consideration; and measure performance for
program improvements as needed.  Additional services that are provided include pretrial
monitoring of over 600 defendants with accountability of pretrial misconduct and
reinforcement of compliant behavior and automated notification of court dates for those on
pretrial release through Twilio, which works with the Court's Odyssey case management
system.  PSO's utilize Automon for interviews, data collection and general case
management.  Outcome measure reporting are a project focus.
Program Category: Other Programs and Services
Program Objectives: The mission of the Yamhill County Pretrial Justice Agency is to promote pretrial justice for
people who are accused of committing crime and for the people and communities who have
been harmed by crime.  We achieve this mission by maximizing three things: pretrial jail
release of appropriate defendants who can be safely managed in the commun ity, court
appearance and community/victim safety.  Current meta-analysis reveals that pretrial
incarceration is directly correlated with the severity and length of the sentence imposed upon
conviction.  This finding was recently replicated in Oregon via a Portland State University
research study.  Per the principle of “presumption of innocence”, release of the correctly
assessed defendants both decreases risk and the length of jail or prison sentences imposed
upon case resolution. Through assessment, we are able to make evidence-informed
decisions about jail release of low risk defendants who can remain in the community safely
without additional criminal activity.  We also increase pretrial defendant accountability
through pretrial monitoring services (i.e., office visits, phone contacts, response to
community complaints, UA, electronic/alcohol monitoring, etc.) and automated court
notification.
Method(s) of Evaluation: Yamhill County pretrial output measures include risk assessment, pretrial interview of all
incarcerated defendants, release recommendation to court upon arraignment , future release
consideration past arraignment hearing date, pretrial monitoring and response to pretrial
misconduct, and court notification.   Pretrial outcome measures that are currently and/or will
be collected and analyzed are: court appearance rate, safety rate (the percentage of
released defendants who are not arrested with a new offense during the pretrial stage),
concurrence rate (the ratio of defendants whose supervision level or detention status
corresponds with their assessed risk of pretrial misconduct), success rate (the percentage of
released defendants who are not revoked for technical violations of the conditions of their
release and appear for all scheduled court appearances, and are not arrested with a new
offense during pretrial supervision), pretrial detainee length of stay, number of defendants
released by release type, caseload ratio (the number of monitored defendants divided by the
number of case managers), time on pretrial monitoring and pretrial detention ratio
(proportion of pretrial defendants who are detained throughout pretrial case processing).To
date, we have reduced our pretrial jail population by approximately 20% since 2011, while
maintaining our pretrial court appearance rate of approximately 5%.

Program Name: Victim Services
Program Description: Post conviction advocacy is performed by a half-time victim advocate located within the
Department of Community Justice.  They spend the majority of their time working with our
domestic violence Parole/Probation Officers (PPOs) due to extensive communicati on of
safety plans, reunification and victim outreach. Advocacy also occurs with any case where
there is a victim to provide Post Conviction Rights, notification of Probation Violations,
Earned Discharge, Revocations and obtaining victim input on Early Terminations of
probations.
Advocacy work can lead to support at court hearings, communication with treatment
providers, PPOs and providing education and information to victims regarding community
resources.
As part of our Justice Reinvestment Initiative, Community Justice partners with Juliette's
House and Henderson House, both local nonprofit organization which provide victim
services in the community.  Juliette's House is Yamhill County's only children's advocacy
center and primary point for child abuse assessment, intervention, treatment and support.
They provide medical and forensic assessments of children ages 0-18 who have been
referred by law enforcement and child protective services, who have been suspected a
victim of sexual, physical abuse, serious neglect, drug endangerment and witness to
domestic violence.  Henderson House provide advocacy for victims of domestic violence,
sexual assault, and human trafficking.  They also operate a 30-day shelter, and 24-hour
crisis line.
Program Category: Other Programs and Services
Program Objectives: Provide Victims with Post-Conviction rights by notifying them of Probation Violations, Earned
Discharge and Revocations and asking for input from Victim's in Early Terminations.
Provide support by attending court hearings if requested.
Work with the Parole/Probation Officer (PPO), treatment provider and victim to assist in the
reunification process.
Provide continued support throughout the probation/parole process by communicating
effectively with the victim, supporting victims where they are at in the process, providing
victims with community resources and making sure they are fully informed of their rights.
Assist PPOs with clients who have also been victims.
Provide Advocacy to Children ages 0-18 who have been victims of abuse.
Provide Mental Health Treatment and support for child victims of abuse.
Provide Safe Boundaries Workshops for families impacted by abuse.
Provide prevention education to children in pre-school through Grade 8.
Reduce incidents of child abuse through prevention education and collaborative intervention
with local community agencies and organizations working with children.
Method(s) of Evaluation: By upholding Victims Rights and following state laws in regards to Victim Rights.
Reduction of the number of child abuse cases in Yamhill County.
