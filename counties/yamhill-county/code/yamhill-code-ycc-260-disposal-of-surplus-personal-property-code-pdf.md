2.60.01 Definitions. The following definitions shall apply to the provisions of this ordinance:
a) “Appropriate Newspaper” - A newspaper determined in the discretion of the director to be suitable to
provide appropriate notice to bidders, giving the value and type of property, and the expected return.
b) “Board” - The Board of Commissioners of Yamhill County.
c) “Competitive Bid” - A bid received as a result of an advertisement for the sale of surplus property including
a bid received either at public auction or a sealed bid.
d) “Director” - The Director of the Department of Administrative Services of Yamhill County or the director's
designee for purposes of general personal property; the Director of Public Works or the director's designee
for motor vehicles; the Yamhill County Sheriff or the sheriff's designee for property in the custody of the
sheriff.
e) “Negotiated Sale” - The sale of property to an individual, business or other organization without competitive
bid, provided that such method of sale is in the best interests of the county and without favoritism.
f) “Public Auction” - A sale of surplus property to which the public is invited to submit either written or oral .
bids for the surplus property.
g) “Sealed Bid” - A bid submitted in writing and sealed in an envelope, to be opened at a specific time and place
set forth in the appropriate advertisement.
h) “Surplus Property” - Personal property of the county which is no longer needed for public purposes.

2.60.02 Duties of Director.
a) Duties. The Director shall be responsible for collecting, appraising and securing surplus property of the
county. In so doing, the Director may make such arrangements as determined appropriate for the storage,
repair or recondition of the surplus property. The director shall also be responsible for the sale or other disposal
of the surplus property in accordance with the terms of this ordinance.

2.60.03 Disposal.
a) Sale or Gift to Other Governments and Nonprofit Corporations. Upon such terms as the Board may direct, or
upon the director's discretion, surplus property of the county may be sold or conveyed to a unit of local
government, school district, the State of Oregon or its agencies, the U.S. government or its agencies, or those
nonprofit corporations which are eligible under federal law to acquire surplus property, so long as the property
is to be utilized for public purpose, without competitive bid, and with or without compensation. The restriction
that the property be utilized for a public purpose applies to disposal of surplus property, pursuant to this
section, regardless of identity of the recipient of the property.
b) P
roperty With an Estimated Value Under $1000. Upon terms deemed appropriate by the director, the director
may sell or convey to a private party any surplus property valued at less than $1000, upon approval by the
Board.
c) P roperty With an Appraised Value of $1000 or More. Property with an estimated value of $1000 or more may
be sold by the director to private parties as follows:
(i) Property which is customarily sold on a recognized market may be sold on that market without
competitive bid, provided the price received is equal to or greater than wholesale value determined
under Section 2.60.02(c) of this ordinance. If there is no wholesale value, then the appraised value
shall apply. Property shall not be sold under this subsection for less than the wholesale value  or
the appraised value.
(ii) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (i) above, or Section 2.60.03(b), any surplus property
may be sold by competitive bid. Property which does not fall within the terms of subsection (i),
Section 2.60.03(b), or property which is not disposed of under Section 2.60.03(a), shall be sold in
the manner prescribed in this subsection. Sale of property by competitive bid shall be either by
public auction or sealed bid, whichever is determined at the discretion of the director to be
appropriate. In either event, the director shall publish public notice of  the sale at least once in an
appropriate newspaper. The notice shall appear not more than 15 days and not less than 7 days
before the sale. The notice shall include descriptions of the items to be sold, a time and place for
viewing the items, and the time and place for the scheduled sale or receipt of bids. At the director's
discretion, a minimum bid may be applied to any item to be sold. Nothing in this section prevents
the director from publishing other notices as may be deemed appropriate.
d) Board Approval. Except as provided in Section 2.60.03(a), the director shall not sell surplus property for less
than the wholesale value or appraised value, whichever is lower, without approval by the Board.
e) E xpedited Sale. The  Board may approve any sale of surplus property without compliance with Sections
2.60.03(a) to (d) upon the Board's finding that the sale meets all of the following criteria:
(i) The steps taken by the director in securing a purchaser were reasonable;
(ii) Further efforts to secure a better price or other purchaser are not likely to be successful or will
result in additional costs which would off-set any expected increase in the purchase price;
(iii) The price offered was not determined as a result of collusion;
(iv) The sale will not result in a financial benefit to the Board, the director, or any members of their
immediate families; and

2.60.04 List of Property.
a) List of Surplus Property. During the second quarter of the fiscal year the director shall present a list of surplus
property to the Board for its consideration. The list shall contain all personal property proposed for disposal,
the appraised value of the property where applicable, and the manner used to determine the value. Where such
property may be of interest to other public jurisdictions, the director may send the surplus property list to
various county departments, cities, school districts and s pecial districts located within Yamhill County. To
the extent consistent with sound financial practices, a public entity shall be accorded preference in the
purchase of surplus property. The actual date of sale shall be set to insure that a sufficient amount of property
is available for sale to attract public interest. Where possible, such sale shall be held in  cooperation with the
sales of other public agencies.
b) I
nteragency Cooperation . The publication and notice requirements of this ordinance may be waived if the
county is offered the opportunity to jointly participate with another public agency in an auction, provided
however that the sponsoring agency has provided reasonable notice of sale.
c) T
rade- In. At the discretion of the director, surplus property may be used as a trade to acquire new property
for the county. The property traded shall be from the same fund or department as the new property will be.
Where property is traded between funds, appropriate compensation shall be made.

2.60.05 Other Laws. This ordinance is in addition to any other requirement  which may arise as the result of
federal or state law, rule  or regulation which restricts the use or transfer of surplus  property in general, or of a
particular piece of surplus property.
