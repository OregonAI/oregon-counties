SUBJECT: Public Information and Community/Media Relations Guide

DATE: August 14, 2002   REVISED: January 2018
       December 2022
       February 2026

I. POLICY

Clatsop County is a public service agency and recognizes that everyone who reads a
paper, listens to TV or radio, uses social media, or the Internet is a customer.

Employees will promote transparency and establish and maintain open and honest
relations with community residents, businesses and media representatives that supplies
Clatsop County communities with information about county activities and actions.

A. Objectives

1. To assure that information from the County is accurate, truthful, clear,
useful and timely.

2. To keep the public informed about County policies, services, activities
and plans.

3. To promote timely and accurate information about County policies,
services, activities or plans.

4.       To develop understanding by the public and the news media of the
      County and its programs.

II.  PROCEDURES AND GUIDELINES

A. General Guidelines

Employees are to treat the public and representatives of the news media equally
and with courtesy and respect. Information provided shall be accurate and clear
and provided in a timely manner.

Employees will keep the following in mind and act accordingly.

B. Open Meeting and Records

1. Oregon Law specifically gives citizens the right to attend most meetings of boards
and commissions (ORS 192.610-690).  Employees with responsibilities for
organizing meetings of these groups are responsible for complying with the law.
Governing bodies must give notice of the time, place and agenda for any regular,
special or emergency meeting.

Public notice must be reasonably calculated to give actual notice to interested
persons and media who have asked in writing to be notified of meetings and general
notice to the public at large. Adequate notice is defined as a week to ten days.
At least 24-hour notice is required to members of the governing body, the public and
media for any special meeting, unless the meeting is considered an emergency
meeting. Notice for emergency meetings must cite the emergency.

A meeting notice must include a list of the principal subjects to be considered at the
meeting. This list should be specific enough to permit those wishing to attend to
recognize matters of interest.  Employees and departments should consult and abide
by the latest edition of the Attorney General’s Public Records and Meetings Manual.

2. Public records of the County including written materials, photographs, recording of
sounds or pictures, maps, magnetic tapes, disks, digital and other documents are
available for public inspection unless expressly exempted by the Oregon Public
Records and Meetings Law or by federal or state law outside the state public record
law. The public is to be granted convenient access to all records during normal
business hours. Copies are to be provided in accordance with the current fee
schedule.

C. Media Contacts

A. General

1. Reporters are people doing a job. Employees can help them be successful by
providing complete, accurate and timely information to the Public Information
Officer.
2. Honor media deadlines. If a media proposed deadline cannot be met, inform the
media representative and secure a new deadline.
3. Employees should be alert to issues of public interest that can be passed to
news media via the PIO or their designee. Anything that affects the public is
newsworthy; i.e., road closures, public meetings, new laws, programs. Think of
the public’s viewpoint. What does the issue or topic mean for people, businesses,
communities and taxpayers?

4. If a story is given to one media agency, we give it to all. If a media representative
develops a story, it is not our job to share it with other media.
5. Be honest. Do not hide facts. Direct questions deserve direct answers.
6. If the media reports a serious misstatement, contact the PIO so they can request
a correction. Even if a correction is not published or aired, repetition will be
avoided.
7. If special events are being planned, check media deadlines and schedules to
avoid conflicts if possible. Be alert to differing deadlines.
8. For planned media interviews, schedule practice time with the PIO and have
three to five approved talking points ready for the interview
9. If you do not want to be quoted, do not say it. You can’t go “off the record”.
10. If you do not know an answer, say so. If you are not authorized to speak to the
media, provide the facts to the PIO who will forward them on to the media
representatives.
11. Direct all media inquiries to the PIO via media@ClatsopCounty.gov. Provide as
much background information as possible.
12. Make sure the PIO has advance information that can be provided to news
representatives if the media will agree to a specific release date and time. Major
study reports and budgets are typical examples of when to provide that
information.

B. Routine Distribution of Written Materials

The County will electronically distribute written materials to all media. In addition
to news releases and public service announcements, the distribution will include
agendas for board and commission meetings and news conference notices.

News releases are drafted by the PIO or their designee, approved by the
department subject matter expert and department director. Then they are
distributed by the PIO through the media@ClatsopCounty.gov email. All news
releases will be posted on the County web page.

News releases shall generally be distributed to all news media located within
Clatsop County or that regularly covers the county and via FlashAlert.

C. Live and Virtual News Conference

Major stories may deserve a formal news conference where all media
representatives are invited.  When scheduling news conferences work to meet
news deadlines when possible. The PIO will prep the newsmaker before the
news conference, and have them present and prepared to answer questions.

The County Manager’s Office and the PIO must approve of, participate in the
planning of and attend all press conferences.

D. News Releases and Public Service Announcements

Have an issue, topic or report on a County activity that could be of interest to a
fairly substantial part of the community?  Contact the PIO at
media@ClatsopCounty.gov. The PIO will draft a news release for review by the

PREPARATION OF NEWS RELEASES

The news media likes tight, well -organized, significant copy. News releases should not be
disguised advertisements or present opinion rather than fact.

MUST DO
• Doublecheck Spelling. Review names and check copy for typos. Incorrect spelling takes
away from the news release and focuses attention on errors instead of content.
• Source of information. Doublecheck with the SME and Department director to confirm data
is accurate.
• Day and Date. Double check to make sure the day and date listed in the release are correct.

GUIDELINES:
• First paragraph should contain who, what, where, when. Specify the exact day and date. Say
“Tuesday, May 3" to avoid confusion.
• Paragraphs and sentences should be short and to the point. No fluff.
• Draft a dynamic quote from the SME, department head or other County representative.
• Subsequent paragraphs may contain other facts, figures, etc.
• Proofread carefully.
• Read to yourself aloud or have someone else read it. How does it sound?
• No editorial or opinion comments.
• List the address as well as the place of an event.
• If an event has already occurred, get the information to the media ASAP.
• Respect deadlines. Radio stations have deadlines throughout the day. Weekly newspapers
need a couple week s’ notice. If possible, get the release a couple days before the desired
publication date.
• Use Associated Press style.

PREPARING NEWS RELEASES

1. Draft media release using the media release template.
2. Have the media release approved by the:
• Subject Matter Expert (SME).
• Department head (related to the release) and
• Public Affairs Officer.
3. The name of the release document should end with _FINAL. For example, a press
release going out is named, “Clatsop County Approved Budget_FINAL.pdf”
Place the final documents (in both .docx and .pdf formats) into the folder W:\CM\Public
Affairs\Media Releases\

6. Place the media release text in the email.
7. Attach the .pdf version of the media release to the email.
8. Send the media release.

Below Freezing Temps, Snow, Ice and Wind Gusts;
Clatsop County Emergency Management Urges
Public to Prepare

Dec. 21, 2022 (Astoria, OR) —Clatsop County Department of Emergency Management is urging
residents and businesses to brace themselves and prepare for extremely cold and icy weather
and to limit travel to the essentials and emergencies on Thursday, Dec. 22 and Friday, Dec. 23.

“If you must travel in your car, make sure to have a winter emergency kit. Items you should
include are candles and matches, a flashlight, pocket knife, snacks, phone charger, a blanket and
extra clothing,” said Justin Gibbs, Clatsop County emergency management director.

Gibbs said that due to freezing rain, hazardous road conditions will likely persist even with the
application of sand or other treatments. “We want people to understand that there are limited
resources to cover a large geographic area that will be impacted by this weather event and we
don’t want them to put themselves in harm’s way.”

Thursday morning lows likely will be the coldest since 2014, according to the Portland National
Weather Service which is urging residents to prepare now to protect people, pets and livestock
and plants. Thursday morning temperatures may drop to low 20s throughout Clatsop County.

Keep in mind that frostbite can occur on exposed skin in less than 10 minutes. Symptoms
include a loss of feeling and a white or pale appearance in fingers, toes, ear tips and the tip of
your nose. Signs of hypothermia include shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands,
memory loss, slurred speech and drowsiness.
