CLATSOP COUNTY                                       Policy A-3  Page-1

SUBJECT:

DATE: September 2024

The purpose of this policy is to provide organizational guidance prior to removing homeless
individuals from an unapproved campsite on publicly owned property within
unincorporated Clatsop County.

Applicability:  This policy applies to unapproved camping on all publicly owned property within
unincorporated Clatsop County.

I. POLICY

 A guiding principle for Clatsop County is to protect the livability of our community and to
ensure the humane treatment of persons removed from camping sites on all publicly
owned property.

1. Identifying Campsites for Removal
1.1. The County will prioritize removal based on the impact of particular campsites.
Campsite impact is determined based on a combination of factors, including but not
limited to:

1.1.1.   Evidence of conspicuous drug use, paraphernalia, or improperly disposed of
syringes
1.1.2.   Impact on neighborhood livability.
1.1.3.   Proximity to school, parks with playground, retail/hospitality businesses or
private    residence
1.1.4.   Environmental impact on natural areas and/or the presence of hazardous
materials
1.1.5.   Restriction of access as defined in the Americans with Disabilities Act standards
1.1.6.   Areas that are posted “no-trespassing”
1.1.7.   Size of camp
1.1.8.   Verified reports of violence or criminal activity other than camping
1.1.9.   Blocking public access including County rights-of-way
1.1.10. Restricting construction or maintenance activities

2. Notice – Except for situations subject to the exceptions set forth in ORS 195.505(9)
2.1. The County will provide 72-hour notice prior to a campsite removal in both English and
Spanish
2.2. Notice location
2.2.1.   The County shall post a written notice at all entrances to the extent that the
entrances can be reasonably identified.
2.2.2.   The County will take steps to ensure that the posting is visible and not obscured
from view
2.2.3.   The County’s posted notice will be placed in a plastic cover, or other steps will
be taken to ensure that the notice is protected.
2.3. The County’s posting notice will:
2.3.1.   State the address location and phone number where they may contact to locate
their property.
2.3.2.   State the hours of operation where they may contact to locate their property.
2.3.3.   State that all personal property of value will be stored per ORS 195.505 for 30
days from the date of publication of notice.
2.3.4.   The County’s posting notices will refer people to social services that may be
available to them
2.3.5  Upon posting notice at a location, the County will inform homeless outreach
social service organizations of the campsite removal.

3. Removal Process
3.1. Upon arriving at a campsite location for removal, County agents will take reasonable
steps to make all persons present aware that there will be a removal and cleanup.
Except as provided in Section 4.3, County agents will notify campsite residents that
personal property will not be removed for at least 30 minutes.  However, trash pickup
will begin immediately, and County agents will reasonably coordinate with residents to
identify trash.
3.2. People may observe County employees or agents performing removal and may assist
residents removing their property, provided they do not interfere with the work of County
employees or agents, and they comply with reasonable requests of County employees
or agents related to actions that interfere with the work of County employees or agents.
3.3. Residents present and outside the campsite at the time of removal will be offered a
business card with the phone number of the storage location where their property can
be retrieved.
3.4. The County will photograph the campsite location, including property and garbage, prior
to bagging items.

4. Law Enforcement Involvement
4.1. The County is committed to the safety and wellbeing of its residents and agents and
therefore will use a compassionate approach, prioritizing the use of de-escalation
techniques to avoid conflict and achieve positive outcomes when feasible.
4.2. The 72-hour warning notice requirement is not necessary if deputies reasonably believe
that illegal activity unrelated to the camping is occurring at an established camping site

or in the event of an exceptional emergency such as possible site contamination by
hazardous materials, a public health emergency, or other immediate danger to human
life or safety (ORS 195.505).
4.3. The County will not delay for 30 minutes, per section 3.1, the removal of personal
property from a campsite when the campsite occupant has been arrested.

5. Personal Property, trash, weapons, drugs
5.1. The County will store all personal property, meaning any item that can be reasonably
identified as belonging to an individual and has apparent value.
5.1.1. Property kept greater than 15 feet away from a dwelling will generally not be
considered to belong to an individual unless specific circumstances during the
removal or cleanup suggest otherwise.
5.1.2. Property kept within a pile of unsanitary or non-utile property will generally not be
considered to belong to an individual unless specific circumstances during the
removal or cleanup indicate otherwise.
5.2. The County will discard upon removal items that have no apparent value or utility or are
in unsanitary condition.
5.2.1. An unsanitary condition is a condition or circumstance that will likely lead to injury
or health problems for individuals in the vicinity, including but not limited to areas
where hazardous or contaminated materials are stored in a manner that is not likely
to protect those in the vicinity.  Personal property is not deemed to be in an
unsanitary condition solely due to wetness from outdoor weather conditions.
5.2.2. A substance or material is hazardous or contaminated if it is capable of posing an
unreasonable risk to health, safety, and property when contacted or transported.
5.3.   The mandatory storage and non-storage policy included as Exhibit A provides
additional guidance on how items are evaluated for storage.
5.4. The County will provide all deadly weapons – instruments, articles, or substances
specifically designed for and presently capable of causing death or serious physical
injury – to law enforcement for storage.
5.5. The County will store unexpired prescription medication that has a patient’s name on it.
However, expired prescription medication, illegal drugs, or a mixture of pills contained in
a prescription medication container will be disposed of as medical waste unless it is
provided to law enforcement as evidence of a crime.
5.6. The County will not limit the number of articles of personal property stored for any
individual person.
5.7. Redeemable bottles and cans collected together in a bag will be stored as personal
property.
5.8. The County will store all identification, money, credit/debit cards, and prescription
medication, in a bag separate from other personal property.

6. Timeline for Property Storage
6.1.   The County will store, in accordance with ORS 195.505, all personal property for 30
days from the date the notice was posted.  Certain personal property will be stored
longer, as indicated below.

6.2. The County will extend the period of time for which personal property is stored in up to
90-day increments for individuals experiencing extenuating circumstances that impede
their ability to retrieve their personal property, such as a hospital stay or incarceration.
A request for extended storage can be made by calling the number of the storage
location.  The County may request reasonable documentation of the extenuating
circumstances.

7. Retrieval Processes
7.1. The County will require persons to provide identification or be properly identified to
recover personal property stored following a removal.
7.2. To recover personal property, a person must provide the approximate date and place of
the removal as well as a description, by color and type of certain articles of property.
7.3. The phone number listed on the posting notices will generally be answered during the
operating hours indicated on the posting notice.

8. Post-removal Notice
8.1.  The County will post on-line, within one week of removal, the location of the removal
and the date by which removed property must be claimed.

Mandatory Storage Policy
Exhibit A

County campsite clean-up crews are instructed to store the items identified below
regardless of condition:

• Tents – unless damaged beyond useful purpose
• Tarps – unless damaged beyond useful purpose
• Backpacks/Purses
• Cash, debit/credit cards, Oregon Trail Cards (cash will be counted in the
presence of two people and documented)
• Prescribed unexpired medication with a patient’s name
• Any personal identification i.e.: Driver’s License, Social Security Card, Birth
Certificate, Passport, TPI Card, etc.
• Bikes/Bike Trailers (If operable and in working order)
• Tools (not fasteners i.e. screws, bolts, nails)
• Cell Phones/Computers/Electronic Devices

Non-Storage Policy
County clean-up crews cannot store any of the following:
• Pets
• Food/Beverages
• Waterlogged items
• Items so contaminated with human or animal bodily fluid or waste; vermin, bugs, insects,
or other materials that are known to be harmful, that the item is either unusable or
exposure to the item during transportation and storage will likely lead to injury or health
problems for people who are exposed to the item.
• Gasoline, kerosene, lighter fluid
• Empty gasoline can containers
• Propane tanks
• Charcoal, cooking/heating briquettes/wood chips and wood pellets –
• Portable or makeshift toilets
• Explosives of any type
• Shopping carts
• Liquor, beer, wine
• Illegal substances, controlled substances, or drug paraphernalia under state or federal
law
• Bleach, ammonia, other hazardous chemicals
• Hazardous materials and environmental pollutants (e.g., degreasers, fertilizers, drain
cleaners, paint thinner, pesticides).
• Electrical storage system (i.e. batteries) with corrosion or damage.
• Miscellaneous bike parts
• Building materials that appear to be found or repurposed materials, not originally
designed to be used as building materials, such as cardboard and pallets.
