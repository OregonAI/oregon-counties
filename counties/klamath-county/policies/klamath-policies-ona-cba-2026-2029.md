COLLECTIVE BARGAINING AGREEMENT
between the
OREGON NURSES ASSOCIATION (ONA)
and
KLAMATH COUNTY
July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2029
PREAMBLE
This Agreement is entered into by Klamath County, Oregon, hereinafter referred to as 1
the "County", and the Oregon Nurses Association, hereinafter referred to as the 2
"Association". Unless indicated otherwise, references to the "Employer" or "County" 3
herein shall include the Board of County Commissioners and the Director of the Public 4
Health Department of Klamath County as the officials responsible to the citizens of the 5
County for operation of the department covered by this Agreement. 6
7
The purpose of this Agreement is to set forth those matters pertaining to the rates of 8
pay, hours of work, fringe benefits, and other matters directly related to employment of 9
Registered Nurses in the bargaining unit covered by this Agreement. 10
11
The parties both agree as follows: 12
13
ARTICLE 1. – RECOGNITION
The County recognizes the Association as the sole and exclusive bargaining agent for 14
the purposes of establishing salaries, wages, hours, and other conditions of 15
employment for all of its Registered Nurses employed on a regular full-time or half-time 16
one thousand five hundred and sixty (1,560) to one thousand nine hundred and forty 17
nine (1,949) hours per year basis within the Klamath County Public Health Department, 18
excluding Registered Nurses employed on an irregular part-time one thousand five 19
hundred and fifty nine (1,559) hours or less per year, on-call, or temporary/seasonal 20
basis and supervisors as defined in ORS 243.650(23). 21
22
For purpose of this Agreement, the following definitions apply: 23
 1.1 Section 1 – Regular Full-Time. 24
A regular full-time employee shall be an employee who is hired into, appointed 25
to, or currently occupies a position that is authorized, budgeted, and designated 26
by the County as a regular full-time position. Such a regular full-time position will 27
normally be scheduled to work thirty-seven and a half (37.5) hours to forty (40) 28
hours per week, providing that, nothing in this Agreement shall constitute a 29
guarantee of any number of hours per day, per week, nor per year. 30

 1.2 Section 2 – Regular Half-Time. 1
A regular half-time employee shall be an employee who is hired into, appointed 2
to, or currently occupies a position that is authorized, budgeted, and designated 3
by the County as a regular half-time position. Such regular half-time position will 4
normally be scheduled to work at least thirty (30) and less than thirty-seven and a 5
half (37.5) hours per week. Such regular half-time employee shall be considered 6
a member of the bargaining unit covered by this Agreement and shall be entitled 7
to employer paid benefits on a prorated basis based on their FTE; with the 8
exception of the medical contribution amount. Employees that are a point seven 9
five (.75) FTE or greater shall receive the medical contribution amount equivalent 10
to a full-time employee. For example, a zero point six zero (0.60) FTE employee 11
would receive sixty percent (60%) benefits.  12
ARTICLE 2. – ASSOCIATION MEMBERSHIP 1
2.1 Section 1 – Membership. 2
Membership or non-membership in the Association shall be the individual choice 3
of employees covered by this Agreement. The Human Resources Department 4
will provide a fifteen (15)- minute opportunity for a Nurse representative to 5
communicate with new hires within thirty (30) days. If a Nurse elects to join the 6
Association, the Association representative will provide the Human Resources 7
Department with the signed union dues deduction, which will be placed in the 8
Nurse’s personnel file. Such payment shall be equal to and shall in no event 9
exceed periodic dues uniformly required. The County agrees that no disciplinary 10
or other action will be taken by the County against employees covered by this 11
Agreement by reason of action or conduct as provided in this Article.  12
2.2 Section 2 – Membership Dues. 13
The County agrees to continue to deduct from the paycheck of each employee 14
who has so authorized it, the regular monthly Association membership dues of 15
the Association. The amounts deducted shall be transmitted monthly to the 16
Association on behalf of the employees involved. Authorization for deduction of 17
dues shall be in writing, signed by the employee on forms furnished by the 18
County and may be revoked by the employee upon written request. It is the 19
employee's responsibility to ensure the authorization forms are returned to the 20
Human Resources Department within the first ten (10) days of employment. 21
Failure to do so may result in back payment of dues accordingly. The 22
performance of this service is at no cost to the Association. The employee must 23
submit, in writing, any change in membership status to the ONA Membership 24
Coordinator at the following address: 25

Oregon Nurses Association 1
18765 SW Boones Ferry Road, Suite 200 2
Tualatin, OR 97062 3
Attn: Membership Coordinator 4
 5
If the employee has elected payroll deduction, the ONA will notify the Human 6
Resources Department of the change in membership status and the County will 7
begin deducting the new amount reflecting the change in status. 8
 9
2.3 Section 3 – Indemnify and Save. 10
The Association shall indemnify and save the County harmless against any and 11
all suits, claims, demands and liabilities that arise out of or by reason of action 12
taken by the County pursuant to this Article 2. 13
 14
2.4 Section 4 – Member Lists. 15
Upon request, the County shall provide a digital list to the Association labor 16
relations representative and Association member services of all bargaining unit 17
Nurses, which indicates their current unit, step level, address, telephone number 18
(unless directed not to do so by the Nurse), status, and date of employment. This 19
list shall also indicate new hires and terminations subsequent to the prior list. 20
Within thirty (30) days of the first day of employment, the County shall provide 21
the Association with a list of all new employees represented by the Association 22
which indicates their current unit, step level, address, telephone number, (unless 23
directed not to do so by the Nurse), status and date of employment.  24

ARTICLE 3. – MANAGEMENT RIGHTS
The County retains all the customary, usual, and exclusive rights, decision-making, 1
prerogatives, functions, and authority connected with or in any incident to its 2
responsibility to manage the affairs of the County or any part of it. The rights of 3
employees in the bargaining unit and the Association are limited to those specifically set 4
forth in this Agreement. The County retains all prerogatives, functions, and rights not 5
specifically limited by the terms of this Agreement. 6
 7
The exercise of any management prerogative, function, or right which is not specifically 8
modified by this Agreement is not subject to the grievance procedure, to arbitration, or 9
as set forth above, to bargaining during the term of this Agreement. Operational policies 10
or management procedures not specifically addressed in this Agreement shall be 11
subject to the current Klamath County Human Resources Policy & Procedures Manual. 12

ARTICLE 4. – STRIKES AND LOCKOUTS
4.1 Section 1 – Continuity of Service. 1
No employee covered by this Agreement shall engage in any work stoppage, 2
slowdown, picketing, or strike at any County facility or place where County 3
functions are performed during the life and duration of this Agreement. If any 4
such work stoppage, slowdown, picketing, or strike shall take place, the 5
Association will immediately notify such employees engaging in such activities to 6
cease and desist, and it shall publicly declare that such work stoppage, 7
slowdown, picketing, or strike is in violation of this contract. Employees in the 8
bargaining unit, while acting in the course of their employment, shall not refuse to 9
cross any picket line established in the County by any labor organization when 10
called upon to cross such picket line in the line of duty. Any employee engaging 11
in any activity in violation of this Article may be subject to disciplinary action or 12
discharge. 13
 14
4.2 Section 2 – Lockouts. 15
There will be no lockout of employees in the unit by the County during the life 16
and duration of this Agreement. 17

The following shall be recognized as holidays for Nurses: 2
• New Year’s Day3
• Martin Luther King, Jr. Day4
• Labor Day5
• President's Day6
• Memorial Day7
• Veterans' Day8
• Independence Day9
• Thanksgiving Day10

Whenever a holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday shall be observed 15

pay when a holiday occurs, such holiday shall not be charged against such 18
leave.  19
20
5.2 Section 2 – Holiday Pay. 21
Eligible Nurses shall receive one (1) day’s pay for each of the holidays listed 22
above on which they perform no work. Holiday accrual and payment shall be 23
prorated in direct proportion to a Nurse’s hours paid during any period of time in 24
which a Nurse works less than the normal forty (40)-hour two thousand and 25

28
When a holiday falls on a day off for a less than full-time, forty (40)-hour per 29
week Nurse, that Nurse shall be awarded another day off with pay at the prorated 30
rate. Such day off shall be as mutually agreed to between the Nurse and the 31

Public Health Director, or his/her designee, but shall be approved and taken 1
within ten (10) working days from the date of such holiday. 2
5.3 Section 3 – Holiday Work. 4
If a Nurse is required by the Public Health Director, or designated person, to work 5
on any holiday listed above, he/she shall receive, in addition to his/her regular 6
pay, a full day off to be scheduled by mutual agreement between the employee 7
and the Public Health Director. 8

ARTICLE 6. – PAID TIME OFF (PTO)
Section 1 – Purpose. 1
Paid Time Off (PTO) replaces all other sick leave, vacation leave and floating 2
holiday leave except for the longevity floating holiday described in Section 12.4. 3
The County will award a PTO bank as provided herein for employees to take time 4
off as needed.  5
 6
PTO is a flexible plan that allows each employee to accrue one kind of time 7
(PTO), this can be used for sick leave or vacation leave in any proportion an 8
employee may see fit. PTO can also be used for other unscheduled leave for 9
which sick leave would not be appropriate such as school activities, daycare 10
issues, and extended bereavement. 11
 12
Section 2 - Accrual. 13
Regular Nurses shall be entitled to earn PTO time in accordance with 14
subsections listed hereafter. All regular full-time employees are eligible for the 15
PTO bank. Half-time employees will accrue at a pro-rated amount, equal to or 16
greater than the Oregon Sick Leave Requirement PTO shall accrue on a per-17
pay-period basis from the date of hire. Thereafter, PTO shall accrue for each 18
regular hour paid at the rates in accordance with the PTO accrual table. 19
 20
PTO will accrue at the following rates beginning July 1, 2025: 21

Years of Service PTO Accrual Rate
Up to 5 years (beginning at date of hire) 8.67 hours per pay period
6 to 10 Years (beginning at 5-year anniversary) 9.67 hours per pay period
11 to 15 Years (beginning at 10-year
anniversary)
11.67 hours per pay period
16 to 19 Years (beginning at 15-year
anniversary)
12.5 hours per pay period

Section 3 - PTO Maximum Exceptions. 22

All employees will be subject to a maximum PTO accrual of 1040 hours except 1
for employees who were hired prior to January 1, 1998 will be allowed a 2
maximum PTO of 1360 hours. If an employee is denied a request for PTO due to 3
staffing or business concerns, and the denial would result in the employee’s 4
accrual exceeding the maximum allowed, the employee shall not lose the accrual 5
at that time. The employee has until the end of the fiscal year to utilize any PTO 6
over the maximum accrual. Any remaining amount over the maximum accrual as 7
of July 1st will be forfeited.  8
 9
Section 4 – Continuous Service. 10
Continuous service for the purpose of accumulating PTO credit shall be service 11
unbroken by separation from the County. Time spent on compulsory military 12
leave, paid sick leave, or authorized educational leave shall be included as 13
continuous service. All other types of authorized leave, including lay-off, will not 14
be counted as continuous service. Nurses returning from this type of leave will be 15
entitled to credit for service prior to the leave or layoff. 16
 17
Section 5 – PTO Use & Scheduling. 18
During absences from work, eligible employee wages will be continued for the 19
amount of time accrued in the employee’s PTO account. An employee is allowed 20
to use any or all of the employee’s PTO to allow the employee to provide care for 21
a family member with a mental or physical illness, injury, or health condition; or to 22
care for a family member who needs preventive medical care. Family member in 23
this case is equivalent to the OFLA family member definition. 24
Planned Time Off: Planned time off is when the employee has made a request 25
and has been approved for time off in advance of taking the time. These would 26
include things such as vacations, scheduled medical absences, other scheduled 27
appointments and other absences for which time off is requested and approved 28
in advance of the immediate need. Employees requesting time off for personal 29
reasons shall provide their supervisor with as much advanced notice as possible. 30
If insufficient notice is given, supervisors may deny personal PTO requests 31
based on departmental needs. Subject to the foregoing, PTO preference shall be 32
determined based upon seniority. Each Nurse shall be permitted to exercise 33
his/her right of seniority one (1) time each year. 34

Unplanned Time Off: Unplanned time off is when the employee requests time off 2
without prior notice, such as for emergencies, including but not limited to illness, 3
transportation, or childcare issues. When reasonably possible, an employee shall 4
notify the Public Health Director, Nursing Supervisor or designated person at 5
least one-half (1/2) hour before the start of the scheduled work shift if the 6
employee is unable to report to work as scheduled. An employee must notify the 7
Public Health Director, Nursing Supervisor, or designated person informed of 8
their condition if the unplanned use of PTO is more than three (3) working days in 9
duration. Depending on the length and circumstances of the absence, a 10
physician’s release may be required upon returning to work. In certain cases, 11
FMLA or OFLA may apply. 12
 13
Section 6 – Payout, Transfer & Termination. 14
Payout for PTO: Employees may request up to forty (40) hours of PTO in a fiscal 15
year to be paid out at eighty percent (80%) of the hourly wage. Payout will only 16
be allowed if the employee has already used forty (40) hours of PTO during the 17
fiscal year.  18
 19
Transfer Credits: 20
If a Nurse transfers to another County Department, all PTO accumulation 21
shall be transferred to the new department provided that the labor contract 22
language of the bargaining unit, if any, to which the Nurse transfers does 23
not conflict with this stated transfer right. In the event of a conflict, the 24
language of the new bargaining unit or employee group to which the 25
Nurse will be employed shall prevail.  26
 27
Terminal PTO Pay: 28
Nurses shall be entitled to cash compensation at the regular rate of pay 29
for unused accumulated PTO as described below based on seniority. In 30
case of death, the Nurse's estate would be paid. 31
• For years 0-5, maximum payout is 160 hours. 32
• For years 6-10 (complete 5 full years), maximum payout is 240 hours.  33
• For years 11-15 (complete 10 full years), maximum payout is 288 hours. 34

• For years 16-20 (complete 15 full years), maximum payout is 352 hours. 1
• For years 21-25 (complete 20 full years), maximum payout is 400 hours.  2
Section 7 – PTO Donations 4
Employees may, with approval of the Public Health Director and the Human 5
Resources Director, donate up to a combined maximum of forty (40) PTO hours 6
(on an hour-for-hour basis) per calendar year to other eligible Klamath County 7
employees in need of paid leave time. This shall follow the County’s Donation 8
policy in the Human Resources Policy and Procedures Manual.  9
 10
Section 8 – Conversion of Vacation & Sick Leave 11
Employees as of June 30, 2025 will have any hours remaining in their sick and 12
vacation banks rolled over into their PTO bank. For those that have sick leave 13
and vacation leave that exceeds the maximum allowed amount for their group, 14
employees may keep excess sick leave in their sick leave bank to use for sick 15
leave.  16
 17
Section 9 – Integration of Workers’ Compensation 18
All employees are covered for accidents on the job under the Workers’ 19
Compensation laws of Oregon. When an injured employee must take time off by 20
reason of occupational injury or disability, the employee will receive time-loss 21
payments from the County’s Workers’ Compensation insurer. In addition, 22
supplemental benefits will be provided as follows on approved Workers’ 23
Compensation claims: 24
• The first three (3) months following an on-the-job injury and an approved 25
workers’ compensation claim, the County will supplement the employee’s 26
time-loss benefits in an amount necessary to equal the injured employee’s 27
net regular salary after taxes. 28
• After three (3) months following the date of an on-the-job injury, accrued 29
PTO and compensatory time may be used to supplement the employee’s 30
time-loss benefits in an amount necessary to equal the injured employee’s 31
net regular salary after taxes.  32
• Following the exhaustion of the employee’s benefits, the employee shall 33
receive Workers’ Compensation as provided under State Law. 34

ARTICLE 7. – HOURS OF WORK
7.1 Section 1 – Normal Workweek. 1
The workweek, to the extent consistent with operating requirements of the Public 2
Health Department, shall normally be Monday - Friday. However, in cases of 3
emergent or extenuating circumstances when a Nurse is required to work on a 4
Saturday or Sunday, the Nurse shall be provided with an alternate time off, equal 5
to the number of hours worked, in either the five (5) days preceding or within 6
thirty (30) days. 7
 8
7.2 Section 2 – Hours. 9
The regular scheduled work hours each day shall normally be consecutive, 10
except for interruptions for rest and meal periods as outlined in Section 7.4. The 11
regular workday and workweek shall be in accordance with the departmental 12
practice and procedures in effect on the date of this Agreement, provided that 13
nothing in this Agreement shall constitute a guarantee of any number of hours 14
worked per day or per week. 15
 16
7.3 Section 3 – Schedules. 17
To the extent consistent with operating requirements and the need to adjust 18
schedules to meet patient needs, all Nurses shall be scheduled to work on a 19
regular work shift, as determined by the Public Health Director or his/her 20
designee. Except in case of emergent or extenuating circumstances, Nurses will 21
be given at least three (3) business days’ notice of any change in schedule. 22
 23
7.4 Section 4 – Rest Periods. 24
For non-exempt employees, a rest period of fifteen (15) minutes for every 25
segment of four (4) hours of work will be given. As much as possible, this break 26
should be taken approximately midway in the segment of the four (4)-hour period 27
and shall be scheduled by the County in accordance with its determination as to 28
the operating requirements of each employee's duties. Exempt employees may 29
take rest periods as described above, but are not required to do so.  30

7.5 Section 5 – Meal Periods. 1
Except in emergency or unusual circumstances, non-exempt Nurses shall be 2
granted an uncompensated meal period of no less than thirty (30) minutes, not to 3
exceed sixty (60) minutes in duration for each six (6) hour work period in 4
accordance with State and Federal Law. To the extent consistent with operating 5
requirements of the department, meal periods shall be scheduled at or near the 6
middle of the work shift. Exempt employees may take meal periods as described 7
above, but are not required to do so.  8
ARTICLE 8. – LEAVES OF ABSENCE
8.1 Section 1 – Parental and Family Leave. 1
Nurses shall be allowed to take parental and family leave in accordance with 2
OFLA and FMLA. Upon application, and with the prior approval of the appropriate 3
administrator or his/her designee, employees shall be allowed to utilize the 4
County Paid Leave Oregon Equivalent Plan and/or PTO for absences resulting 5
from application of this Section. 6
7
Upon termination of OFLA/FMLA, the nurse may request an additional ninety 8
(90) days sick leave absence. All accruals must be exhausted prior to going into9
an unpaid status. If the employee is unable to work and perform their regular 10
duties after this ninety (90) days leave of absence, the employee may be 11
terminated. The County will not maintain the normal contribution for health 12
insurance, but shall make available the option for coverage, during this extended 13
ninety (90) days leave of absence. 14
15
16
8.2 Section 2 – Unpaid Leave. 17
A leave of absence without pay, not to exceed ninety (90) calendar days, may be 18
granted upon establishment of reasonable justification in instances where the 19
work of the department will not be adversely affected by the temporary absence. 20
Requests for such leaves must be in writing. Such leave will not be approved for 21
any Nurse for the purpose of accepting employment outside the service of the 22
County. 23
24
8.3 Section 3 – Bereavement Leave. 25
A Nurse shall be granted five (5) days of paid bereavement leave with regular 26
salary in event of the death of an immediate family member as defined in OFLA. 27
A Nurse's immediate family member shall include spouse; same-gender 28
domestic partner; biological, adoptive, step or foster parent; parent-in-law; parent 29
of domestic partner; biological, adopted, foster, or stepchild; child-in loco parentis 30
including any person with who the employee is or was in a relationship of in loco 31
parentis; child of the employee’s same-gender domestic partner; grandchild; 32
grandparent. An additional 1 week of unpaid bereavement leave may be granted 33

to a qualifying employee per OFLA guidelines. This must occur within sixty (60) 1
days of receiving notification of a family member’s death. The employee may use 2
accrued leave. Additional time off may be requested by the Nurse should 3
personal circumstances demand an extended absence. The granting of such 4
time shall be subject to the approval of the Public Health Director Nursing 5
Supervisor, or designated person and will consider the impact of Nursing 6
operations and services in the approval or denial. 7
 8
8.4 Section 4 – Jury Duty. 9
Nurses shall be granted leave with pay for jury duty upon providing a copy of the 10
jury summons to the Public Health Director. Upon being excused from jury 11
service, a Nurse shall immediately contact the Public Health Director, Nursing 12
Supervisor, or designated person for assignment for the remainder of the regular 13
workday. 14
 15
8.5 Section 5 – Appearances. 16
Leave with pay shall be granted for an appearance before a court, legislative 17
committee, judicial, or quasi-judicial body as a witness in a Nurse's professional 18
capacity and in response to a subpoena or other direction by proper authority. 19
Insofar as is practicable, court appearances shall be scheduled in such a way as 20
to avoid overtime. 21
 22
8.6 Section 6 – Association Business. 23
Nurses elected to any Association office or selected by the Association to do 24
work which takes them from their employment with the County shall, upon written 25
request of the Association and the Nurse, be recommended by the Nurse's 26
supervisor to the Public Health Director for a leave of absence without pay. 27
Members of the Association selected to participate in other Association activity 28
shall, to the extent consistent with the operating requirements of the department, 29
be granted a leave of absence without pay at the request of the employee. Any 30
Nurse who has been granted such a leave of absence and who, for any reason 31
fails to return to work at the expiration of said leave of absence, shall be 32
considered as having resigned his/her position with the County unless the Nurse, 33
prior to expiration of his/her leave of absence, has made application for and has 34
been granted an extension of said leave or has furnished evidence that he/she is 1
unable to return to work by reason of sickness or injury. 2
8.7 Section 7 – Unpaid Educational Leave of Absence. 4
After completing two (2) years of continuous service, a Nurse, upon written 5
request, may be granted a leave of absence without pay by the County for the 6
purpose of upgrading his/her professional ability through enrollment in 7
educational courses directly related to employment at an accredited school or 8
course of study. A period of such leave of absence shall not exceed one (1) year, 9
but may be renewed or extended upon request of the Nurse and approval of the 10
Public Health Director or designated person for up to one (1) additional year. One 11
(1)-year leaves of absence may not be provided more than once in any three (3)-12
year period. When a Nurse returns to the employ of the County after an unpaid 13
leave of absence, the Nurse shall be placed in the following sequence: First (1st), 14
their prior position, if available; Second (2nd), a comparable position, within the 15
department, for which the employee is qualified, if available; Third (3rd), a lesser 16
position, within the department, for which the employee is qualified, if available. 17
Finally, if no work is available, the employee will be separated. 18
19
8.8 Section 8 – Paid Educational Leave. 20
Both the County and the Association affirm their commitment to providing training 21
programs and other similar programs intended to broaden the skills and 22
professional abilities of bargaining unit members in the field of public health. 23
24
The County agrees that each Nurse shall be granted a minimum of twenty-four 25
(24) hours of public health related training per year, at least sixteen (16) hours of26
which will be initiated at the Nurses' request. Such training may consist of in-27
house workshops, training sessions, briefing sessions and other programs of a 28
similar nature as well as similar opportunities that may be available inside or 29
outside of the Klamath Falls/Klamath County area. 30
A training plan will be developed by the bargaining unit Nurses and their 31
supervisor and submitted for approval by the Public Health Director on a semi-32
annual basis. This cooperatively developed Plan will ensure that the professional 33
development of each Nurse and the mission of the County will be served to the 34
optimum. Normally, dollar amounts dedicated in this area shall be limited to a 1
reasonable amount based upon the course or subject, as defined by the County, 2
and shall attempt to be inclusive of all Nurses so that all Nurses have an equal 3
opportunity to benefit from such programs. 4
5
8.9 Section 9 – Reimbursement. 6
The County shall pay all costs associated with such approved educational leave 7
under Section 9.8. Additionally, the County will reimburse expenses in 8
accordance with the County reimbursement policy. 9

ARTICLE 9. – HEALTH, WELFARE AND RETIREMENT
9.1 Section 1 – Health Benefits. 1
The County shall provide health and medical insurance coverage comparable to 2
the present coverage of Major Medical, or comparable plan, coverage for each 3
eligible employee covered by this Agreement. The County will not duplicate 4
premiums paid in the event an employee and his/her spouse are both employed 5
and are eligible for coverage. In such event, monthly payments shall be paid only 6
on behalf of one (1) eligible employee, the other employee being designated as a 7
dependent for health insurance.  8
 9
Effective July 1, 2026, the County will contribute $1780.00 per month, on behalf 10
of each eligible employee, towards the cost of the health, dental, and vision 11
insurance plans. Effective July 1, 2027, the County will contribute $1830.00 per 12
month, on behalf of each eligible employee, towards the cost of the health, 13
dental, and vision insurance plans. Effective July 1, 2028, the County will 14
contribute $1880.00 per month, on behalf of each eligible employee, towards the 15
cost of the health, dental, and vision insurance plans. Dental and vision 16
insurance will be at the option of the employee. Any excess amount will go to the 17
employee’s HRA/VEBA or HSA account.  18
 19
 20
 21
Any required premium in excess of the amounts contributed by the County will be 22
paid by each employee. The County may apply minor cost cutting techniques to 23
the major medical (or comparable) plans and to adjust the medical plan contract 24
year. Such payment shall be applied to purchased group medical and hospital, 25
major medical, dental, vision, prescription, group life and weekly time loss 26
benefits. 27
 28
9.2 Section 2 – Retirement. 29
The County agrees to enroll each eligible and qualified employee in the Klamath 30
County Employees’ Pension Plan (Plan) and pay the employer's contribution. 31
Eligibility for retirement under the Plan shall be age sixty-two (62) or at age sixty 32
(60) with unreduced benefits. 33
The County agrees to maintain the benefits provided in its existing Retirement 2
Plan, providing for new enrollees to start participation on the first day of the 3
month following the date of hire. Effective July 1, 2007 the County will pay one 4

ARTICLE 10. – GRIEVANCE AND ARBITRATION
10.1 Section 1 – Definition of Grievance. 1
A grievance is an allegation by an employee covered by this Agreement or the 2
Association that the County has violated an express provision of this Agreement. 3
4
10.2 Section 2 – Representation. 5
The Association may appoint a primary Nurse representative and one (1) other 6
Nurse representative to act in the absence or in the event of a conflict of the 7
primary Nurse representative. 8
9
 The Association shall maintain a listing of the primary and alternate 10
representatives and shall ensure that the County is furnished with such current 11
listing. The grievant may have the Nurse representative present at any step of 12
the Procedural Steps listed below. 13
14
10.3 Section 3 – Procedural Steps. 15
STEP 1. Written Notice to the Immediate Supervisor. 16
It is the wish of both parties to this Agreement to settle any complaint in the most 17
expeditious and informal manner possible. Not later than five (5) business days 18
after the event giving rise to the complaint, or five (5) business days after the 19
employee should have learned of the event giving rise to the grievance, 20
whichever is later, the employee must discuss the grievance with the immediate 21
supervisor. The immediate supervisor shall respond in writing to the employee 22
not later than five (5) business days, not including the date of presentation, 23
thereafter. 24
25
STEP 2. Written Grievance to Public Health Director. 26
If the complaint is not settled at Step 1, the employee, not later than five (5) 27
business days, not including the date of written response, after the immediate 28
supervisor's written response is given, shall submit a dated and signed written 29
grievance to the Public Health Director, detailing the alleged contract violations. 30
The immediate supervisor shall give his/her written answer to the grievance 31
within five (5) business days, not including the date of presentation, after receipt 32
of the grievance. 33

STEP 3. Written Appeal to the Human Resources Director. 2
If the grievance is not settled at Step 2, the employee, not later than five (5) 3
business days after receipt of the Public Health Director’s written answer at Step 4
2, may file a written appeal to that answer to the Human Resource Director. The 5
Human Resources Director, at their option, may meet with the employee and the 6
employee's Nurse representative to discuss the grievance. The Human 7
Resources Director shall give their written answer to the grievance within five (5) 8
business days after receipt of the grievance or after the date of the meeting, 9
whichever is later. 10
 11
STEP 4. Mediation. 12
If the decision at Step 3 is unacceptable to either party, it may be referred to a 13
mutually-agreed upon mediator who is a member of a nationally-recognized 14
mediation or conflict resolution association. Each party shall be responsible for 15
paying its own cost of meditation, including the payment of bargaining unit 16
participants. The fees and expenses of the mediator shall be borne equally by 17
the Association and the County.  18
 19
STEP 5. Arbitration. 20
If the grievance is not settled after Step 3 or Step 4, either party may, within five 21
(5) business days after either (1) the written answer by the Human Resources 22
Director or their designee or (2) the participation of a mediator at Step 4, have 23
the matter submitted to final and binding arbitration by a third party jointly agreed 24
upon by the County and the Association. If the parties are unable to agree upon 25
an arbitrator, the Oregon State Mediation and Conciliation Services shall be 26
requested to submit a list of seven (7) names. Selection of an arbitrator from the 27
list may be by mutual agreement between the parties or by alternately striking 28
one (1) name each from the list until one (1) name is left. The first strike shall be 29
determined by the flip of a coin. The designated arbitrator shall hear both parties 30
and take testimony and evidence in a hearing on the disputed matter and shall 31
issue a decision which shall be final and binding on the parties. The arbitrator 32
shall be limited to the interpretation of the provisions of this Agreement in making 33
the decision. Each party shall be responsible for paying the cost of presenting its 34

own case in arbitration, including the payment of witnesses and bargaining unit 1
participants. The fees and expenses of the arbitrator and the hearing room shall 2
be borne equally by the Association and the County. If either party desires a 3
verbatim recording of the proceedings, it may cause such a record to be made, 4
providing it pays for the record and makes a copy available without charge to the 5
arbitrator. If the other party desires a copy, both parties shall jointly share the 6
cost of the transcript and all copies. 7
 8
10.4 Section 4 – Written Presentation. 9
All grievances presented in writing shall set forth: the facts giving rise to the 10
grievance; the provision(s) of the Agreement, if any, alleged to have been 11
violated; the names of the aggrieved employee(s); and the remedy sought. All 12
grievances shall be signed and dated by the aggrieved employee. All written 13
answers submitted by the County shall be signed and dated by the appropriate 14
County representative. 15
 16
10.5 Section 5 – Time Limitations. 17
The time limitations set forth in this Article are of the essence of this Agreement. 18
No grievance shall be accepted by the County unless it is submitted or appealed 19
within the time limits set forth in this Article. If the grievance is not timely 20
submitted or appealed at any step, it shall be waived. If the grievance is not 21
timely appealed, it shall be deemed to have been settled in accordance with the 22
County answer in Step 1. If the County fails to answer within the time limits set 23
forth in this Article, the grievance shall automatically proceed to the next step. 24
Any time frames may be mutually waived by agreement between the parties. 25

ARTICLE 11. – PROBATIONARY PERIOD
11.1 Section 1 – Purpose. 1
The probationary period is an integral part of the employee selection process and 2
provides the County with the opportunity to upgrade and improve efficiency of 3
operation and service to the public by observing a new employee's work, training 4
and aiding new Nurses in adjustment to their positions, and by providing an 5
opportunity to reject any Nurse whose work performance fails to meet required 6
work standards. 7
 8
11.2 Section 2 – Duration. 9
Every new Nurse hired into the bargaining unit shall serve a probationary period 10
of six (6) full months. The probationary period may be extended up to an 11
additional six (6) months by mutual agreement between the Nurse, the 12
Association, and the County. PTO may be used as it is accrued. 13
 14
11.3 Section 3 – Application. 15
The Association recognizes the right of the County to terminate probationary 16
Nurses for any reason or at any time during the probationary period and to 17
exercise all rights not specifically modified by this Agreement with respect to 18
such employees, including, but not limited to, the shifting of work schedules and 19
job classifications, the assignment of on-the-job-training, cross-training in other 20
classifications, the assignment to educational courses and training programs, the 21
requirements that such Nurses attend training programs on their off-duty time 22
shall be considered time worked. 23

ARTICLE 16. – SENIORITY, LAYOFF & RECALL
16.1 Section 1 – Employment seniority. 1
Employment seniority is defined as the length of time the employee has been 2
continuously employed in the County. 3
 4
16.2 Section 2 – Continuous employment. 5
Continuous employment is defined as uninterrupted full or half-time service in the 6
County. 7
 8
16.3 Section 3 – Length of Service. 9
For the purpose of determining the length of service within the County, the 10
accumulation period shall begin with the original date of hire for continuous and 11
uninterrupted regular full time or regular half time employment status as a Nurse. 12
 13
16.4 Section 4 – Layoff. 14
In the event of layoff, the least senior Nurse shall be laid off first based on total 15
hours worked. A Nurse whose position has been eliminated or has been laid off 16
by the County, shall be given the opportunity to displace a less senior Nurse, 17
provided he/she possesses the necessary skills and ability to perform the duties 18
of the position. Medical benefits shall be provided during the first thirty (30) days 19
of layoff. 20
 21
16.5 Section 5 – Layoff and recall procedures. 22
Layoff and recall procedures will be implemented on ONA bargaining unit 23
seniority. 24
 25
16.6 Section 6 – Subsequent Reinstatement. 26
In the event of subsequent reinstatement, recall shall be in reverse order of 27
layoff, i.e., the last Nurse laid off will be the first reinstated. 28

16.7 Section 7 – Written Notice. 29
Employees shall not be laid off unless they have received written notice at least 30
thirty (30) days before their layoff date. Either party may propose meetings to 31
consider work sharing or other alternatives to a contemplated layoff. 32
16.8 Section 8 – Seniority. 2
Seniority shall be broken by: 3
17.8.1 – Termination. 4
5
17.8.2 – Layoff Consecutive Six (6) Months. 6
Layoff which is continued for more than six (6) consecutive months. 7
8
17.8.3 – Failure to Accept a Recall. 9
Failure to accept a recall within seven (7) calendar days of receipt of a written 10
recall notice. 11
12
16.9 Section 9 – New Hire. 13
No new Nurse shall be hired by the County until all senior Nurses on layoff (of six 14
(6) months or less) have been offered reemployment in the positions available.15
16
16.10 Section 10 – A written recall notice. 17
A written recall notice shall be sent by registered or certified mail to the last 18
address which the Nurse has given the Human Resources Department of the 19
County. It is responsibility of the Nurse to provide Human Resources with a 20
current address.  21
22
16.11 Section 11 – Recall. 23
Nurses recalled from layoff shall not forfeit previously accumulated seniority, 24
PTO accrual rate, sick leave, educational benefits, or holidays up to the date of 25
layoff.  26
A Nurse may choose to be paid for accrued PTO, in accordance with Article 6, 27
Section 6 of this agreement, on the date of layoff or at the termination of 28
seniority. No benefits, except as provided under COBRA, shall accrue during a 29
layoff. 30

ARTCLE 17. – DISCIPLINE AND DISCHARGE
17.1 Section 1 – Causes for Discipline. 1
An employee who has successfully completed the probationary period as defined 2
in this Agreement, shall not be disciplined, or discharged without just cause. 3
Disciplinary action shall be accomplished in a manner which affords the 4
employee the most protection possible from embarrassment before other 5
employees or the public. Dependent upon the nature of the incident(s) giving rise 6
to such disciplinary action or measure, disciplinary action or measure shall be 7
progressive in nature, commensurate with the offense. Discipline shall include 8
the following steps: oral reprimand, written reprimand, suspension, and/or 9
discharge. More serious offenses may warrant a suspension or discharge at the 10
first occurrence. 11
 12
The employee and his/her Association representative, unless the employee 13
requests otherwise, will be immediately notified in writing that the employee has 14
been issued a disciplinary action. Such notification shall state in detail the nature 15
of the alleged offense and shall include dates, locations, and other corroborative 16
details. 17

The Association shall have the right to take up an action of suspension and/or 18
discharge in accordance with the Grievance Procedure provided the Nurse has 19
successfully completed the probationary employment period. 20
An employee found to be unjustly suspended or discharged shall be reinstated 21
with full compensation for all lost time and with full restoration of all other rights 22
and conditions of employment as determined by the arbitrator or neutral who so 23
found the employee unjustly disciplined. 24
 25
17.2 Section 2 – Personnel Records. 26
Pursuant to ORS 652.750, at the request of the Nurse, the employer shall 27
provide reasonable opportunity for the Nurse to inspect, in the appropriate 28
County office, the Nurse’s personnel records which are used or have been used 29
to determine the Nurse’s qualification for employment, promotion, additional 30
compensation, employment termination, or other disciplinary action. At the 31
request of the Nurse, the employer shall furnish a certified copy of such records. 32

The County may make a reasonable charge, as provided by ORS and County 1
policy, for any copies furnished to the Nurse. 2
17.3 Section 3 – Performance Evaluation. 4
Performance evaluations shall be done at least annually. The Nurse shall be 5
provided with a copy of the evaluation at the time of the evaluation. 6
