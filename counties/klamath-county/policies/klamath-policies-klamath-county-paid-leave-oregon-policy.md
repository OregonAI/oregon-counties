Klamath County Paid Leave Oregon Equivalent Plan
Klamath County is committed to providing every employee the time and support they need to care for themselves and their
loved ones when life’s important moments affect their health and safety. The County supports equitable access to paid leave
for all workers. Klamath County provides a self-insured Paid Leave Plan that exceeds the benefits provided by the state plan.
As part of this plan, employees will receive up to 12 weeks of Paid Leave per benefit year (up to 14 weeks for pregnancy -
related medical leave) without reduction to their salary. In addition, there will be no deductions withheld from employee
paychecks to provide this leave. Eligible employees may apply for benefits beginning September 3, 2023.
A. Eligible Employees
All full-time, part-time, on-call, temporary, and seasonal County employees are eligible for paid leave benefits after 30 days
of continuous employment. The 30- day window is waived if the employee has terminated employment that was eligible to
receive benefits under a state-approved Paid Leave Plan and earned at least $1,000 in the previous 52 weeks (or 53 weeks
if applicable). The County may contact the Oregon Employment Department to determine employee eligibility.
This policy does not apply to independent contractors, state or federal work training participants, work study participants,
volunteers, and employees contracted through a temporary staffing agency.  Employees currently receiving workers’
compensation or unemployment benefits are ineligible for Paid Leave.
B. Qualifying Events
The following are qualifying events for the Paid Leave plan:
• Family leave
o Birth of a child (in the first 12 months of birth, through adoption, or foster care placement); an additional leave
(limited to two weeks) related to pregnancy issues for a birth parent.
o Bonding with a child (in the first 12 months of birth, through adoption, or foster care placement).
o To care for a family member with a serious health condition. Family members include spouses and domestic
partners, children, parents, siblings or stepsiblings, grandparents, grandchildren, and any individual related
by blood or affinity whose relationship is equivalent to family.
o To recover from physical and mental impacts due to miscarriage or stillbirth.
• Medical leave
o To care for yourself when you have a serious health condition.
• Safe leave
o For survivors of sexual assaults, domestic violence, harassment, or stalking , or to obtain legal or law
enforcement assistance, seek medical treatment to recover from injuries, obtai n counseling or support
services, to relocate or take other steps to secure the health and safety of themselves or their dependent
child(ren).
C. Employee Payments
If a claim is approved, the employee will continue to receive their full salary as if the employee were still working, for up to 12
weeks (14 weeks in cases of pregnancy -related medical leave) or the expected duration of the leave, if less than 12 weeks .
This will follow the normal payroll dates of the 10 th and 25th of each month. If the 10 th or 25th falls on a weekend or holiday,
the payment will be received on the preceding business day.
D. Application Process
The employee will need to apply through the Human Resources Department for Paid Leave benefits. The application will be
the same as or similar to the state plan. Human Resources will determine eligibility based on the application received from
the employee. Employee shall be provided a decision in writing, either in hard copy or electronically. This will include the
amount of leave approved and information regarding the continuance of the employee’s salary and benefits.
If the claim is denied, the reason for the denial and explanation of the employee’s right to appeal will be included in the
decision notice.
The application must include the following documentation depending on the type of leave requested:
For Family leave, documentation may be required to show the birth, adoption or placement of a child such as:
• A birth certificate, report of birth abroad, hospital admission form associated with delivery;
• A court order verifying placement, a letter signed by the foster, adoptive parents, family lawyer or social worker,
document from a foster care agency, both must include claimant as parent or guardian of the child, child’s first and
last name, date of child’s birth or placement;
• For limitations related to pregnancy, documentation may be required to show that the employee birthed a child, but
not to verify related limitations.
For a family member experiencing a serious health condition, documentation may require a description of the family
relationship, the healthcare provider’s first and last name, type of medical practice, contact information, patient’s first and last
name, and date on which serious health condition occurred and estimation of the duration of the condition.
For Medical leave, documentation may require the healthcare provider’s first and last name, type of medical practice, contact
information, patient’s first and last name, and date on which serious health condition occurred and estimation of the duration
of the condition.
For Safe l eave, documentation may be requested, but the County will accept self -attestation when the employee cannot
provide documentation for good cause. Types of documentation accepted include a poli ce report showing claimant or
claimant’s child was a victim of domestic violence, harassment, sexual assault or stalking; protective order or other evidence
that the claimant or claimant’s child appeared in or prepared for civil, criminal, or administrative proceeding; or documentation
from an attorney, law enforcement officer, mental health professional, healthcare provider, etc., stating that the claimant or
claimant’s child was undergoing treatment or counseling.
E. Appeal Process
If the employee’s application for Paid Leave is denied by the Human Resources Department, the employee may file an appeal
within 60 calendar days of receipt of initial decision. The appeal will be elevated to the Board of County Commissioners. After
review and determination by a designated board member, the board member will render a decision. If the employee and the
County are unable to resolve an appeal, the employee may request dispute resolution assistance through the Oregon
Employment Department. The Department will review t he dispute and provide the employee and employer with an advisory
decision.
F. Using Leave
Employees will receive up to 12 weeks of Paid Leave per benefit year  (14 weeks in instances of pregnancy -related medical
leave). Paid Leave must be used in single work day or work week  increments and can be taken in consecutive or non-
consecutive periods. Employees may not use Paid Leave on an hourly basis.

Employees who have a foreseeable leave must provide at least a 30-day notice prior to the start of leave. For unforeseeable
leave, employees must provide verbal notice within 24 hours and written notice within 3 days of the start of leave.

The benefit year starts on the Sunday before the period of leave and lasts for 52 weeks. An employee who has started a
benefit year under previous coverage continues the same benefit year under the equivalent plan until it is complete.  The
County can request information on previously established benefit years from the Oregon Employment Department.

G. Employee Health Insurance Continuation
During Paid Leave, Klamath County will continue to pay its portion of health insurance premiums, and the employee must
continue to pay their portion of the premiums, if applicable.

H. Job Protection
Employees who have been employed by the County for more than 90 days prior to the start of leave shall be entitled to return
to their position or an equivalent position if their original position no longer exists. This will include the same benefits, pay and
other terms and conditions of employment. The use of Paid Leave will not impact an employee’s seniority or length of service.

J. Cost
The County shall provide this benefit to all eligible employees at no cost to the employee.

K. Accruals
While on Paid Leave, employees will not accrue any other paid time off (e.g., sick, vacation, PTO, etc). While receiving Paid
Leave benefits, employees do not utilize their own accrual banks.  The employee will receive holiday pay if a holiday occurs
during the leave. The holiday will not be included in the total Paid Leave used.

L. FMLA/OFLA Coordination
When applicable, FMLA and OFLA benefits will run concurrently with Paid Leave. There may be certain circumstances in
which someone applying for Paid Leave does not qualify for FMLA or OFLA. In those cases, the employee would continue to
have the FMLA or OFLA benefit for any other FMLA or OFLA qualifying event. Please refer to the FMLA policy for these
guidelines.

M. Retaliation or Discrimination
Any employee utilizing Paid Leave is free from retaliation and/or discrimination. Any person feeling that they have been
retaliated or discriminated against for utilizing Paid Leave should contact the HR Director.

N. Simultaneous Coverage
If an employee qualifies for Paid Leave through another employer, the employee must apply for benefit s through each plan
separately. The County may ask the employee whether they have additional paid leave coverage, but may not require that
they provide details on other employers or plans. The County may request from the Oregon Employment Department whether
a benefit year was started and whether the leave was exhausted.

If an employee is receiving Paid Leave through the County or another employer, the employee may not work at any other
employer on a day when receiving Paid Leave benefits.

O. Notice to Employees
The County will provide notice to employees regarding the coverage available under this plan. This will be provided at the
time of hire and each time there is a policy or procedure change. In addition, a notice shall be displayed in each building or
worksite in a conspicuous area with other required workplace postings. Remote workers shall be notified via hand delivery or
electronic delivery.

This notice shall include the following information:
• Benefits available under the plan;
• Process for filing a claim;
• Process for appealing a claim;
• Job protections and continuation of health benefits;
• Prohibition of discrimination and retaliation related to the Paid Leave and the right of an employee to bring civil action;
• Confidentiality of any health information provided.

P. Reporting Requirements
Klamath County will file combined payroll reports and provide Paid Leave Oregon wage information for all employees. The
County will submit an annual report on benefits usage, on or before January 31 st or along with applicat ion for re-approval.
This report shall include the following information:
• Number of benefit applications received each quarter and the qualifying purposes;
• Number of benefit applications approved each quarter, qualifying purposes, and total amount of leave;
• Number of benefit applications denied each quarter, qualifying purposes, the number of appeals/disputes made on
denials, and the outcome of the appeals/disputes.

If requested from the Oregon Employment Department, the County shall provide the following information within 10 calendar
days from the date of the request:
• If a benefit year was established;
• The start and end date of the established benefit year;
• Total amount of benefits paid in the benefit year;
• The duration of leave remaining in the benefit year.

The County shall maintain all required reports, information, reporting requirements, and records relating to the equivalent
plan, including employee leave applications and benefits paid or denied for six years.
