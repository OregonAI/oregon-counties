§ 100.01 DEFINITIONS.
A. “Board” means the Umatilla County
Board of Commissioners.
B. “Disease organisms” means those disease
organisms as identified and defined by the Oregon
Department of Agriculture, including, but not
limited to: Pseudomonas blight ( Pseudomonas
syringae ), Coryneum blight ( Coryneum
carpophilum ), Peach leaf curl ( Taphrina
deformans), Fire blight ( Erwina amylovora ),
Apple scab ( Venturia inaequalis), and Pear scab
(Venturia pirina Aderh).
C. “Ground fall fruit” means fruit that has
fallen to the ground.
D. “Host tree” or “hos t plant” means Pear
(Pyrus sp.), Quince (Cydonia sp.), Apple (Malus
sp.), Peach ( Prunus sp .), Apricot ( Prunus sp .),
Cherry (Prunus sp .), Hawthorn ( Crataegus sp.),
Grape Vines ( Vitis spp. ) and may include other
hosts of disease organisms or pests in accordance
with policies established by the Oregon
Department of Agriculture.
E. “Insect-pest” means those insects as
identified and defined by the Oregon Department
of Agriculture, including, but not limited to
Codling Moth (Carpocapsa pomonella), Oriental
Fruit Moth ( Grapholita molesta ), Pear Psylla
(Psylla pyricola ), San Jose Scale ( Aspidiotus
perniciousus ), Shot Hole Borer ( Scolytus
rugulosus), Apple Maggot (Phagoletis pomonella),
Cherry fruit fly (Rhagoletis indifferens), and peach
Twig Borer (Anarsia linetella).
F. “Orchardist” means the owner of property
on which a host tree or plant is located, as well as
tenants, licensees, officers, agents, or other persons
responsible for the management of the property or
the orchard.
G.  “Orchard” means one or more host trees
or plants.
H.  “Organic” means the techniques and
processes or raising and producing agricultural
products in compliance with the United States
Department of Agriculture National Organic
Program.
I. “Pesticide” includes:
(1) ‘Defoliant’ which means any
substance or mixture of substances intended for
causing the leaves or foliage to drop from a plant
with or without causing abscission;
(2) ‘Desiccant’ which means any
substance or mixture of substances intended for
artificially accelerating the drying of plant tissue;
(3) ‘Fungicide’ which means any
substance or mixture of substances intended for
preventing, destroying, repelling or mitigating any
fungus;
(4) ‘Herbicide’ which means any
9-2
substance or mixture of substances intended for
preventing, destroying, repelling or mitigating any
weed;
(5) ‘Insecticide’ which means any
substances intended for preventing, destroying,
repelling or mitigating any insects which may be
present in any environment whatsoever;
(6) ‘Nematicide’ which means any
substance or mixture of substances intended for
preventing, destroying, repelling or mitigating
nematodes;
(7) ‘Plant regulator’ which means any
substance or mixture of substances intended,
through physiological action, to accelerate or
retard the rate of growth or rate of maturation or to
otherwise alter the behavior of ornamental or crop
plants or the produce thereof, but shall not include
substances to the extent that they are intended as
plant nutrients, trace elements, nutritional
chemicals, plant inoculants or soil amendments;
and
(8) Any substance, or mixture of
substances intended to be used for defoliating
plants or for preventing, destroying, repelling or
mitigating all insects, plant fungi, weeds, rodents,
predatory animals or any other form of plant or
animal life which is, or which the county may
declare to be a present, which may infest or be
detrimental to vegetation, humans, animals or be
present in any environment thereof.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.02 CONTROL OFFICER.
2.1 The Board may appoint Control Officers,
who shall be authorized to carry out the provisions
of this chapter on behalf of the County.
2.2 The Board, or its designee, shall supervise
the Control Officers.
2.3 A Control Officer under this chapter shall
have the same duties and authority as a County
Horticultural Inspector as defined and provided by
ORS Chapter 570.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.03 DISEASE AND PEST CONTROL.
A. Except as otherwise provided in sections
100.04 and 100.09, all host trees and plants must
be treated with appropriate and adequate
pesticides, as recommended by the Oregon
Department of Agricultural, to prevent infestation
by disease organisms and insect pests.
   B.  The application of pesticides under this
section shall conform with all federal and state
regulations, including but not limited to all buffer
restrictions.
   C.  The application of pesticides under this
section shall conform with all applicable
manufacturer’s specifications.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.04 ORGANIC CULTIVATION;
REGISTRATION; CONTROL METHODS.
A .   Orchardists employing organic
agricultural methods shall, without a fee, register
their host trees and plants as a “pesticide-free
orchard” with the Umatilla County Department of
Public Works.
   B.  If an insect pest or disease organism is
found within a registered “pesticide-free orchard”
the orchardist, in additional to measures set forth in
section 100.05, shall:
(1)  Clear all undergrowth and objects
from beneath host trees and plants so that all
ground fall fruit will be clearly visible;
(2)  Remove and properly dispose of all
9-3
ground fall fruit at least twice a week until
commercial harvest is complete;
(3) Harvest all fruit as soon as possible
or as otherwise directed by the County;
(4)  Strip all trees, including pollinators,
of fruit by the end of commercial harvest; and
(5) Continue to apply these measures for
a 24 month period after the insect pest or disease
organism is discovered.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.05   TREATMENT OF DISEASED OR
INFESTED HOST PLANTS AND TREES.
If disease organisms or insect pests are found
in an orchard, the orchardist, at the direction of the
County, shall:
(1) Remove any or all host trees and plants
within the orchard, including their complete root
structures;
(2) Cut down any and all host trees and plants
and treat the remaining stump and root systems
with adequate and appropriate chemicals to
prevent the spread of disease or pests;
(3) Strip and dispose of all fruit from host
trees or plants in compliance with section 6; or,
(4)  Employ any other reasonable disease and
pest control measures acceptable to the County.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.06 DISPOSAL OF FRUIT.
Fruit disposed of in accordance with this
chapter shall be:
(1) Processed for juice or sauce using
approved cold treatment methods; or,
(2) Buried under procedures approved by
Oregon Department of Agricultural.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.07 IMPLIED CONSENT; RIGHT OF
ACCESS.
A.  By keeping a host tree or plant, an
orchardist voluntarily and impliedly consents to
permit the County and its Control Officers to
inspect such trees or plants, and to abate any
nuisance under section 100.08, including
permission to access the orchardist’s property.
B.  As provided by ORS 570.020, a Control
Officer has the authority to enter upon or into any
premises for purpose of inspection.
C.  County officials entering upon property
for the purpose of enforcing this chapter, shall not
be subject to civil or criminal liability for trespass.
D.  All inspections and abatements conducted
under this chapter shall take place at reasonable
times.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.08 NUISANCE AND ABATEMENT.
Host trees, host plants, orchards and fruit kept
or disposed of in violation of this chapter shall be
considered to be a public nuisance, and may be
abated at the discretion of the County by
employing any treatment, removal, disposal or
other control method set forth in this chapter,
Umatilla County Code of Ordinances, and Oregon
State Statutes.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.09 EXEMPTIONS.
A. Scientific experiments conducted under the
authority of Oregon State University, and
9-4
employing accepted scientific methods and
controls, are exempt from the provisions of this
chapter.
B.  Scientists conducting experiments under
this section shall take all reasonable steps to limit
any threat, harm, or inconvenience to other
orchardists or their crops.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
§ 100.10 ENFORCEMENT.
A.  If the Orchardist fails to take action
required under this chapter, then enforcement
action may be taken as provided by:
(1) Oregon State Law, including judicial
action;
(2) Chapter 38 of the Umatilla County
Code of Ordinances; and
(3) Abatement by Umatilla County or
Oregon State Department of Agriculture.
B. Judicial enforcement of a violation will be
conducted as provided by ORS Chapter 153.
   C. A violation of this chapter shall constitute
a Class A violation.
(Ord. 2007-11, passed 12-19-2007)
