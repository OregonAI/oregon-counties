 ### Title 1 - GENERAL PROVISIONS

 ### Chapter 1.01 - CODE ADOPTION

 ### 1.01.010 - Findings.

A.
The board of county commissioners of Washington County, Oregon, finds that Book Publishing
Company of Seattle, Washington, has compiled, edited and published a codification
of general ordinances of Washington County, Oregon; said codified book filed with
the original of the ordinance codified in this chapter with the ex-officio county
clerk of the county and marked Exhibit "A" to the ordinance codified in this chapter
and incorporated by reference herein.
B.
The board finds it necessary in the public interest to adopt the code to facilitate
the public's access to county laws as well as aid the pleading and enforcement of
particular county laws.
(Ord. 314 § 1, 1986)

 ### 1.01.020 - Adoption.

There is adopted the "Washington County Code," as compiled and published by Book Publishing
Company, Seattle, Washington, a copy of which is on file with the original of the
ordinance codified in this chapter with the ex-officio county clerk of the county,
marked Exhibit "A" to the ordinance codified in this chapter and incorporated by reference
herein.
(Ord. 314 § 2, 1986)

 ### 1.01.030 - Title—Citation—Reference.

This code shall be known as the "Washington County Code" and it shall be sufficient
to refer to the code as the "Washington County Code" in any prosecution for the violation
of any provision thereof or in any legal proceeding. It shall be sufficient to designate
any ordinance adding to, amending, correcting or repealing all or any part or portion
thereof as "Washington County Code." Further reference may be had to the titles, chapters,
sections and subsections of the "Washington County Code" and such references shall
apply to that numbered title, chapter, section or subsection as it appears in the
code.
(Ord. 314 § 3, 1986)

 ### 1.01.040 - Reference applies to all amendments.

Whenever a reference is made to this code as the "Washington County Code" or to any
portion thereof, or to any ordinance of the county, codified herein, the reference
shall apply to all amendments, corrections and additions heretofore, now or hereafter
made.
(Ord. 314 § 4, 1986)

 ### 1.01.050 - Title, chapter and section headings.

Title, chapter and section headings contained herein shall not be deemed to govern,
limit, modify or in any manner affect the scope, meaning or intent of the provisions
of any title, chapter or section hereof.
(Ord. 314 § 5, 1986)

 ### 1.01.060 - Reference to specific ordinances.

The provisions of this code shall not in any manner affect matters of record which
refer to, or are otherwise connected with ordinances which are therein specifically
designated by number or otherwise and which are included within the code, but such
reference shall be construed to apply to the corresponding provisions contained within
this code.
(Ord. 314 § 6, 1986)

 ### 1.01.070 - Effect of code on past actions and obligations.

Neither the adoption of this code nor the repeal or amendments of any ordinance or
part or portion of any ordinance of the county shall in any manner affect the prosecution
for violations of ordinances, which violations were committed prior to the effective
date of the ordinance codified in this chapter, nor be construed as a waiver of any
license, fee, or penalty at such effective date due and unpaid under such ordinances,
nor be construed as affecting any of the provisions of such ordinances relating to
the collection of any such license, fee, or penalty, or the penal provisions applicable
to any violation thereof, nor to affect the validity of any bond or cash deposit in
lieu thereof required to be posted, filed, or deposited pursuant to any ordinance
and all rights and obligations thereunder appertaining shall continue in full force
and effect.
(Ord. 314 § 7, 1986)

 ### 1.01.080 - Severability.

If any section, subsection, sentence, clause or phrase of this code is for any reason
held to be invalid or unconstitutional, such decision shall not affect the validity
of the remaining portions of this code. If for any reason this code should be declared
invalid or unconstitutional, then the original ordinance or ordinances shall be in
full force and effect.
(Ord. 314 § 8, 1986)

 ### 1.01.090 - Repeal of previous ordinances.

Those ordinances previously adopted by the board of county commissioners of the county
and which are codified in the Washington County Code are repealed and replaced by
the Washington County Code. All ordinances, not so codified, shall remain in full
force and effect. Any ordinance that was repealed by a codified ordinance shall not
be revived.
(Ord. 314 § 9, 1986)

 ### Chapter 1.04 - GENERAL PROVISIONS

 ### 1.04.010 - Definitions.

The following words and phrases, whenever used in the ordinances enacted by the board
of county commissioners for Washington County, Oregon, shall be construed as defined
in this section unless from the context a different meaning is intended or unless
a different meaning is specifically defined and more particularly directed to the
use of such words or phrases:
A.
"Board" means the board of county commissioners for Washington County, Oregon.
B.
"Chairman" means the chairman of the board of county commissioners for Washington
County, Oregon.
C.
"Chamber" or any reference to where the board of county commissioners regularly conduct
meetings means Room 402 of the Washington County Administration Building, 150 N. First
Avenue, Hillsboro, Oregon.
D.
"Charter" means the home rule charter for Washington County, Oregon.
E.
"County" means Washington County, Oregon.
F.
"County commissioners" means the board of county commissioners for Washington County,
Oregon.
G.
"Law" denotes applicable federal law, the Constitution, statutes and rules of the
state of Oregon, the ordinances of Washington County, Oregon, and any and all rules
and regulations which may be promulgated thereunder.
H.
"May" is permissible.
I.
"Meeting" means a regular or special meeting of the board.
J.
"Month" means a calendar month.
K.
"Must" and "shall" are each mandatory.
L.
"Owner" applied to a building or land, includes any part owner, joint owner, tenant
in common, joint tenant, tenant by the entirety, of the whole or part of such building
or land.
M.
"Person" includes a natural person, joint venture, joint stock company, partnership,
association, club, company, corporation, business, trust, organization, or the manager,
lessee, agent, servant, officer or employee of any of them.
N.
"Personal property" includes money, goods, chattels, things in action and evidences
of debt.
O.
"Preceding" and "following" mean next before and next after, respectively.
P.
"Property" includes real and personal property.
Q.
"Real property" includes lands, tenements and hereditaments.
R.
"State" means the state of Oregon.
S.
"Written" includes printed, typewritten, mimeographed, multigraphed, or otherwise
reproduced in permanent visible form.
T.
"Year" means a calendar year.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 1), 1985)

 ### 1.04.020 - Title of office.

Use of the title of any officer, employee, department, division, board or commission
means that officer, employee, department, division, board or commission of the county.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 2), 1985)

 ### 1.04.030 - Interpretation of language.

All words and phrases shall be construed according to the common and approved usage
of the language, but technical words and phrases and such others as may have acquired
a peculiar and appropriate meaning in the law shall be construed and understood according
to such peculiar and appropriate meaning.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 3), 1985)

 ### 1.04.040 - Grammatical interpretation.

A.
Each gender includes the masculine, feminine and neuter genders.
B.
The singular includes the plural and the plural includes the singular.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 4), 1985)

 ### 1.04.050 - Computation of time.

Except when otherwise provided, the time within which an act is required to be done
shall be computed by excluding the first day and including the last day, unless the
last day falls upon any legal holiday or on Saturday, in which case the last day is
also excluded.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 5), 1985)

 ### 1.04.060 - Effect of amendment of ordinance adopted by reference.

When one ordinance refers to another, either by general or by specific reference or
designation, the reference shall extend to and include, in addition to the ordinance
to which reference was made, amendments thereto and ordinances enacted expressly in
lieu thereof unless a contrary intent is expressed specifically or unless the amendment
to, or ordinance enacted in lieu of, the ordinance referred to is substantially different
in the nature of its essential provisions from what the ordinance to which reference
was made was when the ordinance making the reference was enacted.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 7), 1985)

 ### 1.04.070 - Effect of repeal of validating or curative ordinance.

The repeal of a validating or curative ordinance shall not affect any validation or
cure theretofore accomplished.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 8), 1985)

 ### 1.04.080 - Effect of repeal of repealing ordinance.

Whenever an ordinance which repealed a former ordinance, either expressly or by implication,
is repealed, the former ordinance shall not thereby be reviewed unless it is expressly
so provided.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 9), 1985)

 ### 1.04.090 - Severability.

In the enactment of any ordinance, if any part is held unconstitutional or invalid,
the remaining parts shall remain in force unless:
A.
The ordinance provides otherwise;
B.
The remaining parts are so essentially and inseparably connected with and dependent
upon the unconstitutional or invalid part that it is apparent that the remaining parts
would not have been enacted without the unconstitutional part; or
C.
The remaining parts, standing alone, are incomplete and incapable of being executed
in accordance with the legislative intent.
(Ord. 302 § 2(Exhibit A § 6), 1985)

 ### Chapter 1.08 - UNIFORM CITATION PROCEDURES

 ### 1.08.005 - Short title and purpose.

To provide uniform procedures to effectively enforce compliance with county ordinances,
the board of commissioners enacts the provisions set out in this chapter which may
be cited as the uniform citation ordinance.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.010 - Definitions.

As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
A.
"Board" means the Washington County board of commissioners.
B.
"Citing agency" or "administering agency" means an administrative department of Washington
County authorized to issue citations for violations of the code.
C.
"Code" or "county code" means the Washington County Code or the Washington County
community development code or both.
D.
"Complainant" means a private person or county officer authorized to issue a citation.
E.
"County" means Washington County, Oregon.
F.
"County offense" or "offense" means an infraction of the county code.
G.
"County officer" means a county code enforcement officer, an employee whose job description
includes enforcement authority, including the county counsel or his designee, and
any peace officer.
H.
"Court" or "Washington County court" includes the circuit and district courts of the
state of Oregon for Washington County, the Washington County justice court or an administrative
hearings officer for Washington County.
I.
"Hearing" means a trial or an administrative hearing.
J.
"Person" means a natural person or a company, firm, business, association, entity
or corporation.
K.
"Vehicle" means every device in, upon, or by which any person or property is or may
be transported or drawn.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.020 - Minimum bail schedule.

The board, by resolution and order, shall promulgate a minimum bail schedule for county
offenses.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.030 - Enforcement and issuance of citation.

A.
Any county officer may enforce offenses of the code.
B.
A county officer may issue a citation for any county offense committed in his or her
presence, or which the officer personally observes.
C.
A private person may issue a citation for any county offense committed in his or her
presence, or which the person personally observes.
D.
The prosecution of all citations shall be within the discretion and judgment of the
office of county counsel, and shall conform to the procedures for certification and
service as required by this chapter.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.040 - County counsel as prosecutor.

The office of county counsel is designated as the prosecutor for any county offense.
County counsel or a county officer may appear on behalf of the county at a hearing
on any citation issued pursuant to this chapter. The office of county counsel and
any county officer prosecuting the citations under this chapter shall be immune from
any civil liability arising from the prosecution of a citation, provided the prosecution
is undertaken subject to a good faith belief that it is warranted.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.050 - Procedures in general.

A.
The hearing on any offense shall be by the court without a jury.
B.
The hearing on any offense shall not commence until the expiration of seven days from
the date of service, unless waived by the person cited.
C.
The county shall have the burden of proving the alleged offense by a preponderance
of the evidence.
D.
The prehearing discovery rules for county offenses shall be the same as those for
similar offenses under state law.
E.
The person cited shall not be required to be a witness in the hearing on a county
offense. The person cited is not entitled to appointed defense counsel for any citation
under the code.
F.
Any procedural or substantive matter not addressed by this chapter shall proceed or
be conducted in accordance with the procedures for similar offenses under state law,
however, the procedural and substantive matters set forth in this chapter shall prevail
over any conflict with state law.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.060 - Form and content of uniform citation.

A.
A uniform citation, conforming to the requirements of this chapter, may be used for
county offenses. The form and wording of the citation shall be as the county designates.
B.
The uniform citation shall consist of at least four parts. Additional parts may be
inserted for administrative use. The required parts are:
1.
Complaint;
2.
County counsel's record of offense;
3.
Citing agency's record of offense; and
4.
Summons.
C.
Each of the parts shall contain the following information or blanks in which such
information shall be entered:
1.
Name of the court and the citation number;
2.
Name of the person cited;
3.
Section of the code violated;
4.
Brief description of the offense for which the person is cited in such a manner as
can be readily understood by a person making a reasonable effort;
5.
The date, time and place at which the offense occurred;
6.
The date on which the citation was issued and the name of the complainant;
7.
The time and place where the person cited is to appear in court;
8.
The bail, if any, fixed for the violation;
9.
A notice to the person cited that a complaint will be filed with the court based on
the offense.
D.
Each of the parts shall also contain such identifying and additional information as
may be necessary or appropriate for the purposes of the administering agencies.
E.
The complaint shall contain a form of certificate by the complainant to the effect
that he or she certifies, under penalties described in Section 1.08.170 that he or she has reasonable grounds to believe, and does believe, that the person
cited committed the offense contrary to the county code or the rules or regulations
adopted thereto. The certification, if made by a county officer, need not be made
before any other person. Any private person shall certify before a county officer.
F.
The complaint shall contain a statement that a monetary judgment may be entered against
the person up to the maximum amount of fines, assessments, restitution and other costs
allowed by the code for the offense if the person fails to comply with Section 1.08.090A.
G.
Any error in entering information into the blanks in the citation form, when determined
by the court to be nonprejudicial to the cited person's defense, may be corrected
at the hearing or prior to the hearing with notice being given to the person cited.
Nothing prohibits the court from amending the citation in its discretion.
H.
If the complaint does not conform to the requirements of this section, except as provided
in subsection G of this section, it shall be set aside by the court only upon motion
of the person cited before plea or hearing and a determination that the error is prejudicial
to cited persons defense. Failure to move the court to set aside the citation for
nonconformance to the requirements of this section before the commencement of the
hearing shall constitute a waiver, and will be an absolute bar to raising this issue
at the hearing.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.070 - Exception for parking violation summons and complaint.

A.
Notwithstanding Section 1.08.060 (C)(2), no summons or complaint for a county offense in which the offense involves
the parking of a vehicle, including a failure to display a parking permit, shall be
deemed insufficient because it does not include the name of the person who committed
the offense. If the owner or operator of the vehicle cannot be determined by the county
officer at the time the citation is issued, such information may be supplied before
or at the time for appearance on the citation.
B.
A rebuttable presumption exists that the registered owner of the vehicle at the time
of the offense was the operator or person in control of the vehicle.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.080 - Service of citation.

A.
A county officer shall cause the summons to be served upon the person cited, and shall
cause the complaint to be delivered to the court.
B.
Acceptable methods of service shall be any of the methods of service described in
the Oregon rules of civil procedure, rule 7 (D) (1995).
C.
If a citation is issued for any offense involving a parked vehicle, including failure
to display a parking permit, service may be made by placing the summons in a secure
location on the offending vehicle.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.090 - Appearance by person cited—Bail—Request for hearing—Statement—Guilty plea.

A.
The person cited shall either appear in court at the time indicated in the summons,
or prior to such time, deliver to the court the summons, together with a check or
money order in the amount of the bail set forth in the summons, together with:
1.
A request for a hearing; or
2.
A statement of matters in explanation or mitigation of the offense cited; or
3.
The executed appearance, waiver of hearing and plea of guilty. A statement in explanation
or mitigation also may be enclosed with the guilty plea.
B.
Notwithstanding subsection A of this section, if the code provision under which a
person is cited requires the person to appear in court for that particular offense,
then the person cited must appear in court at the date and time indicated on the summons.
C.
In any case in which the person cited personally appears in court at the time indicated
in the summons and the person cited desires to plead guilty and the court accepts
the plea, the court shall hear any statement in explanation or mitigation that the
person cited desires to make.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.100 - Effect of statement in explanation or mitigation.

If a person cited submits to the court a guilty plea or a written statement in explanation
or mitigation under Section 1.08.090 (A)(2), the statement constitutes a waiver of hearing and consent to judgment by the
court. The court may declare a forfeiture of all or any part of the bail as determined
by the court on the basis of such statement and any testimony or written statement
of the complainant or other witnesses which may be presented to the court. The court
may impose a fine in excess of the amount of bail up to the maximum penalty authorized.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.110 - Hearing—Date—Notice.

If the person cited requests a hearing, or pursuant to Section 1.08.120 , the court directs that a hearing be held, the court shall fix a date and time for
a hearing and, unless notice is waived, shall at least five days in advance of the
hearing, mail to the person cited a notice of the date and time of the hearing. The
notice shall be placed in the United States mail addressed to the person cited at
his or her last known address with postage prepaid thereon.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.120 - Hearing discretionary with court—Exceptions.

A.
For any offense for which a citation has been issued, the court may direct that a
hearing be held.
B.
The court may proceed to make a determination under any of the following circumstances:
1.
If a hearing is held, either at the request of the person cited or on the court's
own motion, when the court makes a finding on the evidence presented at the hearing;
2.
When the person cited has complied with Section 1.08.090 (A)(2) or (3), when the court makes a finding on the citation, any plea and any evidence
or other material submitted;
3.
When the person cited has not complied with Section 1.08.090A, or otherwise has failed
to appear at the time indicated in the summons or at a subsequent hearing and the
court makes a finding on the complaint and any other evidence the court determines
appropriate.
C.
Upon completion of its determination, the court may enter the appropriate judgment
and, if the determination is one of guilty may do any of the following as part of
the judgment:
1.
Impose a sentence of a fine along with a money judgment for costs, assessments and
restitution;
2.
Direct that the fine, costs, assessments and restitution, if any, be paid out of the
bail deposit.
D.
Notwithstanding any other section of this chapter, if a person cited pleads not guilty
and requests a hearing and fails to appear at the hearing without prior notification
to the court or the office of county counsel, the court may allow the county to present
its case at the scheduled hearing and shall make a determination and enter a judgment
in accordance with Section 1.08.130C.
E.
If a judgment is entered under this section after a person cited has failed to comply
with Section 1.08.090A or otherwise fails to appear, on motion and upon such terms
as are just, the court may relieve a person from the judgment upon a showing that
the failure of the person cited to comply was due to mistake, inadvertence, surprise
or excusable neglect. The motion must be made within a reasonable time, and in no
event more than one year after entry of judgment in the matter.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.130 - Failure to appear—Designated.

A.
A person commits the offense of failing to appear if the person fails to appear at
the date and time set in the summons or fails to appear at any subsequent hearing.
B.
Failure to appear shall be separate from and in addition to the citation for which
the person failed to appear. A citation for failure to appear may be issued by a county
officer.
C.
Upon a finding by the court that the person cited did not make an appearance as required
in this chapter, the person shall be found guilty of failure to appear.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.140 - Arrest warrant—Issuance.

If a person cited fails to comply with the provisions of Section 1.08.090A, or fails
to appear at any time fixed by the court, a warrant for his or her arrest may be issued
by the court without any further complaint or information being filed, in the same
manner as for similar state offenses. A warrant issued by the court may by served
without further endorsement, in any county in this state.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.150 - Arrest warrant—Expiration.

No warrant of arrest may be issued pursuant to Section 1.08.140 after a period of sixty days from the date of the entry of an order declaring a forfeiture
of bail or other security given by the cited person. Unless a warrant has been issued
before the expiration of that period, the order of forfeiture shall be deemed the
final disposition of the case.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.160 - Failure to appear—Alternative prosecution—Penalty.

A.
At the discretion of the office of county counsel a citation for the offense of failure
to appear may be prosecuted as a misdemeanor with the penalty as designated in subsection
C of this section, or a county offense with a penalty as designated in subsection
D of this section.
B.
The prosecution of and procedures applicable to the misdemeanor offense of failure
to appear shall be the same as similar offenses under state law.
C.
The penalty for conviction of the misdemeanor offense of failure to appear shall be
punishable by up to six months in the county jail and a fine of one thousand dollars.
D.
The penalty for conviction of the county offense of failure to appear shall be punishable
by a fine of up to five hundred dollars.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.170 - False certification—Penalty.

Any person who, in connection with the issuance of a citation or the filing of a complaint
for violation of the county code, willfully certifies falsely to the matters set forth
therein commits the offense of false certification. False certification is punishable
by a fine of not more than one thousand dollars.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.180 - Jurisdiction.

Circuit courts, district courts, justice courts or a Washington County administrative
hearings officer shall have concurrent jurisdiction over prosecutions of offenses
under the county code, unless otherwise specified in a particular ordinance.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.190 - Other enforcement procedures not excluded.

The use of the citation procedures set out in this chapter shall not be construed
to prevent the filing of a complaint, in any other lawful form, alleging violation
of the county code, including administrative or civil proceedings for equitable relief
or damages. Nothing herein shall be construed as preempting prosecution under state
law for any conduct constituting an offense or crime under state law.
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.08.200 - Authority of court to order other measures.

A.
The court, in lieu of, or in addition to any fine, assessment and restitution may
order a person convicted of a county offense to undertake any reasonable measure to
correct any condition on his or her property that contributed to the offense.
B.
County counsel may request restitution upon a conviction for the violation of any
ordinance which results in pecuniary damages. County counsel must present evidence
of the nature and amount of pecuniary damages at or before the time for sentencing.
Restitution may be granted by the court under the same conditions as provided in ORS
137.103 through ORS 137.109 (1995).
(Ord. 491 § 2(A) (part), 1997)

 ### 1.12.010 - Establishment and purpose.

(1)
The purpose of this chapter is to provide for effective and efficient enforcement
and fair and impartial adjudication of alleged infractions of the Washington County
Code (WCC). This chapter establishes a procedure to enforce violations of the county
code that are designated as "Civil Infractions."
(2)
This chapter shall be referred to as the "Administrative Enforcement Ordinance."
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.020 - Definitions.

For the purposes of this chapter, the following words and phrases are hereby defined:
Civil infraction means the failure to comply with any provision of the Washington County Code designated
as a civil infraction, or any order, condition, or restriction lawfully imposed by
a hearings officer, as a result of a civil infraction proceeding or compliance hearing.
Enforcement officer means any law enforcement officer, animal services officer, code enforcement officer,
or employee authorized to enforce all or part of the Washington County Code.
Hearings Officer means the hearings officer designated by the Director of a Washington County department
to be a hearings officer in a particular proceeding or group of proceedings.
Defendant means the person or business allegedly responsible for the conduct constituting an
infraction, for curing or remedying the infraction, or paying monetary fines associated
with the infraction. This is the person named on the notice of civil infraction. The
definition of defendant includes, but is not limited to, the following:
(1)
The owner of the property or the owner's manager or agent or other person in control
of the property on behalf of the owner;
(2)
The person occupying or controlling the property including bailer, lessee, tenant
or other person having possession;
(3)
The person or business who is alleged to have committed or authorized the commission
of the infraction or omitted to perform some action required by ordinance and constituting
an infraction;
(4)
The parent, guardian or other person having custody of a person who is under eighteen
years of age and who has committed an act which is a civil infraction or which if
done by an adult would constitute a civil infraction; or
(5)
The person who procures, aids or abets another in the commission of an infraction;
and
(6)
The owner or keeper of an animal alleged to have committed an infraction.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.030 - Hearings Officer.

The director of a Washington County department responsible for enforcement of the
section of the code violated shall designate one or more Hearings Officers to adjudicate
civil infractions under the administrative enforcement process.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.040 - Initiation.

(1)
When an enforcement officer has probable cause to believe that a civil infraction
has occurred, the enforcement officer may issue a notice of a civil infraction to
the alleged defendant.
(2)
All reports or complaints of civil infractions shall be made to the enforcement officer.
(3)
The enforcement officer will review the facts and circumstances surrounding the alleged
infraction which may include witness statements, photographs and video or audio recordings.
(4)
The enforcement officer may refuse to prosecute the matter upon making a determination
that sufficient evidence does not exist to support the allegation or that further
prosecution is not in the best interests of Washington County.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )
Investigation

 ### 1.12.050 - Inspection and right of entry.

Whenever the enforcement officer has probable cause to believe there has been an infraction
of the Washington County Code, the enforcement officer may enter upon a site or structure
for the purpose of investigation subject to the following requirements:
(1)
The enforcement officer will first obtain consent of the owner or person with actual
authority and ability to consent to entry of the premises. A person with actual authority
must be a person age eighteen years or older and capable of consenting to the enforcement
officer's entry on the premises.
(2)
If consent cannot be obtained, the enforcement officer shall obtain an administrative
search warrant prior to entry onto premises.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.060 - Authority to request administrative search warrant.

If an enforcement officer is denied access to a property for the purpose of an investigation
or inspection on the premises, such investigation shall not be conducted without an
administrative search warrant or without such other authority as a court may grant
in an appropriate civil proceeding. An enforcement officer who has probable cause
to believe that a person has committed a civil infraction may seek an administrative
warrant authorizing search and seizure of any of the following:
(1)
Evidence relating to the civil infraction;
(2)
Any animal where there is probable cause to believe that animal is the subject of
a civil infraction;
(3)
Any animal where there is probable cause to believe that animal has been abused or
neglected; or
(4)
Any animal that has bitten a person and is subject to quarantine.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.070 - Procedure to obtain administrative search warrant.

(1)
If an enforcement officer is denied access to a property, he or she shall notify county
counsel, who may then obtain an administrative search warrant or other appropriate
legal order from a court of competent jurisdiction.
(2)
Issuance of Administrative Search Warrants:
(a)
The enforcement officer shall apply to the Justice Court Judge or Circuit Court Judge
for the issuance of an administrative search warrant.
(b)
The administrative search warrant is an order authorizing the enforcement officer
to enter onto private property, inspect, or seize property.
(c)
The administrative search warrant shall be issued only upon cause supported by affidavit.
Cause shall be deemed to exist if there is probable cause that a civil infraction
has occurred or if a statute, regulation, or if an outside ordinance authorizes the
entry, inspection, search, or seizure.
(3)
Applications for Issuance of Administrative Search Warrants; Requirements of Affidavit:
(a)
An application for an administrative search warrant shall be accompanied by a supporting
affidavit particularly describing the following minimum elements:
(i)
The affiant's employment background and experience;
(ii)
The statute or ordinance requiring or authorizing the inspection or abatement;
(iii)
The address or other description of the property or structure to be inspected, searched,
or seized, which is sufficient to identify the property;
(iv)
The purpose for which the inspection is to be made;
(v)
Either a statement that entry has been sought and refused, or facts or circumstances
reasonably showing that the purposes of the inspection or abatement might be frustrated
if entry were sought without an administrative search warrant;
(vi)
A description, with reasonable particularity, of the violations of statute or ordinance
existing, or believed to exist, with respect to the particular property or structure,
or that an inspection is reasonably believed to be necessary in order to determine
or verify whether any such violations exist at the property or structure.
(vii)
Identification of proposed restrictions upon the service of the warrant, including
a request that it be executed on any day of the week between the hours of 8:00 a.m.
and 6:00 p.m., or if there are special circumstances preventing the effective execution
between those hours, that it be executed at any other time of the day or night; and
(viii)
Any information known to the affiant which could indicate that probable cause may
not exist, or which is relevant to the judge's decision to decline to issue the administrative
warrant.
(b)
Applications for administrative search warrants not involving abatements will request
the warrant allow ten days from its date for execution and return to the Justice Court
Judge or Circuit Court Judge by whom it was issued. Applications for administrative
search warrants involving abatements will request the warrant allow fifteen days from
its date for execution and return to the Justice Court Judge or Circuit Court Judge
by whom it was issued.
(4)
Execution of Administrative Search Warrants:
(a)
Except as provided in subsection (b) of this section, in executing an administrative
search warrant, the person authorized by the magistrate to execute the warrant shall,
before entry, make a reasonable effort to present the person's credentials, authority
and purpose to an occupant or person in possession of the property designated in the
warrant and show the occupant or person in possession of the property the warrant
or a copy thereof upon request.
(b)
In executing an administrative search warrant, the person authorized to execute the
warrant may promptly enter the designated property if it is, or is reasonably believed
to be, vacant or unoccupied. Such person need not inform anyone of the person's authority
and purpose, as prescribed in subsection (a) of this section.
(c)
A peace officer may be requested to assist in the execution of the administrative
search warrant. Such peace officer may assist the person authorized to execute the
warrant, including using any reasonable force necessary, to enter the property if
the administrative warrant specifically allows the use of reasonable force to execute
the warrant.
(d)
After serving the warrant, the inspector may make the authorized inspection and/or
seizure.
(e)
If at any time during the inspection the inspector believes their health or safety
is threatened, they will end the inspection, leave the premises by the most direct
route, and contact the inspection supervisor, police, or other appropriate party for
assistance.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.080 - Stop and detain for civil infraction.

(1)
An enforcement officer may stop and detain any person if the officer has reasonable
suspicion that the person has committed a civil infraction. An enforcement officer
may not arrest a person for a civil infraction.
(2)
The detention may only be as long as reasonably necessary to obtain the identity of
the person believed to have committed the infraction and issue a notice of civil infraction
to the person.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )
Notice of Civil Infraction

 ### 1.12.090 - Notice of civil infraction.

(1)
An enforcement officer shall issue the notice of civil infraction, which includes:
(a)
A uniform civil infraction;
(b)
Summons to the responsible party.
(2)
The uniform civil infraction shall contain the following information:
(a)
The name or names of the alleged responsible party;
(b)
A short statement of the facts constituting the alleged infraction;
(c)
The date, time, and place of the alleged civil infraction;
(d)
The code provision the defendant has allegedly violated;
(e)
The relief requested which may include a monetary civil penalty or curative action
sought;
(f)
A signed certification that the enforcement officer has probable cause to believe
that the responsible party committed the civil infraction; and
(g)
Any additional information as necessary and appropriate for the purposes of giving
notice or administering civil infractions.
(3)
The summons will inform the responsible party that the notice of civil infraction
will be filed with the hearings officer and that the responsible party has the following
options:
(a)
Request a hearing. Appear in front of the hearings officer and request a hearing.
The summons will provide the time and place in which the responsible party shall appear.
(b)
Admit committing the civil infraction, waive a hearing, and submit a check or money
order for the monetary penalty, if any, on the Uniform Civil Infraction with a written
statement of mitigating circumstances and any proposed alternative civil penalty.
The hearings officer, after considering the written statement and any other evidence
presented, will order the responsible party to comply with all or part of the relief
requested.
(c)
Admit or plead no contest to the alleged civil infraction and comply with the relief
requested.
(d)
No action taken will constitute an admission that the allegations in the civil infraction
are true and imply consent to any civil penalty or curative action imposed by the
hearings officer.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.100 - Statute of limitations.

Service must be completed within six months of the civil infraction occurring or within
six months of the county's reasonable discovery of the civil infraction occurring,
whichever is later.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.110 - Service of notice of civil infraction.

Notice of civil infraction must be served by one of the following manners of service:
(1)
Personal service. The notice of civil infraction may be delivered to the Defendant.
(2)
Mail. Service may be made by certified mail, return receipt requested, with service
completed on the date in which the defendant signs a receipt for mailing.
(3)
Substitute Service. Substitute service may be made by delivering true copies of the
notice of civil infraction to the defendant's residence to any person age fourteen
years or older residing in the residence. As soon as reasonably possible, the notice
of civil infraction and a statement of the date, time and place at which substitute
service was made shall be mailed by first class mail to the residence. Service shall
be deemed complete on the date of this mailing.
(4)
Any other manner provided for in the Oregon Rules of Civil Procedure Rule 7D.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.120 - Errors in notice or service of civil infraction.

(1)
Any non-prejudicial error in the notice of civil infraction, including errors transcribing
information into the civil infraction form, may be corrected at or before the hearing
with notice of the correction given to the defendant. A non-prejudicial error in the
notice of civil infraction is not a basis for the civil infraction to be dismissed.
(2)
Any errors in the service of the notice of civil infraction are not a basis for the
civil infraction to be dismissed as long as the defendant was given reasonable and
timely notice of the allegations against the defendant so that defendant was not prejudiced
in defending the civil infraction.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )
Prehearing Matters

 ### 1.12.130 - Discovery.

Upon a written request for discovery by the defendant, the Enforcement Officer (or
county attorney if the defendant is represented) shall provide any books, papers,
documents, photographs, or tangible objects which the Enforcement Officer or county
attorney intends to offer in evidence at the hearing and any material or information
in the possession of the enforcement officer or county attorney which tends to exculpate
the defendant or negate or mitigate the defendant's guilt, or impeach a person whom
the enforcement officer or county attorney intends to call as a witness. The hearings
officer shall not order the disclosure of any additional information or material.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.140 - Subpoena.

If the defendant desires a witness to be ordered to appear by subpoena, the defendant
must request the issuance of a subpoena in writing from the Hearings Officer at least
ten days before the scheduled hearing. The request must include:
(1)
The full name of the witness;
(2)
The witness's address; and
(3)
Witness and mileage fees pursuant to ORS § 44.415.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.145 - Compliance agreement.

(1)
An enforcement officer may enter into a compliance agreement with the defendant alleged
to have violated a provision of the Washington County Code.
(2)
The compliance agreement will impose reasonable conditions on the defendant related
to any of the following: remedying the alleged civil infraction, preventing future
civil infractions from occurring, or providing restitution to a person affected by
the civil infraction.
(3)
The compliance agreement will have a specified time period for satisfying the conditions
of the agreement not to exceed one year, during this time Washington County will stay
prosecution of the civil infraction.
(4)
The compliance agreement shall be in writing and signed by the defendant. The defendant
must consent to the conditions of the compliance agreement.
(5)
If the enforcement officer determines that the defendant has satisfied the conditions
of the compliance agreement by the specified time period, Washington County will dismiss
the alleged civil infraction.
(6)
If the defendant does not satisfy the conditions of the compliance agreement by the
specified time period, or before the specified time period expires it becomes clear
the defendant will not satisfy the conditions of the compliance agreement, Washington
County may proceed with the prosecution of the alleged civil infraction.
(7)
The failure to satisfy any condition of a compliance agreement constitutes a Class
C Civil Infraction and penalties may be imposed in addition to penalties for the alleged
civil infraction giving rise to the compliance agreement.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )
Hearing

 ### 1.12.150 - Notice of hearing.

(1)
The hearings officer shall hold a hearing on any timely challenge to a notice of civil
infraction.
(2)
The hearing date and time shall be sent to the defendant by first-class mail no later
than fourteen days prior to the hearing date.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.160 - Postponements.

For good cause, the hearings officer may postpone the hearing upon request by either
party or on the hearings officer's own motion. The hearings officer may deny a request
for postponement if a postponement will prejudice the interests of either party, if
good cause is not shown, or if the request for postponement was not timely.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.170 - Legal counsel.

The defendant may be represented by counsel, but counsel shall not be provided at
public expense. If legal counsel is to appear, Washington County must be provided
notice of representation at least ten days prior to the hearing date. If notice of
representation is not provided to Washington County at least ten days prior to a hearing,
this is good cause for a postponement.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.180 - Hearing.

(1)
In order to find the defendant guilty of committing the civil infraction, Washington
County must prove the defendant committed the alleged civil infraction by a preponderance
of the evidence.
(2)
Washington County shall proceed first and has the burden of proof.
(3)
Defendant shall proceed second and shall be allowed to call witnesses, present evidence
and present argument.
(4)
After consideration of all the evidence and arguments presented at the hearing, the
hearings officer shall determine whether the civil infraction alleged in the notice
of civil infraction was committed. When the Hearings Officer finds that a civil infraction
was not committed, an order dismissing the civil infraction shall be entered into
Washington County records.
(5)
Upon a finding that the defendant committed the alleged civil infraction, a guilty
finding shall be entered into Washington County records and a penalty assessed according
to WCC 1.12.210.
(6)
The Hearings Officer shall maintain a record of the proceedings. The Hearings Officer
shall enter a written order explaining his or her findings of facts and the civil
penalties ordered.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

 ### 1.12.190 - Evidence.

(1)
The hearing is not subject to Oregon Evidence Code however the following rules apply:
(a)

(b)
All witness testimony shall be under oath and subject to cross examination, and affidavits
and declarations may be admissible subject to the Hearings Officer's discretion.
(c)
Evidence that is cumulative, unfairly prejudicial, not credible, unreliable, or irrelevant
may be excluded.
(2)
The hearings officer will make determinations of the admissibility of evidence.
( Ord. No. 795, § 2(Exh. A), 1-20-2015 )

If the defendant is found guilty of committing the civil infraction the hearings officer

Civil penalties include, but are not limited to:

Monetary penalties according to the schedule of fines on civil infractions;

Restitution for harm caused to victim;

Training, counseling, changes in operations, physical alterations to property;

Revocation or suspension of licenses or certificates or other county authorization

 ### Chapter 1.14 - BUILDING AND LAND USE CODE COMPLIANCE

 ### 1.14.010 - Purpose.

Washington County's first preference is to encourage voluntary compliance. To implement
this philosophy the county will endeavor to develop solutions based on individual
situations and to provide broad based public education. The assumption is that education
of citizens will solve most issues and our initial contacts with affected citizens
will be to take an understanding and helpful approach to resolving potential enforcement
issues.
This code compliance process is for violations of:
1.
The Washington County Community Development Code including any applicable provisions
of the Washington County Comprehensive Framework Plan such as Community Plans and
the Transportation System Plan;
2.
Title 14 of the Washington County Code;
3.
Any conditions, requirements or other remedies of a land use or building permits;
4.
Any conditions, requirements or other aspects of a hearings officer's order; or
5.
Failure to comply with the terms of a voluntary compliance agreement.
This code compliance process is in lieu of the process for violations provided for
under Chapter 153 of the Oregon Revised Statutes and Chapters 1.08 and 1.12 of the
Washington County Code.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.020 - Definitions.

As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
A.
"Extraordinary circumstances" means circumstances preventing a reasonable person from
appearing, such as the death, hospitalization or serious illness of the person or
a family member.
B.
"Parties" means the county, the violator and any other person(s) who appear orally
or in writing and are adversely affected by the outcome of the violation proceeding.
C.
"Person" means all domestic and foreign corporations, associations, syndicates, partnerships
of every kind, joint ventures, societies and individuals capable of owning property
in the county.
D.
"Violation" means failure to comply with:
1.
The Washington County Community Development Code including any applicable provisions
of the Washington County Comprehensive Framework Plan such as Community Plans and
the Transportation System Plan;
2.
Title 14 of the Washington County Code;
3.
Conditions, requirements or other aspects of a land use or building permit;
4.
Conditions, requirements or other aspects of an order of the hearings officer; or
5.
Failure to comply with the terms of a voluntary compliance agreement.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.030 - Citizen complaint.

A.
Any person may file a complaint with the county regarding a violation. The complaint
shall be on a written form as provided by the county. An enforcement action may also
be initiated by a county employee without a written complaint.
B.
All complaints shall be reviewed by staff to determine whether there is sufficient
evidence demonstrating that a violation occurred. Evidence may include but is not
limited to personal observations, pictures, video recordings or other such evidence.
Although circumstantial evidence can be used generally there must be some evidence
that the person witnessed the violation.
C.
If staff or county counsel determine that there is insufficient evidence to support
the complaint, the county may choose not to issue a notice of violation or take any
further action on the complaint.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.040 - Entry and inspection.

A.
A peace officer or any employee authorized by the county to enforce county ordinances,
at all reasonable hours, may enter into any buildings and upon all property to ascertain
whether violations of the county code are present.
B.
The peace officer or any employee authorized by the county to enforce county ordinances
shall first make reasonable efforts to notify the owner or occupant and obtain consent
to enter. In the event consent to enter into buildings or upon property is not granted,
or after reasonable efforts the officer or authorized employee is not able to contact
the owner or occupant, the peace officer or any employee authorized by the county
to enforce county ordinances may seek a warrant as provided in this chapter.
C.
Prior to seeking a warrant, the peace officer or any employee authorized by the county
to enforce county ordinances shall consult with county counsel and obtain approval
and assistance in preparing the affidavit and warrant documents.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.050 - Warrant—Authorization.

Judges authorized by law to issue search warrants upon application by a peace officer
or any employee authorized by the county to enforce county ordinances, may issue a
warrant.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.060 - Warrant—Probable cause.

A.
A warrant shall be issued only upon cause, supported by affidavit and motion which
shall describe with particularity:
1.
The position and authority of the person applying for the inspection warrant;
2.
The statute, ordinance or rule requiring or authorizing the inspection or investigation;
3.
The place, building or property to be inspected or investigated;
4.
The purpose for which the inspection or investigation is to be made including the
basis upon which cause exists to inspect or investigate.
B.
The affidavit shall contain either a statement that consent to enter the building
or property was sought but consent was denied, the property owner did not respond
to the request for consent, or describe the reasonable efforts to contact the owner
or occupant that were unsuccessful.
C.
Cause shall be deemed to exist when there is probable cause to believe that a violation
exists.
D.
Before issuing an inspection warrant, the judge may examine under oath the person
requesting the warrant or any other witness to be satisfied of the existence of the
grounds to grant the inspection warrant.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.070 - Warrant—Contents.

A.
If the judge is satisfied that cause for the inspection or investigation exists and
that the other requirements for granting the warrant are satisfied, the judge shall
issue the warrant, describing with particularity the name or title of the person or
persons authorized to execute the warrant, the buildings or property to be entered
and purpose of the inspection or investigation.
B.
The warrant shall contain a direction that it be executed when the owner or occupant
is present on any day of the week between 8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m., unless the court
finds that:
1.
There is cause to conclude that an urgent risk to the health or safety of a person
or of damage to property exists; or
2.
The owner or occupant is not reasonably able to be located or is avoiding the property.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.080 - Execution of warrant.

A.
In executing a warrant, the person authorized to execute the warrant, before entry,
shall make a reasonable effort under the circumstances to contact the owner or occupant
and arrange for execution of the warrant to present the person's credentials, authority
and purpose to the occupant or person in possession of the building or property and
present the warrant or copy thereof.
B.
A warrant must be executed and returned to the court by whom it was issued within
ten (10) days from its date, unless such court before the expiration of the time,
by endorsement thereon, extends the time for up to an additional five (5) days. After
the expiration of the time prescribed by this subsection, the inspection warrant is
void unless executed.
C.
Reasonable force may be used to execute a warrant. If reasonable force is to be used,
reasonable force shall be undertaken by a certified peace officer. The person authorized
to execute the warrant may be accompanied by a peace officer during the execution
of the warrant to provide for the safety of the participants and to enforce the order
of the court to investigate or inspect the building or property.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.090 - Voluntary compliance agreement.

A.
The county may, in its sole discretion, enter into a written voluntary compliance
agreement with a violator before or after a notice of violation is issued. The agreement
shall include the required corrective action, time limits for compliance and shall
be binding.
B.
The fact that a person enters into a voluntary compliance agreement shall not be considered
an admission of having committed the violation for any purpose.
C.
The county may delay further processing of the violation during the time allowed in
the voluntary compliance agreement for completion of the required corrective action.
In most cases the county will take no further action concerning the violation if all
terms of the voluntary compliance agreement are satisfied, other than steps necessary
to terminate the proceedings against respondent.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.100 - Failure to comply with voluntary compliance agreement.

Failure to comply with any term of the voluntary compliance agreement constitutes
a separate violation. The county may consolidate the hearing on the failure to comply
with the voluntary compliance agreement with the original violation(s). The county
shall provide notice of a hearing on a violation of the voluntary compliance agreement
in substantially the same manner as a notice of violation as provided under Section
1.14.130 of this chapter and include a copy of the original notice of violation.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.110 - Authority of the hearings officer.

A.
The board of county commissioners shall designate one or more hearings officer(s)
responsible for administering the provisions of this chapter.
B.
In addition to administrative and monetary penalties as set forth herein, the hearings
officer may order conditions, restrictions or other remedies reasonably calculated
to address the violation including, but not limited to:
1.
Suspension of any county permit, license or other county authorization otherwise required
to engage in conduct directly related to the violation for a period of time specified
by the hearings officer;
2.
Physical alterations to property and changes in operations;
3.
Requiring that the violation be abated, corrected, removed or otherwise brought into
compliance within such time and manner as directed by the hearings officer; and
4.
Requiring that the person submit credible evidence to the county demonstrating compliance
within a reasonable time as determined by the hearings officer.
C.
The hearings officer may condition suspension of the penalty on the person agreeing
to take reasonable steps to address the violation as required by the hearings officer.
D.
The hearings officer has any and all authority as is necessary to implement the obligations
of this chapter and to otherwise achieve compliance.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.120 - Authority to issue a notice of violation.

A notice of violation may be issued by a peace officer or any employee authorized
by the county to enforce county ordinances.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.130 - Notice of violation.

A.
The notice of violation shall be properly labeled and include text consistent with
the following:
1.
Name of the person to have committed the violation;
2.
Section(s) of the code violated;
3.
Brief description of the violation in such a manner as can be readily understood by
a person making a reasonable effort;
4.
The date, time, and address and/or current county tax map and lot at which the violation
occurred,
5.
The date on which the notice of violation was issued;
6.
A statement that the person may reply to the notice of violation within ten (10) days
of the date of the notice of violation including a written statement: (1) admitting
the violation(s) exist(s) and how it will be mitigated together with payment of any
penalties; (2) denying the violation(s) exist(s) and waiving the right to a hearing;
or (3) denying the violation(s) exist(s) and requesting the right to appear at the
hearing;
7.
A statement that failure to reply to the notice of violation shall be presumed as
a denial of the violation(s) reserving the right to appear unless the person specifies
otherwise;
8.
A statement that failure to appear at the hearing shall constitute an admission that
the violations are true and that the person consents to any monetary penalties, costs
and other remedies as may be imposed by the hearings officer consistent with this
chapter;
9.
The amount of the maximum monetary penalty, if any; and
10.
The time and place at which the person cited is to appear before the hearings officer
which shall be not less than thirty (30) days from the date of the notice of violation
unless the director of land use and transportation or designee determines that an
expedited hearing schedule is required to remedy a priority 1 classification under
Section 1.14.210 (A).
B.
Any error in the notice of building or land use violation will not preclude the hearings
officer from conducting a hearing unless the hearings officer finds an error prejudices
a party to the proceeding. Failure to raise any error regarding the notice of violation
prior to the close of the hearing shall constitute a waiver of any right to object
to the notice at any subsequent hearing or on appeal.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.140 - Service.

A.
The county shall serve the notice on the property owner and the violator, if the violator
is different than the property owner, by any of the following methods:
1.
Personal service;
2.
Mailing by First Class Mail return receipt requested via the United States Post Office,
postage prepaid, and addressed to the home or business address of the party(ies);
3.
Any method authorized by Oregon Rules of Civil Procedure; or
4.
Any other method authorized by the hearings officer and reasonably calculated to inform
the violator.
B.
All parties shall serve a copy of any written correspondence with the hearings officer
on all other parties consistent with this section.
C.
There shall be a rebuttable presumption that any correspondence sent by mail was served
on the third (3rd) business day following deposit in the mail.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 14.15.150 - Legal representation.

A.
A violator may be represented by legal counsel. A violator must notify the county
within ten (10) days of the hearing if they are represented by an attorney. Failure
to do so may result in continuance of the hearing.
B.
County counsel, peace officer or an authorized employee may appear before the hearings
officer and prosecute the violation on behalf of the county.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.160 - Pre-hearing discovery.

A.
Upon motion demonstrating that the information is relevant and necessary, the hearings
officer may issue a pre-hearing order authorizing discovery of documents and things
or ordering the deposition of witness(es).
B.
The party requesting discovery shall pay the reasonable costs associated with the
responding party including copying for production of documents.
C.
If a party fails to comply with a pre-hearing discovery order, the hearings officer
may issue a subpoena compelling a party to produce the documents or things or to appear
as a witness. Any person other than the county or parties will be paid fees and mileage
reimbursement consistent with a civil action.
D.
If a person or party fails to comply with a pre- hearing discovery order, a party
may file an action in circuit court for contempt.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.170 - Hearing.

A.
The hearings officer shall conduct a hearing on each violation, unless the case is
dismissed, as follows:
1.
All testimony shall be under oath;
2.
A tape recording of the proceedings shall be maintained;
3.
County counsel, peace officer or other authorized employee prosecuting the violation
shall proceed first followed by the violator;
4.
Other persons may testify at the discretion of the hearings officer;
5.
Rebuttal is permitted as determined by the hearings officer;
6.
The hearings officer shall determine whether the preponderance of the evidence demonstrates
the violation occurred;
7.
The hearings officer may discount or exclude any evidence that the hearings officer
deems to be irrelevant immaterial, cumulative, or not reasonably credible;
8.
Circumstantial evidence is admissible; and
9.
The Oregon Evidence Code does not apply.
B.
If a violator fails to appear the county shall present a prima facie case demonstrating
that the violation occurred.
C.
If a violation is not remedied within the time ordered by the hearings officer, the
county may request a compliance hearing to determine if the person is failing to comply
with an order and decision of the hearings officer. Notice and conduct of a compliance
hearing shall be substantially in the same manner as the original violation.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.180 - Order of the hearings officer.

All orders of the hearings officer shall be in writing and contain findings of fact
and conclusions of law addressing the allegations contained in the notice of violation.
Orders may also include findings of fact supporting the penalties, restrictions, conditions
or other remedies as required by the hearings officer. The order shall become final
and effective fourteen (14) days from the date of the decision or such other time
as determined by the hearings officer to address priority I classification under Section
1.14.210 (A).
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.190 - Reconsideration.

A.
A hearings officer may reconsider a decision to address any errors or mistakes without
any additional process within fourteen (14) days from the date of the final decision.
B.
If the person accused of committing a violation fails to appear at a hearing due to
extraordinary circumstances, the person may file a petition for reconsideration and
request a new hearing within fourteen (14) days of the date of the final order and
decision.
C.
The hearings officer shall decide whether to allow a new hearing based on the petition
for reconsideration within fourteen (14) days of the date the request is received.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.200 - Appeals.

The sole method for appeal of a hearings officer's decision is by filing a writ of
review under ORS Chapter 34.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.220 - Administrative monetary penalties for a continuing violation.

A.
Administrative monetary penalties may be imposed when the hearings officer believes
that a violation may continue based on a repeat violation, failure to comply with
an order, or such other evidence that reasonably demonstrates a concern that the violation
will continue.
B.
The amount of the administrative monetary penalty shall be two hundred dollars ($200.00)
per month for the first year and four hundred dollars ($400.00) per month thereafter
for a time period not to exceed five (5) years.
C.
The administrative monetary penalty will be imposed on a monthly basis without a hearing
unless the violator demonstrates compliance as determined by the county consistent
with the order of the hearings officer.
D.
The county will provide notice of the administrative monetary penalty stating the
amount; the method of payment, reference to the decision of the hearings officer authorizing
imposition of the penalty, and the right to an appeal.
E.
An appeal of the administrative penalty may be filed with the county within fourteen
(14) days from the date of the notice of an administrative monetary penalty.
F.
An appeal of an administrative monetary penalty shall be accompanied by a two hundred
fifty dollar ($250.00) refundable fee that will be returned only if the appellant
prevails on appeal.
G.
The county will provide notice of and the hearings officer shall conduct an appeal
hearing in substantially the same manner as the original violation.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.230 - Collection of monetary penalties.

A.
Any monetary penalty imposed by the hearings officer, and not paid on the date specified
by the hearings officer, shall be turned over to the Washington County finance department
or assigned to a private collections company for collection.
B.
Any monetary penalty not paid by the date specified by the hearings officer, shall
be assessed an additional administrative collection fee equal to twenty-five percent
(25%) of the penalty.
C.
If an administrative monetary penalty or monetary penalty is not paid within sixty
(60) days of the date ordered by the hearings officer or as provided in the notice
of administrative monetary penalty, the violator is personally liable to the county
for the amount and the county may file and record the order for payment in the county
clerk lien record as authorized by ORS 30.460. No order for payment will be filed
in the county clerk lien record until sixty (60) days have elapsed from the date of
payment ordered by the hearings officer.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)

 ### 1.14.240 - Enforcement.

In addition to the provisions set out in this chapter, the county may enforce (1)
any provision of the Washington County Code, (2) Community Development Code including
but not limited to any Comprehensive Framework Plan such as Community Plans and the
Transportation System Plan, (3) conditions, requirements or other aspects of a land
use or building permit, (4) conditions, requirements or remedies of an order of the
hearings officer, or (5) failure to comply with the terms of a voluntary compliance
agreement, in any manner permitted by law.
(Ord. 707 § 3 Exhibit A (part), 2008)
