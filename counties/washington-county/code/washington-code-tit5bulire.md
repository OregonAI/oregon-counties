 ### Title 5 - BUSINESS LICENSES AND REGULATIONS

 ### Chapter 5.04 - SECONDHAND DEALERS

 ### 5.04.010 - Definitions.

As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
A.
"Acceptable identification" means either a valid driver's license, or two pieces of
identification issued by a government agency, one of which shall include a physical
description and a photograph of the person from whom the secondhand goods are being
purchased.
B.
"Sheriff" means the sheriff of Washington County, or his or her designee, and for
the purpose of this chapter means that person authorized to carry out the administration
and enforcement of this chapter.
C.
"Criminal arrests or convictions" means any offense defined by the statutes of the
state of Oregon or ordinances of Washington County, unless otherwise specified. Any
arrest or conviction for conduct other than that denoted by the statutes of the state
of Oregon or ordinances of Washington County, as specified herein, shall be considered
to be equivalent to one of such offenses if the elements of such offense for which
the person was arrested or convicted would have constituted one of the above offenses
under the applicable Oregon Statutes or Washington County ordinance provisions.
D.
"Investment purposes" means the purchase of personal property by businesses, and the
retention of that property in the same form as purchased, for resale to persons who
are purchasing the property primarily as an investment.
E.
"Business" means a "commercial or mercantile activity customarily engaged in as a
means of livelihood and typically involving some independence of judgment and power
of decision" (Webster's Third New International Dictionary, 1986). This should not
be construed to include individuals engaged in doing infrequent business involving
strictly personal property (e.g. twice a year garage sales, sale of business inventory
upon retirement, etc.).
F.
"To do business" means to engage in any activity, including but not limited to any
transaction involving the sale or rental of property, the manufacture or sale of goods
or the sale or rendering of services other than as an employee. To do business includes
activities carried on by a person through officers, agents or employees as well as
activities carried on by a person on his own behalf.
G.
"Occasional secondhand dealer" means any person:
1.
Who engages in, conducts, manages or carries on a business; and
2.
Who purchases or offers for sale no more than fifty items of regulated property in
any one year period; and
3.
Either:
a.
Purchases regulated property at or from premises located within Washington County,
or on behalf of such a business regardless of where the purchase occurs; or
b.
Offers for sale regulated property within Washington County.
H.
"Person" means any real person, or any partnership, association, organization, or
corporation.
I.
"Purchase" means to take or transfer any interest in personal property in a voluntary
transaction, including but not limited to: sales, consignments, leases, trade-ins,
loans or any transfer involving a condition of selling the property back at a stipulated
price in the future. "Purchase" does not include any loans made in compliance with
state laws by pawn brokers licensed by the state of Oregon.
J.
"Secondhand dealer" means any person:
1.
Who engages in, conducts, manages or carries on a business; and
2.
Who purchases or offers for sale fifty-one or more items of regulated property in
any one-year period; and
3.
Either:
a.
Purchases regulated property at or from business premises located within Washington
County or on behalf of such a business regardless of where the purchase occurs; or
b.
Offers for sale regulated property within Washington County.
K.
"Regulated property" means any of the following property which is used or secondhand:
1.
Precious metals including but not limited to: any metal that is valued for its character,
rarity, beauty or quality, including gold, silver, platinum or any other metals, whether
as a separate item or in combination as a piece of jewelry;
2.
Precious gems including but not limited to: any gem that is valued for its character,
rarity, beauty or quality, including diamonds, rubies, emeralds, sapphires or pearls,
or any other precious or semiprecious gems or stones, whether as a separate item or
in combination as a piece of jewelry;
3.
Watches and jewelry containing precious metals or precious gems including but not
limited to: rings, necklaces, pendants, earrings, brooches, chains, pocket watches,
wrist watches, or stop watches;
4.
Sterling silver including but not limited to: flatware, candleholders, coffee and
tea sets, or ornamental objects;
5.
Audio equipment including but not limited to: tape players, tape decks or players,
compact/digital disc players, sound metering devices, tuners, amplifiers, speakers,
transceivers, equalizers, receivers, phonographs, turntables, stereos, radios, clock
radios, car stereos, car speakers, radar detectors, or citizen band radios/transceivers.
6.
Video equipment including but not limited to: color televisions, black and white televisions,
videotape or videodisc recorders, videotape or videodisc players, video cameras or
video monitors;
7.
Photographic and optical equipment including but not limited to: cameras, camera lenses,
camera filters, camera motor drives, light meters, flash equipment, movie projectors,
slide projectors, photography processing equipment, photography enlarging equipment,
binoculars, telescopes, opera glasses, microscopes, surveying equipment, rifle scopes,
spotting scopes, or electronic sighting equipment;
8.
Electrical office equipment including but not limited to: telefax machines, laser
printers, copiers, duplicators, typewriters, calculators, cash registers, transcribers,
dictaphones, computers, home computers, modems, monitors, or any computer equipment,
accessories, or components;
9.
Power yard and garden tools including but not limited to: garden tractors, lawn mowers,
rototillers, lawn sweepers, weed or brush cutters, edgers, trimmers, or blowers;
10.
Power equipment and tools including but not limited to: air hammers, air tools, nail
guns, power staplers, power saws, power sanders, chainsaws, power planers, power drills,
routers lathes, joiners, shop vacuums, paint sprayers and accessory equipment, generators,
air compressors, pressure washers, or logging equipment;
11.
Automotive hand tools including but not limited to: wrench sets, socket sets, screw
driver sets, pliers, vise grips, tool boxes, auto body hammers, jacks, or timing lights;
12.
Telephones or telephone equipment limited to office telephones, portable home telephones,
mobile telephones, cellular telephones, or answering machines;
13.
Musical instruments including but not limited to: pianos, organs, guitars, violins,
cellos, trumpets, trombones, saxophones, flutes, drums, percussion instruments, or
electronic synthesizers;
14.
Firearms including but not limited to: rifles, shotguns, handguns, revolvers, pellet
guns, or BB guns;
15.
Sporting equipment limited to bicycles, golf clubs, skis and ski boots;
16.
Outdoor motors, props, and outdrives;
17.
Regulated property does not include any of the following property:
a.
Vehicles required to be registered with the Oregon Motor Vehicles Division,
b.
Boats required to be certified by the Oregon Marine Board,
c.
Books,
d.
Glassware,
e.
Furniture,
f.
Refrigerators, stoves, washers, dryers and other similar major household appliances,
g.
Property which is purchased by a bona fide business for investment purposes, limited
to:
i.
Gold bullion bars (0.995 fine or better),
ii.Silver
bullion bars (0.995 fine or better),
iii.
All tokens, coins, or money, whether commemorative or an actual medium of exchange
adopted by a domestic or foreign government as part of its currency, or
iv.
Postage stamps, stamp collections and philatelic items.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 3), 1991)

 ### 5.04.020 - Permit—Required.

A.
No person shall engage in, conduct or carry on an occasional secondhand dealer business
or a secondhand dealer business in the county without a valid occasional secondhand
dealer permit or a valid secondhand dealer permit issued by the sheriff.
B.
Upon purchasing fifty items of regulated property during any one-year period, an occasional
secondhand dealer shall apply for and obtain a secondhand dealer permit before purchasing
any more items of regulated property.
C.
Any person who advertises or otherwise holds themselves out to be purchasing or offering
for sale regulated property within the county shall be presumed to be operating a
business subject to the terms of this chapter.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 4), 1991)

 ### 5.04.030 - Permit—Application.

A.
An application for an occasional secondhand dealer's permit or a secondhand dealer's
permit shall set forth the following information:
1.
The name, address, telephone number, birth date and principal occupation of the applicant
and any other person who will be directly engaged or employed in the management or
operation of the business or the proposed business;
2.
The name, address and telephone number of the business or proposed business and a
description of the exact nature of the business to be operated;
3.
Written proof that the applicant is at least eighteen years of age;
4.
The applicant's business occupation or employment for the three years immediately
preceding the date of application;
5.
The business permit history of the applicant in operating a business identical to
or similar to those regulated by this chapter;
6.
A brief summary of the applicant's business history in the county or in any other
city, county or state including:
a.
The business license or permit history of the applicant, and
b.
Whether the applicant has ever had any license or permit revoked or suspended, the
reasons therefor, and the business activity or occupation of the applicant subsequent
to the suspension or revocation;
7.
If the business or proposed business is the undertaking of a sole proprietorship,
partnership or corporation:
a.
If a partnership, the application shall set forth the names, birth dates, addresses,
telephone numbers, principal occupations, along with all other information required
of any individual applicant of each partner, whether general, limited, or silent,
and the respective ownership shares owned by each,
b.
If a corporation, the applicant shall set forth the corporate name, copies of the
articles of incorporation and the corporate bylaws, and the names, addresses, birth
dates, telephone numbers, and principal occupations, along with all other information
required of any individual applicant, of every officer, director and shareholder (owning
more than five percent of the outstanding shares) and the number of shares held by
each,
8.
Any criminal arrests or convictions relating to fraud or theft of each applicant and
all natural persons enumerated in subdivisions 1 through 7 of this subsection; and
9.
Any other information which the sheriff may reasonably feel is necessary to accomplish
the goals of this chapter.
B.
The personal and business information contained in the application forms required
pursuant to Section 5.04.020 (A) shall be treated as confidential and exempted from disclosure to the maximum extent
permitted by law.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 5), 1991)

 ### 5.04.040 - Permit—Issuance and renewal.

A.
Upon the filing of an application for an occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand
dealer permit and payment of the required fee, the sheriff shall conduct an investigation
of the applicant. The sheriff shall issue or renew such permit within ninety days
of receiving a complete application if no cause for denial as noted herein exists.
B.
The sheriff may deny an application for a permit or renewal if:
1.
The applicant, or any other person who will be directly engaged in the management
or operation of the business, or any person who owns a five percent or more interest
in the business has previously owned or operated a business regulated by this chapter,
and:
a.
The license or permit for the business has been revoked for cause which would be grounds
for revocation pursuant to this chapter, or
b.
The business has been found to constitute a public nuisance and abatement has been
ordered, or
c.
Any of the persons involved in the business has been convicted of any criminal offense
noted in Section 5.04.030 (A)(8);
2.
The operation as proposed by the applicant is not in compliance and/or would not comply
with all applicable requirements of the county code including but not limited to:
the building, health, planning, zoning and fire codes of the county;
3.
Any statement in the application is found to be false or any required information
is withheld;
4.
Any employee is convicted of any criminal offense relating to fraud or theft and the
offense either occurred on the premises of the business subject to the permit or was
connected in a time and manner with the operation of the business so that the person(s)
in charge of such business knew, or should reasonably have known, that such offense(s)
would occur;
5.
Evidence exists that supports a finding by a preponderance of the evidence that any
of the persons involved in the business has been convicted of any offense noted in
Section 5.04.030 (A)(8); or
6.
Evidence exists to support a finding by a preponderance of the evidence that any of
the persons involved in the business has violated any provision of any law or statute
where the elements of such law or statute are equivalent to the provisions of the
Occasional Secondhand Dealers and Secondhand Dealers Code; or
7.
Evidence exists to support a finding that either:
a.
The location of the business for which the application has been filed has a history
of violations of the provisions of this chapter, or
b.
A statistically significant record exists of criminal offenses relating to fraud or
theft in the area located within five hundred feet of the premises, or
8.
The operation does not comply with applicable federal or state licensing requirements.
C.
Notwithstanding subsection B of this section, the sheriff may grant a permit despite
the presence of one or more of the enumerated factors if the applicant establishes
to the sheriff's satisfaction that:
1.
The behavior evidenced by such factor is not likely to recur, or
2.
The behavior evidenced by such factor is remote in time, or
3.
The behavior evidenced by such factor occurred under circumstances which diminish
the seriousness of the factor as it relates to the purpose of this chapter.
D.
Occasional secondhand dealer permits and secondhand dealer permits shall be for a
term not to exceed one year and shall expire on January 15 of the following year.
The permits shall be nontransferable and shall be valid only for a single location.
Applications for subsequent locations, if the business moves or changes address, shall
be required and processed according to the provisions of Section 5.04.070 .
E.
All occasional secondhand and secondhand dealer permits shall be displayed on the
business premises in a manner readily visible to patrons.
F.
1.
The sheriff, upon denial of an application for a permit or renewal, shall give the
applicant written notice of the denial by causing notice to be served upon the applicant
at the business or residence address listed on the application.
2.
Service of the notice shall be accomplished either by mailing the notice by certified
mail, return receipt requested, or at the option of the sheriff, by personal service
in the same manner as a summons served in an action at law.
3.
Refusal of the service by the person whose permit or renewal is denied shall be prima
facie evidence of receipt of the notice. Service of notice upon the person in charge
of a business during its hours of operation shall constitute prima facie evidence
of notice to the person holding the permit to operate the business.
4.
The denial shall be effective and final the date the notice is received by applicant
as evidenced by the return receipt or the return of service.
G.
Denial of a permit or renewal may be appealed first to the sheriff and then to the
county administrator's office by filing written notice of an appeal within ten days
of the date of denial, in accordance with Section 5.04.150 .
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 6), 1991)

 ### 5.04.050 - Permit—Fees.

A.
Every person engaged in, conducting or carrying on an occasional secondhand dealer
business shall:
1.
For an occasional secondhand dealer's permit, file an application within sixty days
of the effective date of the ordinance codified in this chapter with the sheriff and
pay a fee as established by board resolution and order.
2.
For renewal of an occasional secondhand dealer's permit, shall pay a fee as established
by board resolution and order.
B.
Every person engaged in, conducting or carrying on a secondhand dealer's business
shall:
1.
File an application within sixty days of the effective date of the ordinance codified
in this chapter with the sheriff and pay a fee as established by board resolution
and order. The sheriff shall allow occasional secondhand dealer application fee against
the charge for the secondhand dealer application fee.
2.
For renewal of a secondhand dealer's permit, pay a fee as established by board resolution
and order.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 7), 1991)

 ### 5.04.060 - Permit—Denial—Fee refund.

If any applicant is denied a permit under the provisions of this chapter, the fee
submitted with the permit application, as required by Section 5.04.050 , shall be refunded, except that portion of the fee that covers expenses incurred
in processing the application shall be retained as a nonrefundable investigation fee.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 8), 1991)

 ### 5.04.070 - Permit—Subsequent locations.

A.
The holder of a valid permit shall file an application for a permit for a subsequent
location with the sheriff and shall not be required to pay any fee, provided the information
required for the subsequent location is identical to that provided in the application
for the prior location with the exception of that required by Section 5.04.030 (A)(2).
B.
Permits issued for subsequent locations shall be subject to all the requirements of
this chapter, and the term of any permit issued for a subsequent location shall expire
on the same date as the initial permit.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 9), 1991)

 ### 5.04.080 - Seller identification—Purchase report forms.

A.
The sheriff shall provide all occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand dealers
with purchase report forms. The sheriff may specify the size, shape and color of the
purchase report form. The sheriff may require the purchase report forms to provide
any information relating to the regulations of this chapter. Occasional secondhand
dealers and secondhand dealers may utilize their own forms, in lieu of those supplied
by the sheriff, if such forms have been approved by the sheriff.
B.
1.
When purchasing regulated property, occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand dealers
shall obtain acceptable identification and a current residential address from the
seller.
2.
All occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand dealers shall write a description
of the purchased property upon a purchase report form at the time of purchasing any
item of regulated property. The description of the purchased property shall be as
called for by the purchase report form. All occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand
dealers shall fill in all of the blank spaces on the purchase report forms with the
date required by the form and require the person selling any regulated property to
sign his or her name on the form. All purchase report forms shall be filled out in
clearly legible, printed English.
3.
The information required to be furnished on purchase report forms is to assist in
the investigation of the theft of property. The information is of a confidential nature
and related to the personal privacy of persons doing business with the dealer, as
well as certain trade secrets and practices of occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand
dealers. The information shall be treated as confidential and exempt from disclosure
to the maximum extent possible under applicable laws.
C.
All occasional secondhand dealers and secondhand dealers shall mail or deliver to
the sheriff at the close of each business day the original and second copy of all
report forms describing articles purchased that business day.
D.
The third copy of all completed report forms shall be retained by occasional secondhand
dealers and secondhand dealers for a period of not less than one year from the date
of purchase on their business premises.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 10), 1991)

 ### 5.04.090 - Regulated property sale limitations.

A.
No regulated property by any occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand dealer shall
be sold for a period of fifteen calendar days after the date of purchase. The dealer
shall maintain the purchased property in substantially the same form as purchased
and shall not commingle the property to preclude identification during this fifteen-day
holding period. The purchased property shall be located on the business premises during
normal business hours during this holding period so that it can be inspected as provided
in Section 5.04.110 . Notwithstanding this requirement, the sheriff may authorize the sale or transfer
of an item of purchased regulated property before the expiration of this period, in
cases in which the dealer shows that extreme financial hardship will result from holding
property for the fifteen-day period.
B.
The sheriff may provide written notice, upon reasonable belief that the purchased
property is the subject of theft, to any occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand
dealer not to dispose of any specifically described property purchased. The dealer
shall retain the property in substantially the same form as purchased. The dealer
shall not sell, exchange, dismantle or otherwise dispose of the property for a period
of time, as determined by the sheriff, not to exceed one hundred eighty days from
the date of purchase.
C.
If an occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand dealer purchases regulated property
with serial numbers, personalized inscriptions or initials, or other identifying marks
which are or have been altered, obliterated, removed, or otherwise rendered illegible,
the occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand dealer shall hold such property on
the business premises for a period of ninety full days after purchase. The dealer
shall maintain the purchased regulated property in substantially the same form as
purchased and shall not commingle the property so as to preclude identification during
this ninety-day holding period. Such property shall be located on the business premises
during normal business hours during this holding period so that it can be inspected,
as provided in Section 5.04.110 .
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 11), 1991)

 ### 5.04.100 - Tagging regulated property.

Any occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand dealer purchasing any regulated property
shall affix to property, during the holding period required by Section 5.04.090 , a tag upon which shall be written a number in legible characters. The number shall
correspond to the number on the purchase report forms required by Section 5.04.080 .
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 12), 1991)

 ### 5.04.110 - Inspection of property and records.

Upon presentation of official identification, the sheriff may enter onto the business
premises of any person with an occasional secondhand dealer or secondhand dealer permit
to ensure compliance with the provisions of this chapter. The inspection shall be
for the limited purpose of inspecting any regulated property purchased by the dealer,
held by the dealer pursuant to Section 5.04.090 , or the records incident thereto. Any such inspection shall only be authorized to
occur during normal business hours.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 13), 1991)

 ### 5.04.120 - Prohibited acts.

It is unlawful for any person acting as owner, manager, agent or employee of a business
regulated by this chapter to violate the provisions of Sections 5.04.020 , 5.04.080 , 5.04.090 , 5.04.100 , 5.04.110 and/or 5.04.130 .
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 14), 1991)

 ### 5.04.130 - Permit—Revocation or suspension.

A.
The sheriff may revoke or suspend any permit issued pursuant to this chapter as provided
below:
1.
For any cause which would be grounds for denial of a permit;
2.
Where investigation reveals that any violation of the provisions of this chapter or
any offense noted in Section 5.04.110 has been committed by any person and such offense is connected in time and manner
with the operation of the business so that the person(s) in charge of such establishment
knew, or should reasonably have known that such violations have been permitted to
occur on the premises by the permit holder or any employee; or
3.
A lawful inspection has been refused; or
4.
If payment of civil penalties has been received by the sheriff within ten working
days after the penalty becomes final; or
5.
If any occasional secondhand dealer business or secondhand dealer business fails to
meet the federal or state licensing requirements.
B.
The sheriff upon revocation or suspension of any permit issued pursuant to this chapter
shall give the permittee written notice of such revocation or suspension in accordance
with the procedures in Section 5.04.040 (F) outlined for the denial of permit application.
C.
Suspension or revocation shall be effective and final ten days after the giving of
such notice unless such suspension or revocation is appealed, in accordance with Section
5.04.180.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 16), 1991)

 ### 5.04.140 - Permit—Revocation or suspension—Permit fee refund.

If any permit holder has that permit revoked or suspended under the provisions of
this chapter, the fee submitted with the permit application, as required by Section
5.04.050 , shall be retained as a nonrefundable fee.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 17), 1991)

 ### 5.04.150 - Appeals.

A.
1.
The filing of a notice of appeal of revocation or suspension of a permit, or of a
civil penalty imposed by the sheriff, under this chapter shall stay the effective
date of the action until the appeal is determined.
2.
The notice of appeal shall state the name and address of the appellant to which all
notices required herein may be mailed. The notice shall also indicate the reasons
why the action was incorrect and what the correct determination should be.
3.
The appellant shall be deemed to have waived the right to object and the appeal shall
be dismissed if:
a.
The notice of appeal is not filed within the specified time; or
b.
The notice of appeal does not otherwise conform to these requirements.
B.
1.
Notice of appeal shall be filed with the county sheriff's office, which shall set
a date for the hearing of the appeal and shall notify the appellant of the hearing
date.
2.
The hearing shall be conducted by an impartial and uninvolved member of the sheriff's
office. At this hearing, the sheriff shall report the reasons for the action. The
appellant shall have an opportunity to present evidence and oral argument, and to
file a written statement. A record shall be made of this hearing. At the conclusion
of this hearing, the officer shall determine the appeal. If the appellant is dissatisfied
with the officer's decision, the appellant may appeal to the county administrator's
office by filing the appropriate documents with the clerk of that office.
3.
At the county administrator's officer hearing, the sheriff shall report to the county
administrator's office the reasons for the action. The appellant shall have the opportunity
to present evidence and oral argument to the county administrator's office and to
file a written statement. A record shall be made of this hearing. At the conclusion
of the hearing, the county administrator's office shall determine the appeal and direct
that written findings be prepared. If the county administrator's office denies the
appeal, the action shall be effective upon the county administrator's office signing
the findings. The decision of the county administrator's office shall be final.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 18), 1991)

 ### 5.04.160 - Nuisance declared—Abatement.

Any business maintained in violation of the provisions of this chapter is declared
to be a public nuisance. The county counsel is authorized to bring any action or suit
as provided by law to abate such nuisance including seeking injunctive or other appropriate
relief to:
A.
Cease all unlawful activities; or
B.
Close the unlawful business establishment; or
C.
Return property obtained through unlawful activities to the rightful owners; or
D.
Seek payment of civil penalties assessed by the sheriff; or
E.
Seek other such relief as may be appropriate.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 19), 1991)

 ### 5.04.170 - Violation—Penalty.

A.
The sheriff may seek civil penalties for violations of this chapter in accordance
with the schedule established by board resolution and order.
B.
The commission of any prohibited act listed in Section 5.04.120 is punishable, upon conviction, by a fine of not more than five hundred dollars,
and a jail sentence of up to one year.
(Ord. 389 § 2(Exhibit A § 15), 1991)

 ### Chapter 5.08 - CABLE COMMUNICATIONS SYSTEMS

 ### 5.08.010 - Short title.

This chapter shall be known and referred to as the "county cable communications ordinance."
(Ord. 460 § 2 (part), 1994: Ord. 225 § 2, 1980)

 ### 5.08.020 - Purpose and intent.

A.
The Washington County board of county commissioners has determined that it is in the
public interest and necessary for the promotion of the safety, convenience, comfort,
prosperity and general welfare of the citizens of the county to have an ordinance
which identifies the conditions upon which the county will grant a franchise or franchises
to construct and operate a cable communications system.
B.
Therefore, the board declares its intent to adopt the ordinance codified in this chapter
pursuant to the county's police power and Home Rule authority as provided by Article
VI, Section 10 of the Oregon Constitution, and the Washington County Charter adopted
by the citizens on November 6, 1962, and as subsequently amended.
C.
The board recognizes that at the time of the adoption of the ordinance codified in
this chapter, there are persons, firms or organizations which are in the process of
developing or constructing a cable communications system. This construction is occurring
without the benefit of an overall county plan defining the standards for construction
and operation of such systems. It is the board's belief that it is in the public's
interest to have a cable communication plan in place prior to the development and
construction of a system. Therefore, the board believes there is a public necessity
in imposing a temporary moratorium preventing construction of a system until the development
and adoption of a plan. Sections 5.08.150 through 5.08.180 of this chapter authorize and define the scope of the moratorium.
A statement of reasons of public benefit justifying the adoption of a moratorium on
the construction of a cable communications system in the county until the development
and adoption of a plan governing such systems is attached and incorporated herein
as a part of this chapter as Appendix I.
(Ord. 460 § 2 (part), 1994: Ord. 225 § 1, 1980)

APPENDIX I
STATEMENT OF REASONS OF PUBLIC BENEFIT JUSTIFYING THE ADOPTION OF A POLICY WHICH IMPOSES
A MORATORIUM ON THE CONSTRUCTION OF A CABLE COMMUNICATIONS SYSTEM IN WASHINGTON COUNTY
UNTIL THE DEVELOPMENT AND ADOPTION OF A PLAN GOVERNING SUCH SYSTEMS

 ### I. - INTRODUCTION AND STATEMENT OF INTENT.

The Washington County Board of County Commissioners has determined that it is in the
public's interest and necessary for the promotion and enhancement of the safety, convenience,
comfort, prosperity and general welfare of the citizens of Washington County to have
a regulation which identifies the conditions upon which the County will allow for
the construction and operation of a cable communications system.
The County is cognizant of the fact that it has previously issued placement permits
pursuant to the authority of ORS Chapter 374, and that the construction of cable communication
systems are presently ongoing within the County. This construction is occurring without
the benefit of an overall county plan defining the standards for construction and
operation of such systems. Further, there is presently no legal prohibition barring
any person or organization from applying for a placement permit and initiating the
construction of a cable system.
It is the Board's belief that the County has a responsibility to have a cable communications
plan in place prior to the development and construction of a system. Therefore, the
Board believes there is a public necessity in imposing a temporary moratorium preventing
construction until the development and adoption of a plan. The ability of the County
to protect the health, safety and welfare of its citizens will be diminished if a
moratorium is not imposed.
It is not the Board's intent to impose a moratorium for the purpose of restraining
trade, limiting competition, allowing one cable television organization to gain a
competitive edge over another, or to interfere with the constitutionally guaranteed
right of freedom of speech, but rather for the heretofore stated and following reasons.

 ### II. - IDENTIFICATION OF THE SIGNIFICANT IMPACT OF CABLE TELEVISION ON THE COMMUNITY AND THE COUNTY'S RESPONSIBILITY TO IMPOSE A MORATORIUM AT THIS TIME.

The Board recognizes:
1.
That the cable television (hereinafter referred to as "CATV") industry is carrying
on a business which is in the nature of a public utility and has developed as a natural
monopoly like gas, electric, telephone, water and is usually served by one system
within the community;
2.
That CATV provides great flexibility and technical capabilities, including two-way
communication, which gives it the potential of providing the community an efficient
medium for communication;
3.
That CATV has the capability of providing channels for public access for the exchange
of community information; education and classroom networks; government information;
fire, burglary protection and consumer services;
4.
That a properly functioning cable communications system within the community is energy
efficient which is of interest to the citizens of Washington County;
5.
That the potential of a cable communications system as a medium for community ideas
and information is so great that it demands a certain amount of regulation to assure
that the systems potential is fulfilled for the benefit of the citizens of the County;
6.
That a cable communications system in the County must facilitate the unique demands
of the community;
7.
That prior to the development and the construction of a cable communication system
there must be a determination by the public of how CATV can serve the needs of the
community which will allow for the system to be designed so that the system will realize
its full potential and have social utility;
8.
That the effect of CATV on the community can be compared with the advent of the telephone
or other invention or technology which has the remarkable potential for reshaping
the lives of the general-public, and the environment in which we live, and therefore
underscores the importance of developing an overall plan for a community cable communications
system prior to its construction;
9.
That the County has imposed moratoriums in the past regarding the issuance of sewer
permits and subdivision development until the development and adoption of a plan which
would ensure that an activity would occur in the best interests of the citizens of
the County. Further, the Board recognizes that because of the significant impact of
certain activities within the community and because planning is essential prior to
allowing such activities, the County therefore has an overwhelming responsibility
to prevent those activities unless there are defined standards in place beforehand;
10.
That there is no other alternative than to impose a moratorium to allow for the development
of such an overall plan, because the public cannot be assured that the needs and interests
of the community will be met by allowing for the construction of a system prior to
the development of a plan, and the definition of local needs;
11.
That it is not the intent of the Board to impose a moratorium for an indefinite period,
which would be detrimental to the CATV industry or the public. The moratorium will
be for a four month period to allow for the development and adoption of a plan. However,
the Board may renew the moratorium for 30 day periods at the conclusion of the 4 month
period if a plan has not been adopted.

 ### III. - IDENTIFICATION OF REASONS WHY IT IS IN THE PUBLIC'S INTEREST TO HAVE A MORATORIUM.

1.
Without a plan, the County has not defined the design of the system. The County may
determine that it is more environmentally and aesthetically sound to require the system
to be entirely underground, rather than attaching wires to utility poles. To require
all undergrounding would require the removal of wire already installed which would
cause unnecessary disruption within the right-of-way and be an inefficient use and
waste of energy and other resources;
2.
Without a plan, the County has not determined whether it is in the public's best interest
to allow for the construction of duplicate systems within the county right- of-way.
Without a moratorium, more than one CATV person, firm or organization could construct
a system within the right-of-way. The Board recognizes at this time a variety of disadvantages
which flow from duplicate systems:
a.
CATV is a decreasing cost industry. The initial cost is largely fixed, and the more
subscribers, the cheaper the cost per subscriber. Duplication would lead to an inability
to spread fixed costs effectively.
b.
Duplication can injure service, because in order to keep costs down, competitors are
likely to curtail postponable expenditures that the consumer would, in the long run,
be better off having. Programming quality might decrease because consumers would be
unable to detect quality differences.
c.
Historically, duplication of service in decreasing cost industries has led to ultimate
consolidation, the result being that consumers eventually were required to pay higher
rates to compensate for unnecessary capital investment. The other possibility is segmentation
of the market by agreement, which again results in high rates because fixed costs
cannot be spread among as many consumers.
d.
An increase in the disruption of travel due to maintenance and repair work required
on two or more cable systems within a single right-of-way.
e.
The unsightly effects produced by adding additional strand, cable, and related appurtenances
to power poles along a single right-of-way.
f.
An increase in the risks of injury presented to abutting landowners and the public
as more electrically- charged facilities are installed.
3.
Without a plan, which defines the design of the system, there is no assurance that
duplicate systems will be able to physically interconnect and interface for the purpose
of providing consistent CATV service to all users of the system within the County,
including two-way telecommunication services for police and fire station accessibility.
4.
Without a plan, there is no definition of who will get service and when. A plan will
ensure that the construction of a system will provide equitable service to all areas
of the County, and ensure that outlying or lower economic areas will be served in
an economic and efficient manner.
5.
Without a plan, any person, firm or organization will construct a system not knowing
if it will comply with the needs of the community. Even though the Board recognizes
that certain CATV organizations have in good faith assured the County they will do
anything required of them after a plan is adopted, there are no assurances that compliance
could be made. It would be a disservice to the community and unrealistic to allow
for the construction without a plan, and then subsequently request a person, firm
or organization to make a change which might be economically or physically impossible
to accommodate.

 ### Chapter 5.16 - SOCIAL GAMBLING

 ### 5.16.010 - Definitions.

As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
A.
"Board" means the board of county commissioners of the county.
B.
"Charitable, fraternal or religious organization" means any person or entity organized
and existing for charitable, benevolent, eleemosynary, humane, patriotic, religious,
philanthropic, recreational, social, educational, civic, fraternal or other nonprofit
purposes, and which is also exempt from payment of federal income taxes because of
its charitable, fraternal or religious purposes.
C.
"Department" means the department of public safety of the county, which is under the
administration of the county sheriff.
D.
"Player" means a person who engages in any form of gambling solely as a contestant
or bettor, without receiving or becoming entitled to receive any profit therefrom
other than personal gambling winnings, and without otherwise rendering any material
assistance to the establishment, conduct or operation of the particular gambling activity.
A person who gambles at a social game of chance on equal terms with the other participants
therein is a person who does not otherwise render material assistance to the establishment,
conduct or operation thereof by performing, without fee or remuneration, acts directed
toward the arrangement or facilitation of the game, such as inviting persons to play,
permitting the use of premises therefor and supplying cards or other equipment used
therein. A person who engages in bookmaking is not a player.
E.
"Social game" means a game, other than a lottery, between players in a charitable,
religious or fraternal organization where no house player, house bank or house odds
exist and there is no house income from the operation of the social game.
(Ord. 211 § 1, 1978)

 ### 5.16.020 - Authority—Enforcement.

The department of public safety, under the supervision of the board, shall be responsible
for the administration of, and shall have the authority to enforce, this chapter and
its enacted rules and regulations.
(Ord. 211 § 2(A), 1978)

 ### 5.16.030 - Authority—Investigations.

The department shall have authority to administer oaths; certify to all official acts;
subpoena and require the attendance of witnesses at public hearings before the department
or board; subpoena or require production of relevant documents; swear witnesses; take

the business premises of any person regulated by this chapter at reasonable times

by the board.
(Ord. 211 § 2(B), 1978)

 ### 5.16.040 - Promulgation of rules and regulations.

and order, promulgate rules and regulations pertaining to the administration and enforcement

(Ord. 211 § 3, 1978)

A social game between players in a charitable, religious or fraternal organization
is authorized when licensed by this chapter, when otherwise legally constituted under
all applicable county ordinances and state and federal laws and regulations, and where
the following applicable conditions, set out in Sections 5.16.060 through 5.16.120 , have been met.
(Ord. 211 § 4(part), 1978)

No doors, screens, or other obstructions shall conceal or be likely to conceal a player
or players at any table or unit.
(Ord. 211 § 4(D), 1978)

gambling between the hours of two a.m. and ten a.m. of any day.

C.
Shall have a term as directed by the applicant, including the date of issuance and
the date of expiration, limited only by the provisions of this chapter, except that
all licenses issued for a term of one year shall be dated as of the first day of the
month of its issuance, and shall expire one year from that date;
D.
Shall not exceed a term of one year.
(Ord. 211 § 5(C), 1978)

 ### 5.16.160 - License—Nonrenewable.

There shall be no right to automatic renewal of a license issued pursuant to this
chapter. Every licensee shall be required to make application for a new license, upon
the expiration of its former license.
(Ord. 211 § 5(D), 1978)

 ### 5.16.170 - License—Fee nonrefundable.

In the event of the surrender, suspension, revocation, or expiration of any license
issued pursuant to this chapter, no license fee or portion thereof shall be refunded.
(Ord. 211 § 5(E), 1978)

 ### 5.16.180 - Records—Audit authority.

The department may, at its discretion, cause an audit to be performed upon any licensee.
Such audit shall be performed by a qualified and licensed accountant who shall be
selected by the department. The department may at any time view the books and records
of the licensee to ascertain compliance with this chapter.
(Ord. 211 § 6(A), 1978)

 ### 5.16.190 - Records—Grounds for revocation or suspension of license.

Any failure to keep, or a refusal to allow the audit or view of any books, records
or accounts of any licensee shall constitute sufficient grounds for the revocation
or suspension of the license at the discretion of the department.
(Ord. 211 § 6(B), 1978)

 ### 5.16.200 - Application—Forms.

Application for the license issued pursuant to this chapter shall be made upon forms
provided by the department and shall be accompanied by the fee required in Section
5.16.130 .
(Ord. 211 § 7(A), 1978)

 ### 5.16.210 - Application—Contents.

The application shall require:
A.
The true name and address of the licensee and of all persons directly responsible
for the operation of the organization including, but not limited to:
1.
Members of the board of directors or other similar governing body,
2.
Officers,
3.
Managers or any other similar employee;
B.
The past criminal record, if any, of all such persons;
C.
A certification of exemption from payment of federal income taxes of the charitable,
fraternal or religious organization;
D.
The address and a description of the premises; and
E.
Any additional information required by the department, which information may be required
in the form of a supplemental application or applications.
(Ord. 211 § 7(B), 1978)

 ### 5.16.220 - Application—Oath.

All persons required to supply information in the application or supplemental applications,
shall by oath or affirmation swear to the veracity of the information supplied by
them.
(Ord. 211 § 7(C), 1978)

 ### 5.16.230 - Application—Investigation.

Whenever an application for a social gambling license has been filed with the department,
the department shall review the application and determine the scope of investigation,
if any, that shall be made by the department. Investigation may be made of the applicant
and all persons directly responsible for the operation of the organization or any
one or more of such persons. An investigation may include, but shall not be limited
to, financial stability, business experience, personal history, reputation for habits,
traits and character and prior criminal record.
(Ord. 211 § 8, 1978)

 ### 5.16.240 - Denial—When.

The application for a social gambling license may be denied if the department finds
that:
A.
The applicant, or any of the officers, agents, or principal managing employees of
the applicant has:
1.
Pleaded no contest to, or been convicted of any felony, or
2.
Within three years, forfeited bail on, pleaded no contest to, or been convicted of
any misdemeanor or violation related to gambling, or
3.
Falsified any statement in the license application required by this chapter;
B.
The applicant has been convicted of any crime related to obscene behavior as defined
in ORS Chapter 167, or the commission of any criminal act on the premises described
in the application, or has failed to maintain the premises in conformance with all
requirements of this chapter;
C.
The applicant has had a license which was revoked or suspended three times by the
Oregon Liquor Control Commission, the last of which was within five years of the request
for application;
D.
The applicant has engaged in any other conduct which in the discretion of the department
would be valid reason for denial of the license.
(Ord. 211 § 9(A), 1978)

 ### 5.16.250 - Denial—Fee refund.

If any applicant is denied a license under the provisions of this chapter, the fee
submitted with the license application, as required by Section 5.16.130 , shall be refunded, except that twenty-five percent of that fee shall be retained
as a nonrefundable investigation fee.
(Ord. 211 § 9(B), 1978)

 ### 5.16.260 - Revocation and suspension.

The license required under this chapter may be revoked, or temporarily suspended for
up to thirty days, by the department for any reason that would be grounds for denial
of an application for a license, or any act in violation of this chapter. Revocation
or suspension shall become effective ten days after the department notifies the licensee
in writing of the grounds for such revocation or suspension, unless the licensee shall
give notice of appeal to the department prior to the effective date of the revocation
or suspension. Upon proper notice of appeal the revocation or suspension shall be
stayed until final determination by the board, except as provided in Section 5.16.370 . All notice required in this section shall be given either by personal service or
registered mail, return receipt requested.
(Ord. 211 § 10, 1978)

 ### 5.16.270 - Appeal—Time limit.

When an application for a license required by this chapter is denied, the applicant
shall have the right to appeal such decision by making and delivering a written request
for review to the department within ten days following receipt by applicant of notice
of denial.
(Ord. 211 § 11(A), 1978)

 ### 5.16.280 - Appeal—When rights waived.

Failure to make appeal as provided in Section 5.16.260 or in Section 5.16.270 shall be deemed to be a waiver of the right of appeal. The provisions set out in
Sections 5.16.270 through 5.16.370 shall be the exclusive method of appeal of a denial, revocation or suspension of
a license required by this chapter. An appeal shall be heard by the board in the manner
provided as set out in Sections 5.16.290 through 5.16.370 .
(Ord. 211 § 11(B), 1978)

 ### 5.16.290 - Hearing—Date, time and place.

Upon receipt of the notice of appeal, and within ten days, the department shall fix
a date, time and place where the review hearing shall be conducted, and shall so notify
the appellant.
(Ord. 211 § 11(C), 1978)

 ### 5.16.300 - Hearing—Time limit.

The date upon which the hearing shall be conducted shall be not more than thirty days
from the receipt of the notice of appeal by the department.
(Ord. 211 § 11(D), 1978)

 ### 5.16.310 - Hearing—Right to counsel.

The appellant shall have the right to be accompanied and represented by legal counsel.
(Ord. 211 § 11(E), 1978)

 ### 5.16.320 - Hearing—Record.

A verbatim record of the proceeding shall be made by electronic, written or mechanical
means.
(Ord. 211 § 11(F), 1978)

 ### 5.16.330 - Hearing—Justification of findings.

For all cases the board shall make findings based on the record before it and any
testimony or other evidence received by it as justification for its action.
(Ord. 211 § 11(G), 1978)

 ### 5.16.340 - Hearing—Board choice of actions.

The board may affirm a denial of a license, direct that additional investigation be
made or order issuance of a license. The board may affirm, modify or reject a revocation
or suspension of a license.
(Ord. 211 § 11(H), 1978)

 ### 5.16.350 - Notice.

All notice required by Sections 5.16.270 through 5.16.370 shall be given either by personal service or registered mail, return receipt requested.
(Ord. 211 § 11(I), 1978)

 ### 5.16.360 - Review.

All final orders made pursuant to the procedures as set forth in this chapter shall
be reviewable by the Circuit Court of the state of Oregon solely and exclusively by
writ of review in the manner set forth in ORS 34.010 through 34.100; provided, however,
that any aggrieved party shall have standing to have such final order reviewed.
(Ord. 211 § 11(J), 1978)

 ### 5.16.370 - Actions not stayed by appeal—When.

When the appeal is from a notice of revocation or suspension of license, the revocation
or suspension shall not be stayed by the appeal if the department finds that the grounds
for revocation or suspension are of an especially serious nature. Grounds which are
of an "especially serious nature" include, but are not limited to:
A.
Conviction of or no contest plea to any felony; or
B.
Any activity which may result in a conviction for a gambling-related offense under
any state or federal law or regulation.
(Ord. 211 § 11(K), 1978)

 ### 5.16.380 - Interpretation.

This chapter and all its provisions shall be liberally interpreted and construed so
as to maintain and effectuate the intent and purpose of this chapter and its provisions.
(Ord. 211 § 13, 1978)
