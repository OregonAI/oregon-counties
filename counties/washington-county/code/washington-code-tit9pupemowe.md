 ### Title 9 - PUBLIC PEACE, MORALS AND WELFARE

 ### Chapter 9.04 - SEXUAL CONTACT FOR A FEE

 ### 9.04.010 - Findings.

The board of county commissioners of the county finds that sexual contact as hereinafter
defined, for a fee, encourages public disorder, derogates from public morality, and
creates a public nuisance which is inimical to public peace, health and welfare.
(Ord. 108 § 1(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.020 - Purpose and construction.

The purpose of this chapter is to carry out the legislative intent and policy of this
board to prohibit such sexual contact within the unincorporated areas of the county.
This chapter shall be liberally construed to that end.
(Ord. 108 § 1(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.030 - Definitions.

For the purpose of this chapter, words in the present tense include the future, the
singular number includes the plural and the plural number includes the singular; the
word "shall" is mandatory and not directory; the masculine gender includes the feminine
and neuter and the term "this chapter" shall be deemed to include the text of this
chapter and all amendments hereafter made thereto. As used in this chapter, unless
the context requires otherwise, the following words and their derivations shall be
utilized:
A.
"Private parts" means the genital organs or the external genital procreative organs
of a male or female human being.
B.
"Sexual contact" means any touching of the private parts of one person by another
person or causing one person to touch the sexual or other intimate parts of another
for the purpose of arousing or gratifying the sexual desire of either party.
(Ord. 108 § 2, 1972)

 ### 9.04.040 - Sexual contact for a fee prohibited.

No person shall pay or receive a fee, directly or indirectly, for sexual contact.
(Ord. 108 § 3(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.050 - Causing or permitting prohibited.

No person who manages or controls any place of business or commercial activity shall
cause or permit any agent, employee, or other person under his control or supervision
to participate in conduct prohibited by Section 9.28.040.
(Ord. 108 § 3(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.060 - Solicitation prohibited.

No person shall solicit, employ or engage another, or confederate with another to
violate Section 9.28.040.
(Ord. 108 § 3(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.070 - Attempting prohibited.

No person shall attempt to commit the offense set forth in Section 9.28.040, but for
any reason is prevented from consummating such act.
(Ord. 108 § 3(part), 1972)

 ### 9.04.080 - Violation—Penalty.

A.
Violation of Sections 9.28.040 through 9.28.070 of this chapter shall be punishable,
upon conviction, by a fine not to exceed five hundred dollars, or by imprisonment
in the county jail for a period not to exceed six months, or by both such fine and
imprisonment.
B.
Each violation of any subsection of Sections 9.28.040 through 9.28.070 of this chapter
shall constitute a separate offense and each day that any person violates Sections
9.28.040 through 9.28.070 of this chapter shall constitute a separate offense.
(Ord. 108 § 4, 1972)

 ### Chapter 9.08 - CURFEW

 ### 9.08.010 - Findings.

Protection of the health, safety and welfare of the entire community requires that
the conduct of certain specified age groups or unaccompanied or unemancipated minors
be regulated so as to promote community peace and order.
(Ord. 388 § 1, 1991)

 ### 9.08.020 - Authority and purpose.

The board of county commissioners finds that the county is authorized to regulate
the conduct of certain minors in public places during certain hours pursuant to ORS
419.740.
(Ord. 388 § 2, 1991)

 ### 9.08.030 - Definitions.

For purposes of this chapter:
A.
"Emancipated" means that certain rights of the majority have been conferred pursuant
to state law.
B.
"Minor" means a person who is under eighteen years of age.
C.
"Parent" means the natural or adoptive father or mother of a dependent child, or the
stepfather or stepmother of a dependent child when such stepparent has a legal obligation
to support the child.
D.
"Public place" means any premises open to the general public, independent of whether
the premises are publicly or privately owned, or whether or not a fee is charged for
use of the premises.
E.
"Unemancipated" means certain rights of majority have not been conferred by state
law.
(Ord. 388 § 3, 1991)

 ### 9.08.040 - Restrictions—Exceptions.

No unemancipated minor shall be upon any street, highway, park, alley, or other public
place during the hours of curfew specified in Section 9.08.060 of this chapter, unless such minor is accompanied by parent, guardian or other person
twenty-one years of age or older, and authorized by the parent or law to have care
and custody of the minor, or unless such a minor is then engaged in a school activity
or lawful employment which makes it necessary for such minor to be in or upon such
a street, highway, park, alley or other public place during the hours specified in
Section 9.08.060 .
(Ord. 388 § 4, 1991)

 ### 9.08.050 - Emancipation.

This curfew shall not apply to any minor emancipated pursuant to ORS 109.550 to 190.565.
(Ord. 388 § 5, 1991)

 ### 9.08.060 - Curfew hours.

For the purpose of this chapter, the applicable hours of curfew shall be:
A.
As to minors under fourteen years of age:
1.
During the months of September through May, the hours of curfew shall be between nine-fifteen
p.m. and six a.m. the following morning, except that on any day immediately preceding
a day for which no public school is scheduled, the curfew shall be between ten-fifteen
p.m. and six a.m. the following morning.
2.
During the months of June through August, the hours of curfew shall be between ten-fifteen
p.m. and six a.m. the following morning.
B.
As to minors fourteen years of age or older:
1.
During the months of September through May, the hours of curfew shall be between ten-fifteen
p.m. and six a.m. the following morning, except that on any day for which no public
school is scheduled, the curfew shall be between eleven-fifty-nine p.m. and six a.m.
the following morning.
2.
During the months of June through August, the hours of curfew shall be between eleven-fifty-nine
and six a.m. the following morning.
(Ord. 388 § 6, 1991)

 ### 9.08.070 - Parental responsibility.

No parent of a minor shall allow or fail to prohibit such minor to be in a public
place in violation of Section 9.08.060 .
(Ord. 388 § 7, 1991)

 ### 9.08.080 - Newspaper delivery—Employment card—Penalty.

A.
Any child under the age of eighteen years employed in selling, delivering or wrapping
newspapers shall obtain from the newspaper employer, and the employer shall issue
to the child employed a card showing such employment within the protection of Section
9.08.040 .
B.
The penalty for failure to obtain a card under this section shall be the same as provided
for under Section 9.08.110 .
(Ord. 388 § 8, 1991)

 ### 9.08.090 - Temporary custody.

Any minor who violates any provision of this chapter may be taken into temporary custody
as provided in ORS 419.760 and may be subject to further proceedings as provided therein.
(Ord. 388 § 9, 1991)

 ### 9.08.100 - Affirmative defenses.

The following shall be affirmative defenses to the offenses described in Sections
9.08.040 and 9.08.060 . Such affirmative defenses are to be proved by the defendant by a preponderance of
the evidence:
A.
That the minor was emancipated pursuant to ORS 109.550 to 109.565;
B.
That the minor was engaged in or traveling to or from a lawful place of employment,
education or similar activity.
C.
That the minor was accompanied by a parent or by a person twenty-one years of age
or older who was authorized to have care and custody of the minor either by law or
by a parent of the minor.
(Ord. 388 § 10, 1991)

 ### 9.08.110 - Violation—Penalty.

Any violation of Sections 9.08.040 through 9.08.090 shall be punishable by a fine not to exceed five hundred dollars.
A.
Any minor violating any provision of this chapter may be apprehended and taken into
custody as provided in ORS 419.760 and may be subject to further proceedings as provided.
B.
Any violation of the provisions of this chapter is punishable, upon conviction, by
a fine not to exceed five hundred dollars, or by imprisonment not to exceed six months
or both. However, no greater penalty shall be imposed than the penalty prescribed
by Oregon statute for the same offense.
(Ord. 388 § 11, 1991)

 ### Chapter 9.12 - INVENTORY OF PROPERTY IN POLICE CUSTODY

 ### 9.12.010 - Purpose.

The Washington County Board of Commissioners has determined that there is a serious,
imminent risk to public safety associated with transporting and storing items of personal
property in the possession of a person taken into police custody when that personal
property has not been inventoried. Transporting or storing un-inventoried property
in Washington County vehicles or in Washington County facilities puts staff, inmates
and public property at risk by allowing the intentional or unintentional introduction
of personal property that contains toxic, explosive, hazardous or flammable substances,
or weapons. In order to alleviate this serious threat to public safety, the board
has determined that no closed container in the possession of a person taken into custody
should be transported or stored in Washington County facilities or vehicles unless
it is inventoried. In order to balance the need to ensure public safety with the privacy
rights of persons taken into police custody, this ordinance allows a person taken
into custody to turn their property over to a third party present at the scene where
they are taken into custody. For vehicles being impounded, this ordinance also allows
drivers and passengers who are not being taken into custody to remove personal property
from the vehicle to avoid having the property inventoried.
The board has also determined that storage and transport of toxic, explosive, hazardous
and flammable materials is a highly regulated activity under both state and federal
law, and that inventory of all containers that will be stored in a Washington County
facility or transported in Washington County vehicles is necessary to comply with
these restrictions.
This chapter is meant to exclusively apply to the process for conducting an inventory
of the personal property in an impounded vehicle and the personal possessions of a
person in police custody and shall not be interpreted to affect any other statutory
or constitutional right(s) that police officers may employ to search persons or search
or seize possessions for other purposes.
(Ord. 461 § 2 (part), 1995)
(Ord. No. 779, § 2(Exh. A), 1-21-2014)

 ### 9.12.020 - Definitions.

For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions shall apply:
A.
"Closed container" means a container whose contents are not exposed to view.
B.
"Open container" means a container which is unsecured or incompletely secured in such
a fashion that the container's contents are exposed or partially exposed to view,
including a translucent or transparent container.
C.
"Custody" means:
1.
The imposition of restraint as a result of an "arrest" as that term is defined at
ORS 133.005(1);
2.
The imposition of actual or constructive restraint by a police officer pursuant to
a court order;
3.
The imposition of actual or constructive restraint by a police officer pursuant to
ORS Chapter 426, 419 or 430; or
4.
The imposition of actual or constructive restraint by a police officer for the purpose
of taking the restrained person to an approved facility for the involuntary confinement
of persons pursuant to Oregon law.
D.
"Valuables" means:
1.
Cash money of an aggregate amount of fifty dollars or more; or
2.
Individual items of personal property with a value of over one hundred dollars.
3.
Personal electronics, including but not limited to laptop or tablet computers, portable
digital assistants, portable gps units, digital cameras, game systems, portable music
players, or cell phones.
E.
"Toxic, Flammable, Hazardous or Explosive Substances" includes:
1.
Substances, materials or items which have the ability to rapidly conflagrate or detonate
as a consequence of chemical reaction. Examples include ammunition, fireworks, pyrotechnics,
flares, blasting caps, detonators, fuse, primers, explosive charges, detonating cord,
air bag inflators, igniters, rockets, TNT, RDX or PETN.
2.
Substances or materials which are capable of posing serious hazards due to their flammability,
potential as asphyxiants, ability to oxidize and/or their toxicity or corrosiveness
to humans. Examples include but are not limited to aerosols, compressed air, gas cartridges,
fertilizer ammoniating solutions, insecticides, refrigerant gases, lighters, acetylene,
oxyacetylene, carbon dioxide, helium, hydrogen, oxygen, natural gas, oil gas, petroleum
gas, propane, ethane, ether, methane, butane or propylene.
3.
Liquids which are capable of posing serious hazards due to their volatility, combustibility
and potential in causing or propagating severe conflagrations. Examples include but
are not limited to acetone, adhesives, paints, lacquers, alcohols, perfumes, gasoline,
diesel, kerosene, tars, turpentine, pesticides, benzene, methanol, or octanes.
4.
Solids which are capable of posing serious hazards due to their volatility, combustibility
and potential in causing or propagating severe conflagrations. Examples include but
are not limited to metal powder, sodium batteries, matches, camphor, iron oxide, phosphorus
and sulphur.
5.
Substances which are liable either to cause death or serious injury or to harm human
health if swallowed, inhaled or by skin contact. It also includes infectious substances
which are known or can be reasonably expected to contain pathogens, such as bacteria,
viruses, rickettsiae, parasites and fungi, or other agents which can cause disease
in humans or animals. Examples include but are not limited to tear gas substances,
acids, arsenic, biomedical waste, medical cultures or samples, cyanide, lead compounds,
mercury compounds, or chloroform,
6.
Radioactive materials or substances, which present potentially severe risks to human
health. Examples include but are not limited to radioactive ores, medical isotopes,
plutonium, iridium, thorium, or uranium.
7.
Corrosive materials or substances which may cause severe damage when in contact with
living tissue or, in the case of leakage, damage or destroy surrounding materials.
Examples include but are not limited to acids, batteries, fuel cells, dyes, formaldehyde,
flux, paints, chlorides, bromine, phenol, nitric acid, sulphuric acid or iodine.
8.
Other materials or substances which may present serious hazards to human health and
safety. Examples include but are not limited to dry ice, ammonium nitrate fertilizers,
asbestos, or lithium ion batteries.
(Ord. 461 § 2 (part), 1995)
(Ord. No. 779, § 2(Exh. A), 1-21-2014)

 ### 9.12.025 - Manner of conducting inventories.

Deputies must conduct all inventories as set forth in this ordinance in the order
listed. For example, a deputy must first inventory all open containers before moving
on to inventory closed containers designed for or likely to contain valuables. Deputies
may not deviate from the order listed, and deputies must inventory every container
listed in this ordinance. The intent of this paragraph is to specifically limit the
discretion of the deputy conducting the inventory, and to ensure that each inventory
is conducted in the same manner.
(Ord. No. 779, § 2(Exh. A), 1-21-2014)

 ### 9.12.030 - Inventories of impounded vehicles.

A.
The contents of all vehicles impounded by a deputy sheriff will be inventoried. The
inventory shall be conducted before constructive custody of the vehicle is released
to a third-party towing company except under the following circumstances:
1.
If there is reasonable suspicion to believe that the safety of the deputy or deputies
or any other person is at risk, a required inventory will be done as soon as is safely
practical; or
2.
If the vehicle is being impounded for evidentiary purposes in connection with the
investigation of a criminal offense, the inventory will be done after the investigation
is completed.
B.
The purpose for the inventory of an impounded vehicle will be to:
1.
Promptly identify property to establish accountability and avoid spurious claims to
property;
2.
Fulfill the requirements of ORS 133.455 to the extent that such statute may apply
to certain property held by the deputy for safekeeping;
3.
Assist in the prevention of theft of property;
4.
Locate toxic, flammable or explosive substances;
5.
Reduce the danger to persons and property; and
6.
Locate weapons and instruments that may facilitate an escape from custody or endanger
law enforcement personnel.
C.
Inventories of impounded vehicles must be conducted according to the following procedures
and in the order listed:
1.
The driver or any passenger (if they are not being taken into custody) may remove
their personal items (such as backpacks, purses or electronics cases) from the vehicle
prior to the inventory. Deputies will not open or inventory these personal items absent
consent or a recognized warrant exception.
2.
An inventory of personal property and the contents of open containers shall be conducted
throughout the passenger and engine compartments of the vehicle including, but not
limited to, accessible areas under or within the dashboard area, in any pockets in
the doors or in the back of the front seat, in any console between the seats, under
any floor mats and under the seats.
3.
In addition to the passenger and engine compartments as described above, an inventory
of personal property and the contents of all open containers shall also be conducted
in the following locations:
a.
Any other type of unlocked compartments or compartments that are a part of the vehicle
or attached to the vehicle including, but not limited to, unlocked vehicle trunks,
truck beds, whether or not covered by a tonneau cover, and unlocked car-top containers,
and
b.
Any locked compartments that are part of or attached to a vehicle including, but not
limited to, locked vehicle trunks, locked hatchbacks, locked car-top containers, locked
tonneau covers, locked glove boxes, locked center consoles, and other locked compartments
or containers inside or outside the vehicle if either the keys are available to be
released with the vehicle to the third-party towing company or an unlocking mechanism
for such compartment is available within the vehicle.
c.
Closed containers located either within the vehicle or any of the vehicle's compartments
shall be opened, the contents removed and inventoried if the closed container is designed
for or likely to contain valuables, including but not limited to closed purses (such
as a handbag, shoulder bag, satchel, tote or clutch), closed coin purses, closed personal
electronic cases or bags, closed laptop cases or bags, closed wallets, closed backpacks,
closed knapsacks, closed rucksacks, closed briefcases, closed glasses cases and closed
fanny packs.
4.
Any valuables located during the inventory process will be listed on a property receipt.
A copy of the property receipt will either be left in the vehicle or tendered to the
person in control of the vehicle if such person is present.
(Ord. 655 § 2, 2006; Ord. 586 § 2, 2001; Ord. 461 § 2 (part), 1995)
(Ord. No. 779, § 2(Exh. A), 1-21-2014)

 ### 9.12.040 - Inventories of persons in police custody.

A.
A deputy will inventory the personal property in the possession of a person taken
into police custody and such inventory will be conducted whenever:
1.
Such person will either be taken to the Washington County Jail, placed in a secure
police holding room or transported in the secure portion of a sheriff's office vehicle;
or
2.
Custody of the person will be transferred to another law enforcement agency, correctional
facility, or "treatment facility" as that phrase is used in ORS 426.460 or such other
lawfully approved facility for the involuntary confinement of persons pursuant to
Oregon Revised Statute.
3.
At the request of the person being taken into custody, property may be turned over
to a third party present at the scene if that third party provides identifying information
sufficient to locate them in the event of a claim involving lost or stolen property.
The person to whom the property is given will be identified in the deputy's report,
along with a description of property released to the person (e.g. backpack or purse).
For property being released to a third party, a deputy may not search or inventory
the property absent consent, a warrant or a warrant exception.
4.
The inventory or personal property in the possession of a person taken into police
custody will normally be conducted prior to placing the person in a transport vehicle
or a secure facility, however the inventory may be done later due to staffing, officer
safety or logistical issues.
B.
The purpose of the inventory of a person in police custody will be to:
1.
Promptly identify property to establish accountability and avoid spurious claims to
property;
2.
Fulfill the requirements of ORS 133.455 to the extent that such statute may apply
to certain property held by the deputy for safekeeping;
3.
Assist in the prevention of theft of property;
4.
Locate and ensure the safe handling and/or disposal of toxic, flammable, hazardous
or explosive substances;
5.
Locate weapons and instruments that may facilitate an escape from custody or endanger
law enforcement personnel; and
6.
Reduce the danger to persons and property.
C.
Inventories of the personal property in the possession of such persons will be conducted
according to the following procedures, and in the order listed:
1.
A deputy must remove all personal property from the person in custody (except clothing)
including everything in the person's pockets, which are likely to contain cash or
other small valuables.
2.
A deputy must remove and inventory the contents of all open containers.
3.
After completing the inventory of all open containers, a deputy will then open, remove
and inventory the contents of closed containers if the closed container is designed
for or likely to contain valuables or money, including but not limited to, closed
purses (such as a handbag, shoulder bag, satchel, tote or clutch), closed coin purses,
closed personal electronic cases or bags, closed laptop cases or bags, closed tablet
cases or bags, closed wallets, closed backpacks, closed knapsacks, closed rucksacks,
closed briefcases, closed glasses cases and closed fanny packs.
4.
After completing the inventory of closed containers likely to or designed to contain
valuables, a deputy must open, remove the contents, and inventory the contents of
any closed container that is designed for or likely to contain toxic, explosive, hazardous
or flammable substances, including but not limited to closed make-up bags (likely
to contain flammable perfumes) and closed cigarette packages or cigarette cases (likely
to contain matches or lighters) or is designed for or likely to contain weapons or
ammunition, including but not limited to gun safes, knife cases, ammunition cans,
holsters, or gun cases.
5.
After completing the inventory of closed containers designed for or likely to contain
toxic, explosive, hazardous or flammable substances or weapons, a deputy must also
open, remove the contents and inventory the contents of any closed container that
has not already been inventoried if that container will be transported (in a WCSO
vehicle or a vehicle assigned to a WCSO deputy) or brought to the WCSO jail. The express
purpose of this requirement is to ensure that toxic, explosive, hazardous or flammable
substances are identified and safely handled and do not put the public, staff, inmates
or public property at risk. A deputy who finds money or other valuables located in
a closed container pursuant to an inspection under this subsection shall promptly
inventory the valuables or money.
6.
For safety of the deputy and the person taken into custody, a deputy will remove all
personal property from a person prior to transporting that person in a police vehicle,
and will normally put all personal property and valuables into a clear plastic bag.
Pursuant to ORS 133.455, a deputy will issue a property receipt to a person taken
into custody for all money or other valuables taken from the person at the time the
property is taken. A property receipt may be provided at a later time, if officer
safety, staffing or other logistical issues make it impractical to provide the receipt
at the time the property is taken.
7.
For any person in custody delivered to the Washington County Jail by any agency, a
jail deputy will conduct their own inventory of the arrested person's personal property
and verify that all money and valuables listed on the property receipt are present.
A jail deputy will inventory the personal property in the following manner and in
the order listed:
a.
A deputy will remove all personal property from the arrestee's clothes and inventory
it.
b.
A jail deputy will remove and inventory the contents of all open containers.
c.
A deputy will open, remove and inventory the contents of closed containers if the
closed container is designed for or likely to contain valuables or money, including
but not limited to, closed purses (such as a handbag, shoulder bag, satchel, tote
or clutch), closed coin purses, closed personal electronic cases or bags, closed laptop
cases or bags, closed wallets, closed backpacks, closed knapsacks, closed rucksacks,
closed briefcases, closed glasses cases and closed fanny packs.
d.
After completing the inventory of closed containers likely to or designed to contain
valuables, a jail deputy will also open, remove and inventory the contents of all
closed containers that are designed for or likely to contain flammable, toxic, hazardous
or explosive substances, including but not limited to closed make-up bags (likely
to contain flammable perfumes) and closed cigarette packages or cigarette cases (likely
to contain matches or lighters).
e.
After completing the inventory of closed containers that are likely to or designed
to contain toxic, explosive or flammable substances, a deputy will open, remove and
inventory the contents of any closed container designed for or likely to contain weapons
or ammunition, including but not limited to gun safes, knife cases, ammunition cans
or boxes, holsters, or gun cases. Any loaded firearms found will be unloaded, using
a clearing barrel, and stored in a secure location.
f.
After completing the inventory for weapons and ammunition, the deputy will open, remove
the contents and inspect every other closed container which will be stored in a Washington
County facility to determine if the closed container contains toxic, explosive, hazardous
or flammable substances, The express purpose of this requirement is to ensure that
toxic, explosive, hazardous or flammable substances are identified and safely handled
and do not put the public, staff, or county property at risk, and to ensure that no
closed container is stored in the jail without being checked for toxic, explosive,
hazardous or flammable substances. Any toxic, explosive, hazardous or flammable substances
will be disposed of and may not be stored inside the Washington County Jail, except
as permitted by the jail commander (such as small lighters or small bottles of perfumes).
Medical equipment such as an oxygen tank (flammable) may be stored in a safe location
outside the facility, as determined by Jail Command and County Facilities, or may
be turned over to a third party of the inmate's choosing.
g.
Deputies will fill out a property inventory report, specifically noting any valuables
or money found and listing any toxic, explosive, hazardous flammables or weapons located
and their disposition.
(Ord. 461 § 2 (part), 1995)
(Ord. No. 779, § 2(Exh. A), 1-21-2014)

 ### Chapter 9.16 - PROHIBITION OF GRAFFITI

 ### 9.16.010 - Findings.

A.
The board of county commissioners finds graffiti is a growing concern in the county,
and that graffiti detracts from the public health and safety of Washington County
residents.
B.
The board finds that graffiti on public or private property impairs property values,
decreases liveability, and results in additional graffiti.
C.
The board finds that graffiti is often gang-related, is used by gang members to claim
a neighborhood or area as their "turf," and may result in violence between rival gangs.
D.
The board finds that adoption of a graffiti ordinance, consistent with the provisions
adopted by the major cities within the county, will enhance public health and safety,
and will protect the liveability of Washington County neighborhoods.
(Ord. 699 § 1, 2008)

 ### 9.16.020 - Definitions.

As used in this chapter, unless the context requires otherwise:
1.
"Abate" means to remove graffiti from the public view.
2.
"Aerosol paint container" means any aerosol container adapted or made for spraying
paint.
3.
"County officer" means a county code enforcement officer or an employee whose job
description includes enforcement authority, including the county counsel or his designee,
and any peace officer.
4.
"Etching device" means a glasscutter, awl, or any device capable of scratching or
etching the surface of any structure or personal property.
5.
"Felt tip marker" means an indelible marker or similar implement with a tip which,
at its broadest width, is greater than one-fourth (¼) inch.
6.
"Graffiti" means any inscription, word, figure, or design that is marked, etched,
scratched, drawn, or painted on any surface with paint, ink, chalk, dye, or other
similar substance, regardless of content, which is visible from premises open to the
public, such as public rights of way or other publicly-owned property, and that has
been placed upon any real or personal property, such as buildings, fences, and structures,
without written authorization from the owner or responsible party.
7.
"Graffiti implement" means an aerosol paint container, a felt tip marker, an etching
device, or a graffiti stick.
8.
"Graffiti nuisance property" means a property upon which graffiti has been placed
and such graffiti has been permitted to remain for more than fourteen (14) days after
the property owner of record or occupant has been issued written notification, unless
an extension has been granted by a county officer pursuant to Section 9.16.050 .
9.
"Graffiti Stick" means a device containing a solid form of paint, chalk, wax, epoxy,
or other similar substance capable of being applied to a surface by pressure, and
upon application, leaving a mark at least one-fourth of an inch (¼") wide.
10.
"Occupant" means any person, tenant, sub-lessee, successor, or assignee that has control
over property.
11.
"Owner" means any person, agent, firm, or corporation having a legal or equitable
interest in a property and includes but not limited to a mortgagor in possession,
an occupant, or a person, agent, firm, or corporation that owns or exercises control
over items of property, such as utility poles, drop boxes, postal collection boxes,
and other types of containers.
12.
"Permit" means to knowingly allow, suffer, and acquiesce by a failure, refusal, or
neglect to abate.
13.
"Premises open to the public" means all public spaces, including but not limited to
streets, alleys, sidewalks, parks, rights of way, and public open space, and private
property onto which the public is regularly invited or permitted to enter for any
purpose.
14.
"Property" means any real or personal property, including but not limited to items
affixed or appurtenant to real property or premises, house, building, fence, or structure,
and items of machinery, drop boxes, waste containers, utility poles and vaults, and
post office collection boxes.
15.
"Responsible party" means an owner or an entity or person acting as an agent for an
owner by agreement that has authority over the property or is responsible for the
property's maintenance or management. There may be more than one party responsible
for a particular property.
16.
"Unauthorized" means without consent of the owner, occupant or responsible party.
(Ord. 699 § 2, 2008)

 ### 9.16.030 - Prohibitions.

1.
It is unlawful and a violation of this chapter for any person to place or put by any
means any drawing, inscription, figure, symbol, mark, or any type of commonly known
graffiti on any public or private property without the written consent of the owner
of the premises on which the property is located, or upon natural surfaces such as
rocks, trees, or any surface whatsoever. It is unlawful and a violation of this chapter
for any person to solicit or command another person to apply graffiti or aid or abet
another person in applying graffiti. Each wall or object upon which graffiti is placed
constitutes a separate violation. Each day, on which a violation occurs or continues,
is a separate violation.
2.
No person may possess any graffiti implement, with the intent to unlawfully apply
graffiti on any real or personal property of another. Each day on which a violation
occurs is a separate violation.
(Ord. 699 § 3, 2008)

 ### 9.16.040 - Seizure of graffiti implements.

A graffiti implement used or possessed in violation of this section may be immediately
seized and impounded by the sheriff's office. The court, upon disposition of the issued
citation, shall determine whether the instrument shall be returned to the defendant
or deemed to be contraband subject to destruction under Oregon law.
(Ord. 699 § 4, 2008)

 ### 9.16.050 - Failure to remove graffiti.

1.
The owner or occupant of any property within unincorporated Washington County shall
remove any graffiti from that property within fourteen (14) days of the graffiti's
appearance.
2.
Whenever a county officer determines that graffiti exists on any property in the county,
the county officer may issue an abatement notice. The owner or occupant shall have
fourteen (14) days after the date of service of the notice to remove the graffiti.
3.
The notice shall be served by addressing the notice to the owner and occupant and
delivering it by personal service or by mailing it as certified mail. Service may
also be accomplished by posting the notice in a clearly visible location on the subject
property. Service shall be complete upon mailing or posting.
4.
If the person who was served the notice is unable to remove, or cause to be removed,
the graffiti within the fourteen (14)-day period due to a hardship, he or she may
apply to the country officer for an extension of time in which to remove the graffiti.
For purposes of this subsection, "hardship" includes but is not limited to serious
illness or disability, extremely inclement weather that temporarily prevents removal
of the graffiti, or other extraordinary circumstance.
5.
If graffiti is not removed within fourteen (14) days after serving notice on the owner
and occupant, the county officer may cause a citation to be issued to the owner or
occupant or both requiring the person to appear in Washington County Justice Court.
6.
Each day the graffiti remains after the expiration of the fourteen (14) day notice
constitutes a separate offense.
(Ord. 699 § 5, 2008)

 ### 9.16.060 - Penalty and enforcement.

A.
A violation of Section 9.16.030 or 9.16.050 of this chapter is punishable by a maximum fine not to exceed five hundred dollars
($500.00).
B.
Violation of this chapter is deemed a public nuisance and the county reserves any
civil remedy available at law for abatement of a nuisance, including but not limited
to injunctive relief.
(Ord. 699 § 6, 2008)
