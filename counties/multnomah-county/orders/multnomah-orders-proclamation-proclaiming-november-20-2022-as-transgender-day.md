## Full text

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
PROCLAMATION NO. 2022-101
Proclaiming November 20, 2022 as Transgender Day of Remembrance  in Multnomah County,
Oregon.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
a. Transgender Day of Remembrance (TDOR) was started in 1999 by transgender advocate
Gwendolyn Ann Smith as a vigil to honor the memory of Rita Hester, a transgender
woman who was killed in 1998.  Rita’s murder became the catalyst to honoring the lives
and memory of transgender people lost to senseless violence, transphobia, self -harm,
and structural inequalities.
b. Anti-transgender violence has been declared an epidemic by the American Medical
Association; it is an infectious disease in our community, and although more attention has
been raised regarding this epidemic, our transgender and gender non-binary community
members continue be killed and live in fear.
c. Our Transgender and Non-binary communities have endured years of pain and suffering;
and yet, many have risen to fight against senseless bigotry by advocating for policies to
address anti-trans bias, and by living their full selves openly . This bravery often resulted
in tragedy and violence against trans and non-binary people.
d. A disproportionately high number of these victims were and continue to be Black and
Latine, Indigenous, and other Trans Femmes of color. These incidents are underreported,
due to our region, state and nation’s history of systemic racism and transphobia.
e. So far in 2022, at least 29 Trans and Non -binary people have been killed in acts of
violence across the United States. That number is not a complete representation of those
killed by anti-trans acts of violence.
f. While we must continue to pay close attention to the reports of transgender violence we
receive, we must not solely rely on the number of yearly deaths to bring about necessary
change.
g. We must remind ourselves of our responsibility to dive deeper into sy stemic inequities
endured by Transgender folks every single day and focus on developing policy solutions
that address those systemic inequities.
h. All who have lost loved ones and have been lost to anti -transgender violence will be
forever missed by family, friends, partners, and the community and we will commemorate
their life by protecting, advocating for, and uplifting those who are still with us.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Proclaims:
November 20 th, 2022 is proclaimed to be Transgend er Day of Remembrance in Multnomah
County, Oregon. With this proclamation, we affirm our commitment to supporting our Trans and
gender non-binary communities, not just through the words, but also through action to address
systemic inequities, and by uplifting the tenacity of Trans and Non-binary individuals who have
brought and continue to bring strength, beauty and perseverance to our community.
ADOPTED this 17th day of November, 2022.
BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
Deborah Kafoury, Chair
REVIEWED:
JENNY M. MADKOUR, COUNTY ATTORNEY
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON

     Jenny M. Madkour, County Attorney
