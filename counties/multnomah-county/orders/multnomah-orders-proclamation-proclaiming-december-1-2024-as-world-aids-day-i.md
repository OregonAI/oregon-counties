# Proclamation Proclaiming December 1, 2024 As World Aids Day In Multnomah County, Oregon. (Proclamation Proclaiming December 1, 2024 As World Aids Day In Multnomah County, Oregon.)

## Full text

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
PROCLAMATION NO. 2024-101
Proclaiming December 1, 2024 as World AIDS Day in Multnomah County, Oregon.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
A. World AIDS Day is recognized each year on December 1st. It brings attention to
the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and Acquired Immune Deficiency
Syndrome (AIDS) epidemic, to increase HIV awareness and knowledge, and to
stand with people experiencing stigma related to their HIV status. World AIDS
Day is an opportunity for remembering all those we have lost, honoring those
who fought (and continue to fight) for just access to prevention, treatment, and
care, and commit to continuing the progress that our communities need and
deserve. The 2024 World AIDS Day theme is “Collective Action: Sustain and
Accelerate HIV Progress”.
B. For the past 30 years, Multnomah County has engaged in collective action and
progress by partnering with the Portland Area HIV Services Planning Council to
oversee a network of care for low-income people living with HIV across a 6-
county region. This includes the counties of Clackamas, Columbia, Washington,
Yamhill, and Clark (in WA state). The Planning Council is a 30-member
community-led group of people with lived experience, community advocates, and
service providers. Over 40% of Council members are people living with HIV. This
diverse group is committed to ensuring that community voices are centered in
regional HIV service decision-making.
C. At all levels, there is progress to celebrate. Nationally and in Oregon, the overall
number of new HIV cases has decreased since 2018. In 2022, about 90% of
Oregonians with HIV were in care, and over 3 in 4 clients were virally
suppressed. Regional data show even higher rates for those in Multnomah and
our 6-county region. These are outstanding achievements, and are a testament
to our local HIV service system, partners, and advocates. And there is still much
work to be done.
D. People of color, youth, and trans women experience a disproportionate burden of
new HIV and AIDS cases. The most recent national data (2018-2022) show that
Black/African American people make up 12% of the US population, but 37% of all
new HIV cases. Similarly, Hispanic/Latino people make up 18% of the US
population and 33% of all new HIV cases nationally. Both racial groups are
overrepresented in the national HIV incidence when compared to the total
population. About 40% of all new cases were among people aged 25-34. In
2022, about 43% of new cases in Oregon were among Black, Indigenous, and
other people of color. Women and transgender people are also overrepresented

among new cases. While trans women represent 2% of existing cases in the 6-
county Portland TGA, they represent 9% of new cases in 2023. More tailored
programs, representative staff, and engagement with these community members
is critical.

E. Multnomah County also supports the collective action theme through its
commitment to implementing the “End HIV in Oregon Strategy” that includes
three key points: 1) Testing is easy, 2) Prevention works, and 3) Treatment saves
lives. An integrated community approach is critical to address these disparities as
the concurrent public health crisis of racism continues to impact our most
vulnerable populations.

F. Multnomah County affirms our commitment to the health, wellbeing, and safety of
people in our community living with HIV and AIDS through services and
programs offered across County departments. The Health Services Center is a
critical medical provider for people living with HIV or AIDS in the region, and the
Behavioral Health Division’s Promoting Access To Hope program helps ensure
substance use treatment support for vulnerable residents with co-occurring
conditions. Additional community partners supporting this work include Cascade
AIDS Project; Ecumenical Ministries of Oregon’s HIV Day Center; Quest Center
for Integrative Health; OHSU: Partnership Project, Russell Street Dental Clinic,
and Internal Medicine Clinic; Oregon Health Authority; HIV Alliance; Eastern
Oregon Center for Independent Living; Oregon AIDS Education and Training
Center and others.

G. Multnomah County is fortunate to honor all of our siblings, neighbors, friends and
family with HIV through an ongoing display of two panels of the historic National
AIDS Quilt at the Gladys McCoy Building and the Multnomah County East
Building lobby. The AIDS Quilt is a hand-made and community led
commemoration of the resilient community members who fought bravely against
a complex disease. Their ongoing presence in these public spaces serve as a
daily reminder of collective remembrance, honoring, and action.

The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Proclaims:
In observance of Annual World AIDS Day on December 1, 2024, in Multnomah County,
Oregon, we reaffirm our support for people living with and affected by HIV, and
remember those community members who have lost their lives to AIDS.
ADOPTED this 21st day of November 2024.
SUBMITTED BY: Rachael Banks, Health Department Director
