## Full text

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
RESOLUTION NO. 2022-100
Resolution Directing a Process to Rename the Sauvie Island Bridge to Honor the Island’s Native
American History and Communities.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
A. Sauvie Island has been home to the Chinook people, providing safety and sustainability for
more than 2,000 Indigenous people and 15 villages until colonization.
B. When arriving, white settlers brought with them violence, illness, and a capitalist mindset,
forcibly taking, remapping, naming, and renaming swaths of Indigenous land.Indigenous
communities have always resisted the colonial agenda byﬁnding ways to protect their culture
and fend off erasure. This continues today as Indigenous communities actively reclaim
jurisdiction, space, language, and history.
C. There is a painful loss of history and continued harm when traditional names, language, and
communities are replaced and never restored or repaired. Indigenous place names are
powerful vehicles for narrating history and inscribing the landscape with meaning.
D. It is critical to maintain connections between people and the place that has always been
their home, which were compromised by assimilation and erasure. By maintaining the
current name of the bridge, named after a European dairy farmer who capitalized on the loss
of Indigenous life, we are continuing to participate in an act of erasure.
E. With a new name, we can provoke a critical awareness of Indigenous history and culture by
recognizing the connection between language and land while honoring the Indigenous
communities who have always been here.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Resolves:
1. To support the recommendation from the Advisory Committee on Sustainability and
Innovation (ACSI) to rename the Sauvie Island Bridge to honor Native American
communities whose traditional home includes the Island.
2. To direct the Tribal Affairs Advisor in the Office of Government Relations to establish a
community engagement process to work with Sauvie Island residents, appropriate
community-based organizations, and Tribal communities to identify culturally significant
name options for the bridge.
ADOPTED this 17th day of November, 2022.
________________________________
Deborah Kafoury, Chair
REVIEWED:
JENNY M. MADKOUR, COUNTY ATTORNEY
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
