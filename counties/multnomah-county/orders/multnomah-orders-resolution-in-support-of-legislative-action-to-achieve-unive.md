## Full text

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
RESOLUTION NO. 2022-047
Resolution in Support of Legislative Action to Achieve Universal Health Care
Access.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
A. It is impossible for an individual to live a productive, healthy life without
access to quality health care.
B. When all members of a community have equitable access to health care, the
risk of illness from contagious disease decreases; absenteeism for both
students and employees decreases; and the rate of financial hardships
related to financing health-related treatment decreases.
C. Our current healthcare system requires complex, costly, and time -
consuming billing and administrative systems.
D. Investments to address social determinants of health and other preventive
measures will produce better health outcomes than administrative spending.
E. Individual wellbeing and public health both benefit when all members of a
community have real, meaningful access to health care: the risk of illness
from contagious disease decreases; employee and student absenteeism
decreases, which is positive for students, employees, and businesses alike;
and individuals and families are less likely to experience financial
vulnerability, bankruptcy, and homelessness.
F. Our current healthcare system requires complex, costly, and time -
consuming billing and administrative systems that greatly impact local
government budgets.
G. The never-ending rising costs of health care add chal lenges to our already
strapped county budget and our small businesses, which keep our
communities thriving.
H. Oregon law (ORS 414.018) states, "It is the intention of the Legislative
Assembly to achieve the goals of universal access to an adequate level of
high quality health care at an affordable cost."

I. The State of Oregon has an established recent history of working to improve
health care access, evidenced by expanding Medicaid eligibility in 2014, and
several other specific legislative actions such as, O regon Senate Bill 558
("Cover All Kids") which extended Oregon Health Plan coverage to all
children in Oregon regardless of immigration status, and House Bill 3391
(Reproductive Health Equity Act) which extended coverage for the full range
of reproductive health services to individuals who would otherwise be eligible
for coverage if not for their immigration status.

J. In 2016, the Oregon Health Authority contracted with RAND Corporation and
Health Management Associates to complete the study outlined in HB 32 60.
The findings were reported in the 2017 session. The study recommended
two promising options and confirmed that universal health care coverage in
Oregon is achievable and financially viable, and could reduce health industry
costs by streamlining multiple insurance and billing structures.

K. In 2019 the Oregon Legislature established the Joint Task Force on
Universal Health Care to recommend the design of the Health Care for All
Oregon Plan, a universal health care system, administered by the Health
Care for All Oregon Board, that is equitable, affordable and comprehensive,
provides high -quality health care and is publicly funded and available to
every individual residing in Oregon has convened to develop its
recommendations.

L. In December of 2020, the Congressional Budget Office released a report that
examined the cost of universal healthcare proposals based on a Medicare
fee-for-service program, and found that implementing a single payer
healthcare program would not only guarantee coverage for everyon e but it
would also reduce spending nationwide.

M. In the 2021 Oregon legislative session, Senate Joint Resolution 12, also
known as the HOPE Amendment, passed as a referral to voters to declare
healthcare access as a fundamental right in the Oregon Constitution.

The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Resolves:

1. To thank the Oregon Legislature for its work toward universal health care for
all Oregonians, including creating a task force charged with recommending
a universal single payer health care system that offers equitable, affordable,
comprehensive, high quality, publicly funded health care to all Oregon
residents.
2. To strongly urge the Governor to request the federal waivers to allow Oregon
to receive the federal dollars for its own state -based single payer universal
healthcare system.
3. To strongly urge the Oregon Legislature pass, and the Governor sign a bill,
that creates a publicly funded, comprehensive, equitable, and high quality
universal single payer system of health care that is accessible to all – without
discrimination and that is affordable for all.
4. To strongly urge Congress to pass, and the President sign, a bill that will
create a publicly funded, universal single payer healthcare system that is
comprehensive, equitable, affordable, accessible, and of high quality.
ADOPTED this 19th day of May, 2022.
SUBMITTED BY: Commissioners Sharon Meieran & Susheela Jayapal
