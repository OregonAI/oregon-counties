# Ordinance Amending Chapter 25 To Establish A Waste, Inefficiency And Abuse Hotline For Multnomah County. (Ordinance Amending Chapter 25 To Establish A Waste, Inefficiency And Abuse Hotline For Multnomah County.)

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY , OREGON
ORDINANCE NO.
Amending Chapter 25 to Establish a Waste, Inefficiency and Abuse Hotline for Multnomah
County.
(Language stricken is deleted; underlined language is new.)
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
a. The efficient, professional, and legitimate delivery of services is a priority. Organizations
with hotlines experience fraud losses significantly lower than organizations without hotlines.
b. Oregon law provides that as the governing body for Multnomah County, the Board of
County Commissioners may establish a waste hotline. The statutes also lay out how the
hotline should be operated, including placing notices, explaining the purpose of the hotline,
establishing policies and procedures for logging all reports received, conducting initial
investigations, maintaining confidentiality, and reporting requirements for findings of potential
waste, inefficiencies or abuse. In addition, one key function of the hotline is to direct complaints
to agencies to investigate and follow up. See ORS 297.760-765.
c. In 2007, the Multnomah County Auditor established the Good Government Hotline to
provide county employees and the public with a confidential method for reporting suspected
fraud, waste, inefficiency and abuse by county government. Four years later the Oregon
legislature passed ORS 297.760 “Establishment of local government waste hotline” and ORS
297.765 “Policies and procedures for local government waste hotline; confidentiality; required
reporting.” The Auditor has operated the hotline in accordance with these state laws
continuously since 2007.
d. The Board, Chair, and Auditor recognize that hotlines are effective reporting
mechanisms and are an integral part of government accountability. Each independently
elected official seeks to continue operation of the Good Government Hotline in accordance
with their separate authorities identified in sections e, f, and g below.
e. The Multnomah County Charter Section 6.10 provides that the County Chair is “the
chief executive officer and personnel officer of the county”. The Chair is vested with the “sole
authority to appoint, order, direct and discharge administrative officers and employees of the
county” and retains “full responsibility for the acts of the chair’s subordinates”. The Charter
further states that the Chair will “execute the policies of the board and the ordinances of the
county”.
f. As the governing body for Multnomah County, the Board of County Commissioners
formally recognizes and establishes the Good Government Hotline under ORS 297.760 as a
method for employees and the public to report waste, inefficiency or abuse to promote and
protect services at Multnomah County.

g. The Auditor consents to continue operating the Good Government Hotline as a service
to employees and the public.
Multnomah County Ordains as Follows:
Section 1. MCC 25.910 is added as follows:
§ 25.910 HOTLINE ESTABLISHED.
The waste, inefficiency, and abuse hotline (“hotline”) is established pursuant to ORS 297.760.
Section 2. MCC 25.920 is added as follows:
§ 25.920 PURPOSE; METHOD OF MAKING REPORTS.
The hotline’s purpose is to create a mechanism for employees and the public to report
suspected waste, inefficiencies and abuse by the County and its officers, employees, and
contractors. In addition to reports made to the hotline, reports may be made by any other
method, including email.
Section 3. MCC 25.930 is added as follows:
§ 25.930 OPERATION OF HOTLINE.
(A) The hotline will be operated by the Auditor, with their consent. As part of
operating the hotline, policies and procedures will be established.
(B) The hotline will be operated in accordance with guidelines set forth in ORS
297.760 and ORS 297.765.

Section 4. MCC 25.940 is added as follows:
§ 25.940 REFERRAL TO CHAIR.
Reports relating to labor and employment matters, including potential violations of County
personnel rules, collective bargaining agreements, or employment laws will be referred to the
Chair.
