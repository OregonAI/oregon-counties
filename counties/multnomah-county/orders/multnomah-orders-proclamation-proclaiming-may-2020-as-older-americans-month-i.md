## Full text

BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON

PROCLAMATION NO. 2020-032

Proclaiming May 2020 as Older Americans Month in Multnomah County, Oregon.

The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:

a.      People 60 years and older have been asked to take extreme caution because of
COVID-19, including limiting contact with family members outside their household.
These necessary actions have drawn attention to the potential vulnerability of older
adults and have highlighted their remarkable adaptability and grit.

b. There is a direct link for older adults between social connections and overall
physical and mental health emphasizing the importance of connections for older adults
to their loved ones and their community. Older adults who stay engaged are more able
to adapt in the face of adversity, trauma and tragedy.  Historically, older generations
have proven their resiliency, just as is happening now during this global pandemic.

c. The aging services network, which includes community-based organizations and
partners across Multnomah County, has responded to the unprecedented need by
transforming their service delivery approach with unwavering commitment and
remarkable speed. Our community has prioritized the health and wellbeing of older
adults by serving thousands more meals with a no-contact home-delivery approach, by
standing up new video social and support group meetings for LGBTQ elders, by making
hundreds of friendly check-in phone calls by staff and peers, and by learning new video
platforms to offer virtual enrichment activities or convening of long-standing groups.

d. Family caregivers, both daily and in the midst of this pandemic, are essential to
the health and safety of their spouses, family members, and friends who have long-term
care needs. This commitment is built into our social contracts and demonstrated in
caregivers’ actions, such as managing medications, preparing meals, arranging medical
appointments, and supporting self-advocacy. The social distancing measures dictated
by COVID-19 bring the incredible contributions of family caregivers into the light,
illuminating this critical work that often goes unseen.

e. In response to Stay Home, Stay Safe, grandparents and elders who are raising
grandchildren and young relatives are taking on extraordinary additional responsibilities
with no respite and limited opportunity for outside recreation. They are weaving their
experience and talents with their commitment to the children they are raising, ensuring
they have the support needed to participate in distance education and stay connected to
their peers.
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Proclaims:
May 2020 is Older Americans Month, a time to recognize the generosity and resilience
as demonstrated by older adults and elders and the system that serves them.
Consistent with this year’s national theme, “Make Your Mark,” we ask people in
Multnomah County to acknowledge an older adult in your life or community that you
have seen “making their mark,” as a caregiver or in the countless other ways older
adults do on a daily basis. We urge people to plan safe celebrations via the telephone,
letter or virtual celebration and to ask the elders and older adults in your lives, “How are
you making your mark?” and proudly share those stories throughout this month and
beyond.
ADOPTED this 21st day of May, 2020.
SUBMITTED BY: Sharon Meieran, District 1 Commissioner
BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS
FOR MULTNOMAH COUNTY, OREGON
  Deborah Kafoury, Chair
REVIEWED:
JENNY
