BEFORE THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS

Amending M ultnomah County Code Chapter 21  – Health Amending § 21.452 and §
21.453 and Declaring an Emergency.
(Language stricken is deleted; double underlined language is new.)
The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
1. Residential wood smoke is a major contributor to human -caused air pollution in
Multnomah County and is a leading source of fine particulate matter pollution.
2. Disproportionate exposure to air pollution is part of the public health crisis of racism,
contributing to large racial disparities in illness and premature death.
3. A stakeholder work group convened by the Board in 2021 recommended that the
County strengthen air pollution protections and expand the wood smoke curtailment program.
4. Since the adoption of the curtailment program in 2018, new research has demonstrated
harmful health effects of fine particulate pollution at low concentrations.  Thes e effects include
cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, and adverse birth outcomes.  Long term
exposure may also increase the risk of illness from COVID -19.  In recognition of this improving
science, the World Health Organization recommended lower ambient air quality standards for
fine particulate pollution in September 2021.
5. High levels of air pollution outside of the wood smoke season as originally adopted in
2018 (which had extended from just October 1 to March 1) also warrant action by th e County
to reduce exposure to harmful pollutants.  This includes high ozone levels on warmer days.
6. Multnomah County is the Local Public Health Authority.  A core responsibility of the
Local Public Health Authority is to adopt ordinances necessary to admi nister any public health
matter not expressly preempted by Oregon law.
Multnomah County Ordains as Follows:
