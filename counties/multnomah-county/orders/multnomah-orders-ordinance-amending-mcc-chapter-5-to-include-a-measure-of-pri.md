The Multnomah County Board of Commissioners Finds:
a. In 2016, Multnomah County voters approved Measure 26 -184, which incorporated
campaign finance amendments into the Multnomah County Home Rule Charter.  In 2017, the
Board of County Commissioners adopted Ordinance 1243, now codified in MCC 5.200 to 5.206, to
begin implementing campaign finance reform in Multnomah County, while simultaneously seeking
a court ruling on the constitutionality of that ordinance.
b. In light of multiple favorable court rulings, the Board of County Commissioners has been
implementing the provisions of MCC 5.200 to 5.206 that  the courts have declared  constitutional.
The County is awaiting a final court ruling on the constitutionality of the contribution provisions in
MCC 5.201(A) and (B) under the First Amendment to the United States Constitution.  The County’s
expenditure (MCC 5.202(A)) and independent expenditure (MCC 5.202(C)) provisions were ruled
unconstitutional by the Oregon Supreme Court.
c. MCC 5.205 provides that all dollar amounts in the campaign finance laws shall be adjusted
on January 1 of each odd -numbered year to reflect an appropriate measure of price inflation,
rounded to the nearest dollar.  The Charter amendment and Ordinance 1243 did not take effect
until September 1, 2017, making January 1, 2019 , the first odd -numbered year for adjustment of
the dollar amounts.  At that time, the constitutionality of the County’s campaign finance laws was
still before the courts.  As a result, Jan uary 1, 2021, will be the first opportunity to adjust the dollar
amounts in the County’s campaign finance laws.
d. Because the contribution provisions in MCC 5.201(A) and (B) remain under consideration
by a court, and because the expenditure (MCC 5.202(A)) a nd independent expenditure (MCC
5.202(C)) provisions have been ruled unconstitutional, the Board will not adjust the dollar amounts
in those provisions at this time.
e. The November West Region -Size Class A Consumer Price Index All Urban Consumers
(CPI-U), Not Seasonally Adjusted, is an appropriate measure of price inflation for Multnomah
County.  The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is a measure of the average change in prices over time
in a fixed market basket of goods and services as calculated by the US Bureau  of Labor Statistics.
The CPI is one of the most widely used measures of inflation by both government and private
sector organizations. CPI-U measures the percentage change in prices faced by urban consumers
and covers approximately 93 percent of the US po pulation. The West Region captures thirteen
western states including the State of Oregon.   The November 2020 West Region -Size Class A
Consumer Price Index All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), Not Seasonally Adjusted, is 1.4 percent.

Section 3. MCC 5.205 is amended as follows:
§ 5.205 ADJUSTMENTS.
(A) All dollar amounts shall be adjusted on January 1 of each odd-numbered year using
the November West Region-Size Class A Consumer Price Index All Urban Consumers (CPI-U),
Not Seasonally Adjusted, for the preceding even-numbered year to reflect an appropriate measure
of price inflation, rounded to the nearest dollar.
(B) Using the November West Region-Size Class A Consumer Price Index All Urban
Consumers (CPI-U), Not Seasonally Adjusted, for the even-numbered year, the County Chief
Operating Officer shall determine the adjustments for all dollar amounts in Multnomah County
Code Chapter 5 and provide that information in writing to the County Attorney by December 15 of
each even-numbered year.
(C) Based on the information provided by the County Chief Operating Officer in
subsection (B), the County Attorney shall update Multnomah County Code Chapter 5 on January 1
of each odd-numbered year to reflect all adjusted dollar amounts.
Section 4. This Ordinance being necessary for the health, safety and general welfare of  the
people of Multnomah County, an emergency is declared and this Ordinance will take
effect on January 1, 2021 pursuant to Section 5.50 of the Multnomah County Home Rule Charter.
